# Theory: Reflective Two-Elementary Lattices

Definitions and results sourced from **V. Alexeev, "Reflective Hyperbolic 2-Elementary
Lattices, K3 Surfaces and Hyperkahler Manifolds"** (NSF PAR 10653059), Section 9.2. All
section references are to that paper unless noted.

## 1. Basic Lattice Notions

### Lattice (§9.2)

A **lattice** is a free abelian group $S \simeq \mathbb{Z}^r$ equipped with a
nondegenerate $\mathbb{Z}$-valued symmetric bilinear form.

### Even lattice (§9.2)

A lattice is **even** if $x^2$ is even for all $x \in S$.

### Hyperbolic lattice (§9.2)

A lattice of signature $(1, r - 1)$ is called **hyperbolic**. (This convention follows
the algebraic geometry direction.)

### Two-elementary lattice (§9.2)

A lattice is **two-elementary** if its discriminant group $A_S := S^*/S \simeq
\mathbb{Z}_2^a$ for some $a \geq 0$, where $S^* \subset S \otimes \mathbb{Q}$ is the
dual group.

### Nikulin classification (§9.2)

An indefinite even two-elementary lattice is uniquely determined by its signature and a
triple of integers $(r, a, \delta)$, where:
- $r$ = rank,
- $a$ = $\mathbb{Z}_2$-rank of the discriminant group,
- $\delta \in \{0, 1\}$ = **coparity** invariant.

### Coparity (§9.2)

A two-elementary lattice $S$ is:
- **coeven** ($\delta = 0$) if the doubled dual $S^\dagger := S^*(2)$ is even,
- **coodd** ($\delta = 1$) if $S^\dagger$ is odd.

A direct sum of two-elementary lattices is coeven iff every summand is coeven.

### Duality for coeven lattices (§9.3, "Other Coeven Lattices")

If $S = (r, a, 0)$ is coeven, then $S^\dagger = S^*(2) = (r, r - a, 0)$ is also coeven.
Their Coxeter diagrams are dual, with $(-2)$ and $(-4)$ roots interchanged, and there is
a bijection between their maximal parabolic subdiagrams.

## 2. Reflections and Roots

### Reflection (§9.2)

The **reflection** in a vector $\alpha \in S$ is the linear transformation:
$$w_\alpha(v) = v - 2\frac{v \cdot \alpha}{\alpha^2}\alpha.$$

### Root (§9.2)

A **root** is a vector $\alpha \in S$ such that $\alpha^2 < 0$ and $w_\alpha(S) = S$.

### Divisibility (§9.2)

The **divisibility** $\mathrm{div}(\alpha)$ of a vector $\alpha$ is defined by $\alpha
\cdot S = \mathrm{div}(\alpha)\mathbb{Z}$.

### Roots in two-elementary lattices (§9.2)

In an even two-elementary lattice, the roots are:
- the $(-2)$-vectors, and
- the $(-4)$-vectors of divisibility 2.

### Reflection groups (§9.2)

- $W_2(S)$: the group generated by reflections in $(-2)$-vectors.
- $W_r(S)$: the group generated by reflections in all roots (both $(-2)$- and
  $(-4)$-roots).

Both are normal subgroups of the isometry group $O(S)$, and also of its index-2 subgroup
$O^+(S)$ preserving the light cone.

### Reflectivity (§9.2)

A lattice is:
- **two-reflective** if $W_2(S)$ has finite index in $O(S)$,
- **reflective** if $W_r(S)$ has finite index in $O(S)$.

### Coxeter diagrams (§9.2)

- $\Gamma_2$: the Coxeter diagram for $W_2(S)$,
- $\Gamma_r$: the Coxeter diagram for $W_r(S)$.

The vertices of $\Gamma_r$ denote the roots $\alpha_i$ orthogonal to the facets of the
fundamental polyhedron $P_r$. Edge types specify the angle $\theta$ between $\alpha_i,
\alpha_j$:
- single line: $\theta = \pi/3$,
- double line: $\theta = \pi/4$,
- no line: $\theta = \pi/2$,
- bold line: hyperplanes meet at an infinite point of hyperbolic space,
- broken line: hyperplanes are skew.

For a two-elementary lattice, short $(-2)$-roots are denoted by white vertices and long
$(-4)$-roots by black vertices.

### Edge intersection numbers (§9.2)

| $\alpha_i^2$ | $\alpha_j^2$ | Simple | Double | Bold | Broken |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $-2$ | $-2$ | 1 | — | 2 | $>2$ |
| $-4$ | $-4$ | 2 | — | 4 | $>4$ |
| $-2$ | $-4$ | — | 2 | — | $>2$ |

## 3. Standard Root Lattices

### Notation (§9.2)

- $A_n$, $D_n$, $E_n$: standard negative-definite root lattices generated by
  $(-2)$-roots.
- $U = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$: the hyperbolic plane.
- $H(n)$: the lattice $H$ with product $x \cdot y$ in $H(n)$ equal to $n \cdot (x \cdot
  y)$ in $H$.
- $B_n(2)$, $C_n$, $F_4$: root lattices for $(-2)$-roots, which as $(-2)$-root lattices
  are $A_1^n$, $D_n$, and $D_4$ respectively.
- $\widetilde{A}_n$, $\widetilde{D}_n$, $\widetilde{E}_n$: parabolic (affine) diagrams;
  can consist of all short or all long roots, denoted $\widetilde{A}_n(2)$,
  $\widetilde{D}_n(2)$, $\widetilde{E}_n(2)$ for the long case.
- $\widetilde{B}_n$, $\widetilde{C}_n$, $\widetilde{F}_4$: parabolic diagrams with short
  and long versions.

### Elementary hyperbolic summands (§9.2)

Any even hyperbolic two-elementary lattice is a direct sum of one of:
- $\langle 2 \rangle$ with $(r,a,\delta) = (1,1,1)$,
- $U$ with $(r,a,\delta) = (2,0,0)$,
- $U(2)$ with $(r,a,\delta) = (2,2,0)$,

and a direct sum of the following elliptic root lattices:
- $B_n(2)$: $(n, n, 1)$,
- $C_{4n}$: $(4n, 2, 0)$ for $4n \geq 8$,
- $C_{4n+2}$: $(4n+2, 2, 1)$ for $4n+2 \geq 6$,
- $F_4$: $(4, 2, 0)$,
- $E_7$: $(7, 1, 1)$,
- $E_8$: $(8, 0, 0)$,
- $E_8(2)$: $(8, 8, 0)$.

## 4. Isotropic Vectors

### Primitive isotropic vectors (§9.2)

A vector $\nu \in S$ is **primitive isotropic** if $\nu^2 = 0$ and $\nu$ is not a
multiple of another lattice vector.

### Classification of isotropic vectors (§9.2)

By [1, Proposition 5.5], primitive isotropic vectors $\nu \in S$ with $\nu^2 = 0$ are of
three types.
Denoting $\overline{S} = \nu^\perp/\nu$ and with $\nu$ a vector in the first
summand:

1. **Odd**: $S = U \oplus \overline{S}$, $a_{\overline{S}} = a_S$,
   $\delta_{\overline{S}} = \delta_S$.
2. **Even ordinary**: $S = U(2) \oplus \overline{S}$, $a_{\overline{S}} = a_S - 2$,
   $\delta_{\overline{S}} = \delta_S$.
3. **Even characteristic**: $S = I_{1,1}(2) \oplus \overline{S}$, $a_{\overline{S}} =
   a_S - 2$, $\delta_S = 1$, and $\delta_{\overline{S}} = 0$.

Classifying isotropic vectors in an even hyperbolic two-elementary lattice with
invariants $(r,a,\delta)$ is equivalent to classifying even negative-definite lattices
$\overline{S}$ with invariants $(r-2, a, \delta)$, $(r-2, a-2, \delta)$, and $(r-2, a-2,
0)$.

### Parabolic subdiagrams and isotropic vectors (§9.2)

The $O(L)$-orbits of primitive isotropic vectors correspond to the **maximal parabolic
subdiagrams** of the Coxeter diagram.
If a parabolic subdiagram $\Gamma \subset \Gamma_r$ corresponds to a vector $\nu \in S$
with $\nu^2 = 0$, then its image in $\nu^\perp/\nu$ spans an elliptic root system.

## 5. Coxeter Diagram Structure

### Edge $n$-fold graph (§9.1, Def. 9.1.2)

Let $G$ be a graph and $n \geq 2$. The **edge $n$-fold graph** $G^{(n)}$ is obtained by
subdividing each edge of $G$ into $n$ edges and inserting $n-1$ intermediate vertices.
Thus $G^{(n)}$ has $n|E_G|$ edges and $|V_G| + (n-1)|E_G|$ vertices.

The vertices of $G^{(2)}$ are in natural bijection with the set of vertices and edges of
$G$.

### Diagram built on top of a graph (§9.1, Def. 9.1.3)

A diagram $\Gamma$ is **built on top of** a simple graph $H$ if it contains a subdiagram
of the main roots isomorphic to $H$, and the additional roots are defined in terms of
the main roots by specified rules.

For the two-elementary lattices treated in this paper:
- The **main roots** are the $(-2)$-roots $\alpha$ in $L$ of divisibility 1, i.e.,
  satisfying $\alpha \cdot L = \mathbb{Z}$.
- If a main root $\alpha$ and an additional root $\beta$ are connected, then $\alpha
  \cdot \beta = 2$.

## 6. Main Theorems

### Reflectivity on the $r + a = 22$ line (§9.1, Thm. 9.1.1)

The two-elementary lattices $(17,5,1)$, $(18,4,1)$, and $(19,3,1)$ are **not
reflective**. All other even two-elementary lattices on the line $r + a = 22$ are
reflective.

### Diagrams built on complete graphs (§9.1, Thm. 9.1.4)

For $n = 3, 4, 5, 6$, the Coxeter diagram $\Gamma_r$ of the even two-elementary lattice
$(10+n, 12-n, 1)$ is built on top of $G^{(2)}$, where $G = K_n$ is the complete graph on
$n$ vertices.

The diagram for $(18,4,0)$ is built on top of $G^{(2)}$ where $G = K_{4,4}$ (complete
bipartite on 8 vertices).
The diagram for $(14,8,0)$ is built on top of $G^{(2)}$ where $G = D_4$.

In all cases, $\operatorname{Aut}\Gamma_r = \operatorname{Aut}G$ and $O^+(S) =
\operatorname{Aut}G \ltimes W_r$.

### Completeness of the reflective list (§9.3, Remark 9.3.1)

From experimental data, the list of reflective two-elementary lattices given in Figure
9.1 of the paper may be complete.

## 7. Applications to K3 Automorphism Groups

### K3 surfaces with two-elementary Picard lattice (§9.4)

A K3 surface $X$ whose Picard lattice $S_X$ is two-elementary comes with a canonical
nonsymplectic involution $\iota$ on $X$, acting as $+1$ on $S$ and as $-1$ on the
transcendental lattice $T = S^\perp$ in $L_{\mathrm{K3}}$.

Let $Y = X/\iota$ be the quotient surface (rational unless $S = (10,0,0)$, when $Y$ is
Enriques). Let $B$ be the branch divisor of $\pi: X \to Y$, and
$\operatorname{Aut}'(Y,B)$ the subgroup of $\operatorname{Aut}(Y,B)$ acting trivially on
$\operatorname{Pic}Y$.

### Automorphism exact sequence (§9.4, Lemma 9.4.1)

For a two-elementary K3 lattice $S \neq (10,10,0)$, with Coxeter diagram $\Gamma_r$ and
K3 surface $X$ with $S_X = S$: $$0 \to \langle \iota \rangle \times
\operatorname{Aut}'(Y, B) \to \operatorname{Aut}X \to \operatorname{Sym}\Gamma_r \ltimes
W(\Gamma_4) \to 0,$$ where $\Gamma_4 \subset \Gamma_r$ is the subdiagram formed by the
$(-4)$-roots, and $W(\Gamma_4)$ is the corresponding Coxeter group.

## 9. Vinberg's Algorithm

### Application (§9.3)

The Coxeter diagrams $\Gamma_2$ and $\Gamma_r$ are computed via Vinberg's algorithm.
When the lattice is two-reflective (respectively reflective), the algorithm completes in
finitely many steps.

Each lattice can be written as $U \oplus \Lambda$ or $U(2) \oplus \Lambda$ for some root
lattice $\Lambda$. A control vector $\nu_0$ is chosen in the first summand with $\nu_0^2
= 2$ for $U$ or $\nu_0^2 = 4$ for $U(2)$, and the algorithm proceeds.

### Completeness check (§9.3)

Completeness of diagrams is verified by confirming that the cones in the Minkowski
spaces $\mathbb{R}^{1,r-1}$ defined by the roots of $\Gamma_r$ lie entirely in
$\overline{C}$.

For diagrams without broken edges, the sufficient condition of [13] applies: no Lannér
subgraphs, and every connected parabolic subdiagram is contained in a maximal parabolic
subdiagram of maximal rank.
For diagrams with broken edges, the criterion of [12, Proposition 2] applies.
