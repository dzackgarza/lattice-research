## Contract

The recovered Coble API must express the actual construction chain. Start with a
rational sextic curve, construct its nodes, blow up the plane at those nodes, construct
the Coble surface, compute its Picard group from the blowup, construct the relevant
double cover and ramification data, compute the pullback of Picard classes, and only
then compare the resulting lattice to a standard presentation.

The K3 claim is a geometric invariant computation: the canonical class, irregularity,
geometric genus, singularity resolution context, and cover formulas must be expressed
through geometry nouns and maps. Lattice data are downstream outputs of this pipeline.

This proof pipeline is separate from the eventual standard lattice library. During this
derivation, the code may compare the constructed lattice to a standard presentation
such as `I_{1,10}(2)` or an isometric decomposition only as a target of an explicit
isometry verification. After the proof is accepted, downstream Coble research should
use the canonical sourced `T_Co` object rather than constructing an isometric
presentation or supplying a raw Gram matrix.

## Construction Pipeline

The admitted pipeline is a chain of typed objects and maps:

- `C`: a plane sextic curve in `P^2` with source-backed hypotheses.
- `nodes(C)`: the singular subscheme or list of closed points, verified to be ten
  ordinary nodes.
- `pi: S -> P^2`: the blowup at the node center.
- `E_i`: exceptional divisors of `pi`, each tied to its node.
- `H`: pullback of the hyperplane class from `P^2`.
- `B`: the proper transform of the sextic, expected as the unique effective
  anti-bicanonical divisor in `|-2K_S|` after the hypotheses are verified.
- `S`: the Coble surface object, admitted only after the blowup and anti-canonical
  conditions are recorded.
- `K_S^perp`: the Enriques-type numerical sublattice of `Pic(S)`.
- `h_Co`: a degree-2 polarization class in `K_S^perp`, non-degenerately written
  `F_1 + F_2` with `F_i^2 = 0` and `F_1.F_2 = 1`.
- `f: X -> S`: a degree-two cover with branch divisor `B`, ramification divisor, and
  canonical-cover formula.
- `X_smooth` or the appropriate resolved total space if the direct cover is singular;
  the K3 claim belongs to this typed object, not to the raw equation alone.
- `Pic(S)`: generated by `H, E_1, ..., E_10` with intersection pairing derived from the
  blowup maps.
- `f^*Pic(S)`: the pullback lattice in `H^2(X, ZZ)` or the admitted K3 cohomology
  lattice context, with intersection pairing computed by pullback.
- `tilde h_Co = f^*h_Co`: the K3-side polarization vector, with square doubled from
  `2` to `4`.
- comparison isometry: only after `f^*Pic(S)` exists may the implementation compare it
  to a standard presentation such as `<2> + <-2>^10`.

The node count is not a black-box assertion. For a plane sextic, the arithmetic genus
is `(6-1)(6-2)/2 = 10`; ten ordinary nodes have total delta invariant `10`, so the
normalization has genus zero. This justifies the "rational sextic with ten nodes"
input, provided the nodes and delta invariants are actually verified.

## K3 Verification Contract

The cover side must record the exact theorem or formula used. For a double cover
branched along a divisor `B` with `B ~ 2L`, the canonical class is controlled by
`K_X = f^*(K_S + L)` before any resolution corrections. In the Coble branch, the
relevant claim is that `B` is anti-bicanonical, so `K_S + L` is trivial under the
stated hypotheses. The K3 object must also record the smoothness or resolution context
and the invariant checks used, such as `q = 0`, `p_g = 1`, trivial canonical class, and
the appropriate connectedness/simply-connectedness hypothesis for the selected K3
definition.

When the global category diagnostic flag is enabled, docstrings for K3-cover
constructors should warn if the raw double cover is singular and the returned K3 object
is a resolution or normalized cover, or if the method is comparing against a standard
lattice presentation only after constructing the geometric pullback lattice.

## Picard And Lattice Contract

`Pic(S)` is a Picard group object with divisor generators and maps. The Picard lattice
is a later formed object obtained from the intersection pairing on divisor classes.
For the blowup of `P^2` at ten nodes, the expected basis is `H, E_1, ..., E_10`, with
`H^2 = 1`, `E_i^2 = -1`, and cross-terms zero after the blowup construction proves the
generators and incidence.
The canonical class must be computed in this basis:
`K_S = -3H + sum_i E_i`, and the strict transform of the sextic must have class
`B = 6H - 2 sum_i E_i = -2K_S`. A divisor `D = aH + sum_i c_i E_i` is in
`K_S^perp` iff `-3a - sum_i c_i = 0`; equivalently, for
`D = aH - sum_i b_i E_i`, iff `sum_i b_i = 3a`.

For a finite cover of degree two, pullback doubles intersection numbers:
`f^*D . f^*E = 2 * (D . E)` under the hypotheses where the projection formula applies.
Thus the diagonal lattice `<2> + <-2>^10` is a verification target for the constructed
pullback lattice, not an input to the construction. Discriminant groups, Nikulin
invariants, and orthogonal complements are downstream outputs of this constructed
lattice.

The plane hyperplane class and the moduli polarization must remain separate. `H^2 = 1`,
so `f^*H` has square `2` and is the plane-line class in the constructed K3 pullback
lattice. The Coble moduli polarization is `h_Co in K_S^perp`, with `h_Co^2 = 2`;
therefore its K3 pullback `tilde h_Co` has square `4`. Any implementation that computes
arithmetic groups, folding data, or surgery vectors must name which of these classes is
being used.

## Backend Routes

- Singular: solve partial derivatives, verify ordinary nodes, compute singularity and
  delta-invariant evidence for the sextic.
- Macaulay2/Sage: construct the blowup, exceptional divisors, proper transform,
  canonical divisor, and branch data.
- Sage: construct or coordinate the double-cover surface in a weighted/projective
  model when that is the audited route.
- Macaulay2/Singular/Sage: verify smoothness or resolution data and cohomological
  invariants as source admission permits.
- Oscar/Hecke: build the resulting exact integer lattice only after the Picard group,
  divisor basis, pullback map, and intersection pairing have been produced by geometry.

## Non-Preservation Boundaries

- Do not use `coble_lattice()` as a black box returning an expected lattice without
  the construction evidence.
- Do not treat the Picard lattice statement as input data for the Coble construction.
- Do not call the cover "K3" unless the invariants and hypotheses that prove it are
  recorded or computed.
- Do not conflate the Coble-side Picard lattice with its K3 pullback or orthogonal
  complement.
- Do not use `CobleSurface.from_nodes(points)` unless those points are verified as the
  nodes of a rational sextic or the card explicitly states the weaker construction
  being performed.
- Do not let a standard lattice constructor stand in for the geometric Picard-pullback
  computation.
