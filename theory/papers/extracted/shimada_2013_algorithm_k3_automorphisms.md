# Introduction {#sec:Intro}

The automorphism group $\operatorname{\mathrm {Aut}}\nolimits(X)$ of an algebraic $K3$ surface $X$ is an important and interesting object. Suppose that $X$ is defined over the complex number field $\mathord{\mathbb C}$, or is supersingular in odd characteristic. Then, thanks to the Torelli-type theorem due to Piatetski-Shapiro and Shafarevich [@MR0284440] and Ogus [@MR563467], [@MR717616], we can study $\operatorname{\mathrm {Aut}}\nolimits(X)$ by the Néron-Severi lattice $S_X$ of $X$. We denote by $\mathord{\mathrm {O}}(S_X)$ the orthogonal group of $S_X$. Then we have a natural homomorphism $$\begin{equation*}
\label{eq:naturalhom}
\varphi_X\colon  \operatorname{\mathrm {Aut}}\nolimits(X) \to \mathord{\mathrm {O}}(S_X).
%\varphi_X:  \Aut(X) \to \OG(S_X).
\end{equation*}$$ It is known that this homomorphism has only a finite kernel. Using the reduction theory for arithmetic subgroups of $\mathord{\mathrm {O}}(S_X)$, Sterk [@MR786280] and Lieblich and Maulik [@arXiv11023377] proved that $\operatorname{\mathrm {Aut}}\nolimits(X)$ is finitely generated. The Néron-Severi lattices for which $\operatorname{\mathrm {Aut}}\nolimits(X)$ are finite were classified by Nikulin [@MR633160], [@MR1802343] and Vinberg [@MR2429266]. On the other hand, when $\operatorname{\mathrm {Aut}}\nolimits(X)$ is infinite, it is in general a difficult problem to give a set of generators.

We also have the following related problem. Let $\mathord{\mathrm{Nef}}(X)$ denote the nef cone of $X$; that is, the cone of $S_X\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}$ consisting of vectors $x\in S_X\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}$ such that $\langle{x, C}\rangle_{}\ge 0$ holds for any curve $C$ on $X$, where $\langle{\phantom{a}, \phantom{a}}\rangle_{}$ is the intersection form on $S_X$. In order to classify various geometric objects on $X$ (for example, smooth rational curves, Jacobian fibrations, or polarizations of a fixed degree) *modulo* $\operatorname{\mathrm {Aut}}\nolimits(X)$, it is useful to describe explicitly a fundamental domain of the action of $\operatorname{\mathrm {Aut}}\nolimits(X)$ on $\mathord{\mathrm{Nef}}(X)$.

Several authors have studied these problems by using the idea of Borcherds [@MR913200], [@MR1654763] to embed $S_X$ into an even unimodular hyperbolic lattice of rank $26$. In these works, however, they required that $S_X$ should satisfy a certain strong condition (see Section `\ref{subsec:history}`{=latex} below for the details), and hence the range of applications is limited.

The purpose of this paper is to present an algorithm (Algorithm `\ref{algo:main}`{=latex}) that calculates, under assumptions on $S_X$ milder than the preceding works, a finite set of generators of the image of $\varphi_X$ and a closed domain $F$ of $\mathord{\mathrm{Nef}}(X)$ with the following properties:

- For any $v\in \mathord{\mathrm{Nef}}(X)$, there exists an element $g\in \operatorname{\mathrm {Aut}}\nolimits(X)$ such that $v^g\in F$.

- The domain $F$ is tiled by a finite number of convex cones, which we call chambers. Each chamber is bounded by a finite number of hyperplanes and its stabilizer subgroup in $\operatorname{\mathrm {Aut}}\nolimits(X)$ is finite.

See Remark `\ref{rem:weakfund}`{=latex} for the relation of $F$ with a fundamental domain of the action of $\operatorname{\mathrm {Aut}}\nolimits(X)$ on $\mathord{\mathrm{Nef}}(X)$. The detailed description of the assumptions we impose on $S_X$ will be given in Section `\ref{sec:geometricapplication}`{=latex}.

The algorithm can be applied to a wide class of $K3$ surfaces. We give two examples.

**Example 1**. `\label{example:24}`{=latex} As an example of a $K3$ surface with small Picard number and an infinite automorphism group, we consider a complex $K3$ surface $X$ whose Néron-Severi lattice $S_X$ has a Gram matrix $$M:=\left[\begin{array}{ccc}
0 & 1 & 0 \\
1 & -2 & 0\\
0 & 0 & -24
\end{array}\right]$$ with respect to a certain basis $f_{\phi}, z_{\phi},  v$ such that $f_{\phi}$ and $z_{\phi}$ are the classes of a fiber and the zero section of a Jacobian fibration $\phi: X\to \mathord{\mathbb  P}^1$. Then the Mordell-Weil group $\mathord{\mathrm{MW}}_{\phi}$ of $\phi$ is isomorphic to $\mathord{\mathbb Z}$. Hence $\operatorname{\mathrm {Aut}}\nolimits(X)$ contains an infinite subgroup $\mathord{\mathrm{MW}}_{\phi}\rtimes\mathord{\mathbb Z}/2\mathord{\mathbb Z}$ (see Section `\ref{sec:rho3}`{=latex}). We assume that the period $\omega_X$ of $X$ is generic in $T_X\otimes\mathord{\mathbb C}$, where $T_X$ is the transcendental lattice of $X$. We let $\mathord{\mathrm {O}}(S_X)$ act on $S_X$ from the right so that $$\mathord{\mathrm {O}}(S_X)=\{\; {g\in \mathord{\mathrm {GL}}_3(\mathord{\mathbb Z})} \; \mid \; {g \,M\,{}^t g=M} \;  \}.$$ Then $\varphi_X$ is injective, and its image is generated by the following matrices: $$\left[
\begin{array}{ccc}
1 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 1 & 0\\
0 & 0 & -1
\end{array}\right],
\quad
\left[
\begin{array}{ccc}
1 & 0 & 0 \\
12 & 1 & -1\\
24 & 0 & -1
\end{array}\right],
\quad
\left[\begin{array}{ccc}
37 & 12 & -5 \\
36 & 13 & -5\\
360 & 120 & -49
\end{array}\right],
\quad
\left[\begin{array}{ccc}
97 & 48 & -14 \\
0 & 1 & 0\\
672 & 336& -97
\end{array}\right].$$ The first two elements of these four matrices are the images of the involutions that generate $\mathord{\mathrm{MW}}_{\phi}\rtimes\mathord{\mathbb Z}/2\mathord{\mathbb Z}\cong \mathord{\mathbb Z}/2\mathord{\mathbb Z}* \mathord{\mathbb Z}/2\mathord{\mathbb Z}$. We need two more automorphisms to generate the full automorphism group $\operatorname{\mathrm {Aut}}\nolimits(X)$. Moreover we can show that $\operatorname{\mathrm {Aut}}\nolimits(X)$ acts on the set of smooth rational curves on $X$ transitively. In Figure `\ref{figure:Poincare}`{=latex}, we give a tessellation of $\mathord{\mathrm{Nef}}(X)$ by a fundamental domain of $\operatorname{\mathrm {Aut}}\nolimits(X)$. See Section `\ref{sec:rho3}`{=latex} or the author's web-page [@compdataweb] for more examples of this type.

::: example
**Example 2**. `\label{example:singK3disc11}`{=latex} A complex algebraic $K3$ surface is said to be *singular* if its Picard number attains the possible maximum $20$. By the result of Shioda and Inose [@MR0441982], the isomorphism class of a singular $K3$ surface $X$ is determined by its oriented transcendental lattice $T_X$, and $\operatorname{\mathrm {Aut}}\nolimits(X)$ is always infinite. See [@MR1820211] for the standard Gram matrices $$\left[
\begin{array}{cc} 
a & b \\ b & c 
\end{array}
\right]$$ of oriented transcendental lattices of singular $K3$ surfaces. Let $\operatorname{\mathrm {disc}}\nolimits T_X$ denote the discriminant of $T_X$. Since $T_X$ is an even lattice, we see that $\operatorname{\mathrm {disc}}\nolimits T_X=ac-b^2$ is congruent to $0\;\textrm{or}\; 3 \bmod 4$. We consider singular $K3$ surfaces $X$ with small $\operatorname{\mathrm {disc}}\nolimits T_X$. The automorphism groups of the singular $K3$ surfaces $X$ with $\operatorname{\mathrm {disc}}\nolimits T_X=3$ and $4$ were determined by Vinberg [@MR719348], and $\operatorname{\mathrm {Aut}}\nolimits(X)$ of the singular $K3$ surface $X$ with $\operatorname{\mathrm {disc}}\nolimits T_X=7$ was determined by Ujikawa [@MR3113614]. The case where $\operatorname{\mathrm {disc}}\nolimits T_X=8$ can be handled by *usual* Borcherds' method (see Section `\ref{sec:singK3}`{=latex}). Therefore the next example to be considered is the singular $K3$ surface $X$ whose transcendental lattice is given by a Gram matrix $$\left[
\begin{array}{cc} 
2 & 1 \\ 1 & 6
\end{array}
\right].$$ In this case, $\varphi_X$ is injective. We find that $\operatorname{\mathrm {Im}}\nolimits\varphi_X$ is generated by $767$ elements of $\mathord{\mathrm {O}}(S_X)$, and, as in Example `\ref{example:24}`{=latex}, we can present them explicitly as $20\times 20$ matrices with respect to a certain basis of $S_X$. These matrices and related computational data are given in the author's web-page [@compdataweb]. From these data, we see that the number of smooth rational curves on $X$ is at most $347$ modulo $\operatorname{\mathrm {Aut}}\nolimits(X)$. (Note that the number $767$ of generators of $\operatorname{\mathrm {Aut}}\nolimits(X)$ and the upper-bound $347$ of the number of smooth rational curves modulo $\operatorname{\mathrm {Aut}}\nolimits(X)$ are not minimal.) See Section `\ref{sec:singK3}`{=latex} for more examples of this type.
:::

Our main algorithm (Algorithm `\ref{algo:main}`{=latex}) can be applied to a wide class of $K3$ surfaces theoretically. The most crucial assumption that $S_X$ be embedded primitively into an even unimodular hyperbolic lattice of rank $10$, $18$ or $26$ is always satisfied when $X$ is defined over $\mathord{\mathbb C}$ (see Proposition `\ref{prop:embeddable}`{=latex}). However, experiments show that the computational complexity of Algorithm `\ref{algo:main}`{=latex} grows rapidly as the rank and the discriminant of $S_X$ become large.

To the best knowledge of the author, the automorphism group of the complex Fermat quartic surface $X$ is still unknown. This surface is a singular $K3$ surface with $\operatorname{\mathrm {disc}}\nolimits T_X=64$, and we can apply our algorithm to this surface. It turns out, however, that the computation is very heavy, and we could not finish the calculation (see Remark `\ref{rem:complexFQ}`{=latex}).

Even when the computation is too heavy to be completed in a reasonable time, our algorithm yields many interesting automorphisms and projective models of the given $K3$ surface on the way of computation. We have applied this method to the supersingular $K3$ surface in characteristic $5$ with Artin invariant $1$ in [@MR3286672].

It is a totally different problem to give geometrically an automorphism $g$ of $X$ such that $\varphi_X(g)$ is equal to a given matrix in $\mathord{\mathrm {O}}(S_X)$. In [@ShimadaAutSingK3], we discuss an algorithmic approach to this problem, and apply it to certain singular $K3$ surfaces.

## The difference of our algorithm from the preceding works {#subsec:history}

We briefly review Borcherds' method [@MR913200], [@MR1654763], and its applications to $K3$ surfaces. A lattice of rank $n>1$ is said to be *hyperbolic* if its signature is $(1, n-1)$. Let $S$ be an even hyperbolic lattice of rank $<26$. A *positive cone* $\mathord{\mathcal P}_S$ of $S$ is one of the two connected components of $\{ {x\in S\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}} \,|\, {x^2>0}   \}$. We denote by $\mathord{\mathrm {O}}^+(S)$ the stabilizer subgroup of $\mathord{\mathcal P}_S$ in $\mathord{\mathrm {O}}(S)$. Let $W(S)$ denote the subgroup of $\mathord{\mathrm {O}}^+(S)$ generated by the reflections in the hyperplanes $(r)\sp{\perp}$ perpendicular to $r$, where $r$ runs through the set $\mathord{\mathcal R}_S:=\{ {r\in S} \,|\, {r^2=-2}   \}$. The mirrors $(r)\sp{\perp}$ decompose $\mathord{\mathcal P}_{S}$ into the union of standard fundamental domains of the action of $W(S)$ on $\mathord{\mathcal P}_S$. We call each of these standard fundamental domains an *$\mathord{\mathcal R}_{S}\sp*$-chamber*. Let $G$ be a subgroup of $\mathord{\mathrm {O}}^+(S)$ with finite index. Let $N$ be an $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{S}\sp*$-chamber, and let $\operatorname{\mathit {Aut}}\nolimits_{G}(N)$ denote the stabilizer subgroup in $G$ of the $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{S}\sp*$-chamber $N$. In the application to a complex $K3$ surface $X$, $S$ is the Néron-Severi lattice $S_X$, $\mathord{\mathcal P}_S$ is the connected component containing an ample class, the group $G$ consists of elements $g\in \mathord{\mathrm {O}}^+(S)$ liftable to isometries of $H^2(X, \mathord{\mathbb Z})$ that preserve the period of $X$, and $N$ is the intersection of $\mathord{\mathrm{Nef}}(X)$ with $\mathord{\mathcal P}_S$.

Borcherds gave a method to calculate a finite set of generators of $\operatorname{\mathit {Aut}}\nolimits_{G}(N)$. Suppose that $S$ is primitively embedded into an even unimodular hyperbolic lattice $\mathord{\mathbf{L}}:=\textrm{II}_{1, 25}$ of rank $26$ in such a way that every $g\in G$ lifts to an isometry of $\mathord{\mathbf{L}}$. We also assume that the orthogonal complement $R$ of $S$ in $\mathord{\mathbf{L}}$ cannot be embedded into the (negative-definite) Leech lattice. Let $\mathord{\mathcal P}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}\subset \mathord{\mathbf{L}}\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}$ be the positive cone of $\mathord{\mathbf{L}}$ containing $\mathord{\mathcal P}_S$. The structure of $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}\sp*$-chambers is well-understood by Conway [@MR1662447 Chapter 27]. Then the tessellation of $\mathord{\mathcal P}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$ by the $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}\sp*$-chambers induces a tessellation of $\mathord{\mathcal P}_S$, which is invariant under the action of $G$. We call the tiles constituting this induced tessellation *$\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chambers*. Note that the $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{S}\sp*$-chamber $N$ is a union of $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chambers. Two $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chambers $D$ and $D\sp\prime$ are said to be *$G$-congruent* if there exists an element $g\in G$ that maps $D$ to $D\sp\prime$. By the reduction theory for arithmetic subgroups of $\mathord{\mathrm {O}}(S)$, we see that the number of $G$-congruence classes of $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chambers is finite. Let $$\mathord{\mathbb D}:=\{ D_0, \dots, D_{m-1}\}$$ be a complete set of representatives of $G$-congruence classes of $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chambers such that each $D_i$ is contained in $N$. From the list $\mathord{\mathbb D}$, we can obtain a finite set of generators of $\operatorname{\mathit {Aut}}\nolimits_{G}(N)$.

Kondo [@MR1618132] applied this method to the Néron-Severi lattice of a generic Jacobian Kummer surface, and described its automorphism group. Since then, automorphism groups of the following $K3$ surfaces have been determined by this method;

- the supersingular $K3$ surface in characteristic $2$ with Artin invariant $1$ by Dolgachev and Kondo [@MR1935564K],

- complex Kummer surfaces of product type by Keum and Kondo [@MR1806732],

- the Hessian quartic surface by Dolgachev and Keum [@MR1897389],

- the singular $K3$ surface $X$ with $\operatorname{\mathrm {disc}}\nolimits T_X=7$ by Ujikawa [@MR3113614],

- the supersingular $K3$ surface in characteristic $3$ with Artin invariant $1$ by Kondo and Shimada [@KondoShimada].

The classical two examples of Vinberg [@MR719348] can also be treated by this method.

In all these examples, the number $|\mathord{\mathbb D}|$ of $G$-congruence classes of $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chambers is $1$. Borcherds [@MR913200] studied the case where the orthogonal complement $R$ of $S$ in $\mathord{\mathbf{L}}$ contains a root sublattice of finite index, and gave in [@MR913200 Lemma 5.1] a sufficient condition for any two $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chambers to be $\mathord{\mathrm {O}}^+(S)$-congruent. In particular, the method employed in the above examples is limited to $K3$ surfaces $X$ such that $S_X$ can be embedded primitively in $\mathord{\mathbf{L}}$ with $R$ containing a root lattice as a sublattice of finite index. Note that, for example, only a finite number of isomorphism classes of singular $K3$ surfaces satisfy this condition.

We extend Borcherds' method to the situation in which $|\mathord{\mathbb D}|$ is not necessarily $1$. In fact, we have $|\mathord{\mathbb D}|=46$ in Example `\ref{example:24}`{=latex} and $|\mathord{\mathbb D}|=1098$ in Example `\ref{example:singK3disc11}`{=latex} above. Starting from an initial $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chamber, we compute $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chambers adjacent to the $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chambers obtained so far successively until no new $G$-congruence classes appear. Each $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chamber is expressed by its Weyl vector (see Definition `\ref{def:Sweylvector}`{=latex}). Thus our main algorithm contains sub-algorithms that calculate the set of walls of a given $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chamber (Algorithm `\ref{algorithm:aSnS}`{=latex}), compute the Weyl vector of the adjacent $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chamber across a given wall (Algorithm `\ref{algorithm:adjacent}`{=latex}), and determine whether an $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chamber is $G$-congruent to another $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chamber (Algorithm `\ref{algorithm:Gequiv}`{=latex}). In these algorithms, we use methods given in our previous paper [@MR3166075]. In the calculation of the set of walls, we also employ the standard algorithm of linear programming (Algorithm `\ref{algorithm:mindefset}`{=latex}).

# Preliminaries on lattices and hyperbolic spaces {#sec:lattice}

Let $L$ be a free $\mathord{\mathbb Z}$-module of finite rank. We say that a submodule $M$ of $L$ is *primitive* if $L/M$ is torsion-free, and that $v\in L$ is *primitive* if so is the submodule $\langle {v}  \rangle:=\mathord{\mathbb Z}v\subset L$.

Let $L$ be a lattice; that is, a free $\mathord{\mathbb Z}$-module of finite rank with a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form $\langle{\phantom{\cdot}, \phantom{\cdot}}\rangle_{L}\colon L\times L\to \mathord{\mathbb Z}$. The subscript $L$ in $\langle{\phantom{\cdot}, \phantom{\cdot}}\rangle_{L}$ is omitted if no confusion will occur. The symmetric bilinear form on $L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}$ obtained from $\langle{\phantom{\cdot}, \phantom{\cdot}}\rangle_{}$ is also denoted by $\langle{\phantom{\cdot}, \phantom{\cdot}}\rangle_{}$. We denote by $L\sp{\vee}:=\mathord{\mathrm {Hom}}(L, \mathord{\mathbb Z})$ the *dual lattice of $L$*, which is naturally embedded in $L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb  Q}$ by $\langle{\phantom{\cdot}, \phantom{\cdot}}\rangle_{}$. We say that $L$ is *unimodular* if $L=L\sp{\vee}$ holds. The *norm* $\langle{v,v}\rangle_{}$ of $v\in L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}$ is denoted by $v^2$. We let the *orthogonal group* $\mathord{\mathrm {O}}(L)$ of $L$ act on $L$ from the right, and write the action of $g\in \mathord{\mathrm {O}}(L)$ on $v\in L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}$ by $v\mapsto v^g$.

A lattice $L$ is said to be *even* if $x^2\in 2\mathord{\mathbb Z}$ holds for any $x\in L$. For an even lattice $L$, we put $$\mathord{\mathcal R}_L:=\{\; {r\in L} \; \mid \; {r^2=-2} \;  \}.$$ Elements of $\mathord{\mathcal R}_L$ are called *$(-2)$-vectors*. Each $r\in \mathord{\mathcal R}_L$ defines the *reflection* $$s_r \colon x\mapsto x+\langle{x, r}\rangle_{} r,$$ which is an element of $\mathord{\mathrm {O}}(L)$. We denote by $W(L)$ the subgroup of $\mathord{\mathrm {O}}(L)$ generated by all reflections $s_r$ with respect to the $(-2)$-vectors $r\in \mathord{\mathcal R}_L$, and call it the *Weyl group*.

A lattice $L$ of rank $n>0$ is said to be *negative-definite* if the signature of the real quadratic space $L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}$ is $(0, n)$. A negative-definite lattice $L$ is said to be a *root lattice* if $L$ is generated by $\mathord{\mathcal R}_L$.

A lattice $L$ of rank $n >1$ is said to be *hyperbolic* if the signature of $L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}$ is $(1, n-1)$. Let $L$ be a hyperbolic lattice. Then a *positive cone of $L$* is one of the two connected components of $\{ {x\in L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}} \,|\, {x^2>0}   \}$. The closure of a positive cone $\mathord{\mathcal P}_L$ in $L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}$ is denoted by $\overline{\mathord{\mathcal P}}_L$. We denote by $\overline{\mathord{\mathcal P}}^{\,\mathord{\mathbb  Q}}_L$ the convex hull of $\overline{\mathord{\mathcal P}}_L\cap(L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb  Q})$. The stabilizer subgroup in $\mathord{\mathrm {O}}(L)$ of a positive cone is denoted by $\mathord{\mathrm {O}}^+(L)$. We have $\mathord{\mathrm {O}}(L)=\mathord{\mathrm {O}}^+(L)\times\{\pm 1\}$. Note that $W(L)$ is contained in $\mathord{\mathrm {O}}^+(L)$.

Let $L$ be a hyperbolic lattice of rank $m+1$, and let $\mathord{\mathcal P}_L$ be a positive cone of $L$. The multiplicative group $\mathord{\mathbb R}_{>0}$ of positive real numbers acts on $\overline{\mathord{\mathcal P}}_L\setminus\{0\}$ by scalar multiplication. We put $$\begin{eqnarray*}
&&\overline{\mathord{\mathbb H}}_L:=(\overline{\mathord{\mathcal P}}_L\setminus\{0\})/\mathord{\mathbb R}_{>0}, \quad \overline{\mathord{\mathbb H}}^{\,\mathord{\mathbb  Q}}_L:=(\overline{\mathord{\mathcal P}}^{\,\mathord{\mathbb  Q}}_L\setminus\{0\})/\mathord{\mathbb R}_{>0}, \quad
\mathord{\mathbb H}_L:=\mathord{\mathcal P}_L/\mathord{\mathbb R}_{>0},
\\ 
&&\partial\,\overline{\mathord{\mathbb H}}_L:=\overline{\mathord{\mathbb H}}_L\setminus \mathord{\mathbb H}_L, \quad \partial\,\overline{\mathord{\mathbb H}}^{\,\mathord{\mathbb  Q}}_L:=\overline{\mathord{\mathbb H}}^{\,\mathord{\mathbb  Q}}_L\setminus \mathord{\mathbb H}_L,
\end{eqnarray*}$$ and denote by $\pi_L \colon \overline{\mathord{\mathcal P}}_L\setminus\{0\}\to \overline{\mathord{\mathbb H}}_L$ the natural projection. The space $\mathord{\mathbb H}_L$ is naturally endowed with a structure of the hyperbolic $m$-space. A point of $\partial\,\overline{\mathord{\mathbb H}}^{\,\mathord{\mathbb  Q}}_L$ is called a *rational boundary point*. For simplicity, when we are given a subset $T$ of $\overline{\mathord{\mathcal P}}_L$, we denote by $\pi_L(T)\subset\overline{\mathord{\mathbb H}}_L$ the image of $T\setminus\{0\}$ by $\pi_L$. For example, we have $\partial\,\overline{\mathord{\mathbb H}}^{\,\mathord{\mathbb  Q}}_L=\pi_L(\overline{\mathord{\mathcal P}}_L\cap L)$. A subset $K$ of $\mathord{\mathbb H}_L$ is said to be a *linear subspace of $\mathord{\mathbb H}_L$* if there exists a linear subspace $\tilde{K}$ of $L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}$ such that $K=\pi_L(\mathord{\mathcal P}_L\cap \tilde{K})$ holds. Let $b$ be a point of $\partial\,\overline{\mathord{\mathbb H}}_L$. A linear subspace $K$ of $\mathord{\mathbb H}_L$ is said to *pass through $b$ at infinity* if the linear subspace $\tilde{K}$ of $L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}$ that satisfies $K=\pi_L(\mathord{\mathcal P}_L\cap \tilde{K})$ contains a non-zero vector $v$ such that $b=\pi_L(v)$.

We denote by $\mathord{\mathcal H}_L$ the set of hyperplanes of $\mathord{\mathcal P}_L$. We put $$\mathord{\mathcal N}_L:=\{\; {v\in L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}} \; \mid \; {v^2<0} \;  \},$$ and, for $v\in L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}$, we put $$[v]\sp{\perp}:=\{\; {x\in L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}} \; \mid \; {\langle{x, v}\rangle_{}=0} \;  \}
\quad\textrm{and}\quad
(v)\sp{\perp}:=[v]\sp{\perp}\cap \mathord{\mathcal P}_L.$$ Since $[v]\sp{\perp}$ intersects $\mathord{\mathcal P}_L$ if and only if $v^2<0$, the map $v\mapsto (v)\sp{\perp}$ induces a bijection from $\mathord{\mathcal N}_L/\mathord{\mathbb R}\sp{\times}$ to $\mathord{\mathcal H}_L$. For $v\in \mathord{\mathcal N}_L$, we obtain a hyperplane $\pi_L((v)\sp{\perp})$ of the hyperbolic space $\mathord{\mathbb H}_L$.

We recall some properties of *horospheres*. For the details, see Ratcliffe [@MR1299730 Chapter 4]. Let $b$ be a point of $\partial\,\overline{\mathord{\mathbb H}}_L$. Consider the upper halfspace model $$\begin{equation}
\label{eq:upper}
\{\; {(z_1, \dots, z_m)\in \mathord{\mathbb R}^m} \; \mid \; {z_1>0} \;  \}
\end{equation}$$ of $\mathord{\mathbb H}_L$ such that $b$ corresponds to the infinite point given by $z_1=\infty$. Then every horosphere $\mathord{H\hskip -1pt S}_b$ with the base $b$ is defined by $z_1=\gamma$ with some positive real constant $\gamma$. Therefore, as a Riemannian submanifold of $\mathord{\mathbb H}_L$, every horosphere is isomorphic to a Euclidean affine space. If $K$ is a linear subspace of $\mathord{\mathbb H}_L$ that passes through $b$ at infinity, then $K\cap \mathord{H\hskip -1pt S}_b$ is an affine subspace of any horosphere $\mathord{H\hskip -1pt S}_b$ with the base $b$. We say that a horosphere defined by $z_1=\gamma$ is *smaller* than a horosphere defined by $z_1=\gamma\sp\prime$ if $\gamma>\gamma\sp\prime$.

The definition of horospheres can be restated as follows. Let $f$ be a non-zero vector in $\overline{\mathord{\mathcal P}}_L$ with $f^2=0$ such that $\pi_L(f)=b$. Then the function $$h_f\colon v\mapsto \langle{v, f}\rangle_{}^2/v^2$$ on $\mathord{\mathcal N}_L\cup \mathord{\mathcal P}_L\cup (-\mathord{\mathcal P}_L)$ is invariant under the scaling of $v$ by multiplicative constants, and hence its restriction to $\mathord{\mathcal P}_L$ induces a function $$\bar{h}_f\colon \mathord{\mathbb H}_L \to \mathord{\mathbb R}_{>0}.$$ The horospheres with the base $b$ are exactly the level sets of the function $\bar{h}_f$. Note that the horosphere defined by $\bar{h}_f=\alpha$ is smaller than the horosphere defined by $\bar{h}_f=\alpha\sp\prime$ if and only if $\alpha<\alpha\sp\prime$.

The following lemma should be well-known, but we could not find appropriate references. Let $f$ and $b$ be as above. The function $-h_f(v)=-\langle{v, f}\rangle_{}^2/v^2$ restricted to $\mathord{\mathcal N}_L$ measures how far the hyperplane $\pi_L((v)\sp{\perp})$ of $\mathord{\mathbb H}_L$ is from the boundary point $b$. For a horosphere $\mathord{H\hskip -1pt S}_b$ with the base $b$, we put $$c(f, \mathord{H\hskip -1pt S}_b):=\sup\; \{\; {-h_f(v)} \; \mid \; {v\in \mathord{\mathcal N}_L, \;\;\pi_L((v)\sp{\perp})\cap \mathord{H\hskip -1pt S}_b \ne \emptyset} \;  \}.$$

::: lemma
**Lemma 6**. *`\label{lemma:HS}`{=latex} Suppose that $\mathord{H\hskip -1pt S}_b$ is defined by $\bar{h}_f=\alpha$. Then we have $c(f, \mathord{H\hskip -1pt S}_b)\le \alpha$.*
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* We choose linear coordinates $(x_0, x_1, \dots, x_m)$ of $L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}$ such that the quadratic form $x\mapsto x^2$ on $L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}$ is given by $$(x_0, x_1, \dots, x_m)\mapsto x_0^2-x_1^2-x_2^2-\cdots-x_m^2,$$ such that $\mathord{\mathcal P}_L$ is contained in the halfspace $x_0> 0$, and such that $f=(1,1,0,\dots, 0)$. Let $v=(v_0,  v_1, \dots, v_m)$ be a vector in $\mathord{\mathcal N}_L$. Suppose that there exists a vector $x=(x_0, x_1, \dots, x_m)$ in $\mathord{\mathcal P}_L$ such that $x\in (v)\sp{\perp}$ and that $\pi_L(x)\in \mathord{H\hskip -1pt S}_b$. It is enough to show that $$\begin{equation}
\label{eq:target}
%-\frac{\intM{v, f}{}^2}{v^2}\le \alpha.
-h_f(v)\le \alpha.
\end{equation}$$ Rescaling $x$ by a positive real constant, we can assume that $x^2=1$. If $\langle{v, f}\rangle_{}=0$, then `\eqref{eq:target}`{=latex} holds. Suppose that $\langle{v, f}\rangle_{}\ne 0$. Replacing $v$ by $-v$ if necessary, we can assume that $\langle{v, f}\rangle_{}=v_0-v_1$ is positive. Since $\pi_L(x)\in \mathord{H\hskip -1pt S}_b$, we have $h_f(x)=\alpha$. Since $x\in \mathord{\mathcal P}_L$, we have $\langle{x, f}\rangle_{}=x_0-x_1>0$. Combining these, we get $$x_0-x_1=\sqrt{\alpha}.$$ Using $\langle{v, x}\rangle_{}=v_0x_0-v_1 x_1- \dots-v_m x_m=0$, we obtain $$x_0=\frac{(v_2x_2+\cdots+v_m x_m)-v_1\sqrt{\alpha}}{v_0-v_1},
\quad
x_1=\frac{(v_2x_2+\cdots+v_m x_m)-v_0\sqrt{\alpha}}{v_0-v_1}.$$ Combining these with $x^2=1$ and using $v_0\ne v_1$, we get $$\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gsum}
g_2(x_2)+\cdots +g_m(x_m)+\alpha(v_0+v_1)+(v_0-v_1)=0,
\end{equation}$$ where $$g_i(t)=(v_0-v_1)\,t^2-2\sqrt{\alpha} \,v_i t.$$ Since $\langle{v, f}\rangle_{}=v_0-v_1>0$, we have $g_i(t)\ge -\alpha v_i^2/(v_0-v_1)$ for any $t\in \mathord{\mathbb R}$. Thus we obtain $\alpha v^2+\langle{v, f}\rangle_{}^2\le 0$ from `\eqref{eq:gsum}`{=latex}. Since $v^2<0$, we get `\eqref{eq:target}`{=latex}. ◻
:::

# Chamber decomposition {#sec:chamber}

Let $L$ be an even hyperbolic lattice with a fixed positive cone $\mathord{\mathcal P}_L$. For a subset $\Delta$ of $\mathord{\mathcal N}_L=\{ {v\in L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}} \,|\, {v^2<0}   \}$, we define a cone $\Sigma_L(\Delta)$ in $L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}$ by $$\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Sigma}
\Sigma_L(\Delta):=\{\; {x\in L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}} \; \mid \; {\langle{x, v}\rangle_{}\ge 0\;\;\textrm{for all}\;\; v\in \Delta} \;  \}.
\end{equation}$$

::: definition
**Definition 7**. `\label{def:chamber}`{=latex} A closed subset $D$ of $\mathord{\mathcal P}_L$ is called a *chamber* if its interior $D\sp{\mathord{\circ}}$ is non-empty and there exists a subset $\Delta$ of $\mathord{\mathcal N}_L$ such that $D=\Sigma_L(\Delta)\cap \mathord{\mathcal P}_L$ holds. Let $D$ be a chamber. A hyperplane $(v)\sp{\perp}$ of $\mathord{\mathcal P}_L$ is called a *wall of $D$* if $(v)\sp{\perp}\cap D\sp{\mathord{\circ}}$ is empty and $(v)\sp{\perp}\cap D$ contains a non-empty open subset of $(v)\sp{\perp}$.
:::

::: definition
**Definition 8**. Let $\mathord{\mathcal F}\subset\mathord{\mathcal H}_L$ be a locally finite family of hyperplanes in $\mathord{\mathcal P}_L$. Then the closure in $\mathord{\mathcal P}_L$ of each connected component of $$\mathord{\mathcal P}_L\setminus \bigcup_{(v)\sp{\perp}\in \mathord{\mathcal F}}\, (v)\sp{\perp}$$ is a chamber, which we call an *$\mathord{\mathcal F}$-chamber*.
:::

By definition, every wall of an $\mathord{\mathcal F}$-chamber is an element of $\mathord{\mathcal F}$. If $D$ and $D\sp\prime$ are distinct $\mathord{\mathcal F}$-chambers, then $D\sp{\mathord{\circ}}\cap D\sp\prime=\emptyset$ holds.

::: definition
**Definition 9**. Let $D$ be an $\mathord{\mathcal F}$-chamber, and let $(v)\sp{\perp}\in \mathord{\mathcal F}$ be a wall of $D$. Then there exists a unique $\mathord{\mathcal F}$-chamber $D\sp\prime$ such that $D\sp\prime\ne D$ and that $D\cap D\sp\prime\cap (v)\sp{\perp}$ contains a non-empty open subset of $(v)\sp{\perp}$. We say that $D\sp\prime$ is *adjacent to $D$ across the wall $(v)\sp{\perp}$*.
:::

We fix a subgroup $G$ of $\mathord{\mathrm {O}}^+(L)$ with finite index. We assume that $G$ satisfies the following condition of the existence of a membership algorithm:

- There exists an algorithm by which we can determine, for a given $g\in \mathord{\mathrm {O}}^+(L)$, whether $g\in G$ or not.

Let $\mathord{\mathcal V}$ be a subset of $\mathord{\mathcal N}_L\cap L\sp{\vee}=\{ {v\in L\sp{\vee}} \,|\, {v^2<0}   \}$, and consider the family of hyperplanes $$\mathord{\mathcal V}\sp*:=\{\; {(v)\sp{\perp}} \; \mid \; {v\in \mathord{\mathcal V}} \;  \}$$ in $\mathord{\mathcal P}_L$. We assume that $\mathord{\mathcal V}$ has the following properties:

- There exists a positive real number $c\in \mathord{\mathbb R}$ such that, for any $v\in \mathord{\mathcal V}$, we have $-v^2<c$.

- The set $\mathord{\mathcal V}$ is invariant under the action of $G$ on $\mathord{\mathcal N}_L \cap L\sp{\vee}$.

Then we can consider $\mathord{\mathcal V}\sp*$-chambers by the following:

::: lemma
**Lemma 10**. *`\label{lem:locallyfinite}`{=latex} The family $\mathord{\mathcal V}\sp*$ of hyperplanes is locally finite in $\mathord{\mathcal P}_L$.*
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* Since $\{ {v^2} \,|\, {v\in L\sp{\vee}}   \}$ is discrete in $\mathord{\mathbb R}$, the subset $\{ {v^2} \,|\, {v\in \mathord{\mathcal V}}   \}$ of the interval $(-c, 0)\subset \mathord{\mathbb R}$ is finite by $\mathord{\rm [V1]}$ and $\mathord{\mathcal V}\subset \mathord{\mathcal N}_L\cap L\sp{\vee}$. For a negative real number $a$ and a compact subset $J$ of $\mathord{\mathcal P}_L$, the locus $$\{\; {x\in L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}} \; \mid \; {x^2=a,  \,\, (x)\sp{\perp}\cap J\ne \emptyset} \;  \}$$ is compact. Since $L\sp{\vee}$ is discrete in $L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}$, we obtain the proof of Lemma `\ref{lem:locallyfinite}`{=latex}. ◻
:::

::: definition
**Definition 11**. Let $D$ be a $\mathord{\mathcal V}\sp*$-chamber. A subset $\Delta$ of $\mathord{\mathcal N}_L\cap L\sp{\vee}$ is called a *defining set of $D$* if $D=\Sigma_{L}(\Delta)\cap \mathord{\mathcal P}_L$ holds. A defining set $\Delta$ of $D$ is said to be *minimal* if the following hold:

- For any $v\in \Delta$, the hyperplane $(v)\sp{\perp}$ is a wall of $D$, and

- if $v$ and $v\sp\prime$ are distinct vectors of $\Delta$, then $(v)\sp{\perp}\ne (v\sp\prime)\sp{\perp}$.

We define two types of minimal defining sets, each of which is unique for a given $\mathord{\mathcal V}\sp*$-chamber $D$. The one is called the *$\mathord{\mathcal V}$-minimal defining set*, denoted by $\Delta_{\mathord{\mathcal V}}(D)$, and characterized by the following property: $\Delta_{\mathord{\mathcal V}}(D)$ is a subset of $\mathord{\mathcal V}$, and if $v\in \Delta_{\mathord{\mathcal V}}(D)$, then $\alpha v\notin \mathord{\mathcal V}$ for any $\alpha\in \mathord{\mathbb R}$ with $0<\alpha<1$. The other is called the *primitively minimal defining set*, denoted by $\Delta_{L\sp{\vee}}(D)$, and characterized by the following property: Every $v\in \Delta_{L\sp{\vee}}(D)$ is primitive in $L\sp{\vee}$. (Note that $\Delta_{L\sp{\vee}}(D)$ may not be contained in $\mathord{\mathcal V}$, because elements of $\mathord{\mathcal V}$ need not be primitive in $L\sp{\vee}$.)
:::

Let $D$ be a $\mathord{\mathcal V}\sp*$-chamber. Then $D^g$ is also a $\mathord{\mathcal V}\sp*$-chamber for any $g\in G$ by the property $\mathord{\rm [V2]}$. For a $\mathord{\mathcal V}\sp*$-chamber $D$, we put $$\operatorname{\mathit {Aut}}\nolimits_G(D):=\{\; {g\in G} \; \mid \; {D^g=D} \;  \}.$$ Let $D$ and $D\sp\prime$ be $\mathord{\mathcal V}\sp*$-chambers. We say that $D$ and $D\sp\prime$ are *$G$-congruent* if there exists an element $g\in G$ such that $D\sp\prime=D^g$. The following simple observation is the key point of our method: $$\begin{equation}
\label{eq:intpt}
\textrm{
If $g\in G$ satisfies $D^g\cap D\sp{\prime\circ}\ne\emptyset$, 
then $D^g=D\sp\prime$.}
\end{equation}$$ In particular, if $g\in G$ satisfies $D^g\cap D\sp{\mathord{\circ}}\ne\emptyset$, then $g\in \operatorname{\mathit {Aut}}\nolimits_G(D)$.

::: example
**Example 12**. `\label{example:W}`{=latex} The subset $\mathord{\mathcal R}_L$ of $\mathord{\mathcal N}_L\cap L\sp{\vee}$ has the properties $\mathord{\rm [V1]}$ and $\mathord{\rm [V2]}$ for $G=\mathord{\mathrm {O}}^+(L)$. Let $D$ be an $\mathord{\mathcal R}_L\sp*$-chamber. Then $D$ is a fundamental domain of the action of the Weyl group $W(L)$ on $\mathord{\mathcal P}_L$, and $W(L)$ is generated by the reflections $s_r$, where $r$ runs through the $\mathord{\mathcal R}_L$-minimal defining set $\Delta_{\mathord{\mathcal R}_L}(D)$ of $D$. Moreover, any two $\mathord{\mathcal R}_L\sp*$-chambers are $\mathord{\mathrm {O}}^+(L)$-congruent, and $\mathord{\mathrm {O}}^+(L)$ is isomorphic to the semi-direct product $W(L)\rtimes\operatorname{\mathit {Aut}}\nolimits_{\mathord{\mathrm {O}}^+(L)}(D)$.
:::

We consider the following properties of $\mathord{\mathcal V}$:

- Any $\mathord{\mathcal V}\sp*$-chamber has a *finite* defining set.

- For any $\mathord{\mathcal V}\sp*$-chamber $D$, the set $\pi_L(\overline{D})\cap \partial\,\overline{\mathord{\mathbb H}}_L$ is contained in $\partial\,\overline{\mathord{\mathbb H}}^{\,\mathord{\mathbb  Q}}_L$, where $\overline{D}$ is the closure of $D$ in $\overline{\mathord{\mathcal P}}_L$.

The main results of this section are the following:

::: theorem
**Theorem 13**. *`\label{thm:finiteGequiv}`{=latex} Suppose that $\mathord{\mathcal V}$ satisfies $\mathord{\rm [V1]}$-$\mathord{\rm [V4]}$. Then there exist only a finite number of $G$-congruence classes of $\mathord{\mathcal V}\sp*$-chambers.*
:::

::: theorem
**Theorem 14**. *`\label{thm:finiteautG}`{=latex} Suppose that $\mathord{\mathcal V}$ satisfies $\mathord{\rm [V1]}$-$\mathord{\rm [V4]}$. Then the group $\operatorname{\mathit {Aut}}\nolimits_G(D)$ is finite for any $\mathord{\mathcal V}\sp*$-chamber $D$.*
:::

## Proof of Theorem `\ref{thm:finiteGequiv}`{=latex}

We assume that $\mathord{\mathcal V}$ satisfies $\mathord{\rm [V1]}$-$\mathord{\rm [V4]}$.

::: definition
**Definition 15**. A subset $\Pi$ of $\overline{\mathord{\mathcal P}}^{\,\mathord{\mathbb  Q}}_L$ is called a *rational polyhedral cone* if there exist a finite number of non-zero vectors $v_1, \dots, v_n\in \overline{\mathord{\mathcal P}}_L\cap L$ such that $$\Pi=\mathord{\mathbb R}_{\ge 0} v_1+ \cdots+ \mathord{\mathbb R}_{\ge 0} v_n.$$
:::

Recall that $G$ is assumed to be of finite index in $\mathord{\mathrm {O}}^+(L)$. We have the following result from the reduction theory of arithmetic subgroups of $\mathord{\mathrm {O}}(L)$ (see Ash et al. [@MR2590897 Chapter II, Section 4] and Sterk [@MR786280]).

::: theorem
**Theorem 16**. *`\label{thm:ratpoly}`{=latex} There exist a finite number of rational polyhedral cones $\Pi_1, \dots, \Pi_N$ in $\overline{\mathord{\mathcal P}}^{\,\mathord{\mathbb  Q}}_L$ such that $\mathord{\mathcal P}_L$ is equal to $$\bigcup_{g \in G} \bigcup_{i=1}^N \,(\Pi_i^g\cap \mathord{\mathcal P}_L).$$*
:::

Therefore Theorem `\ref{thm:finiteGequiv}`{=latex} follows from the following:

::: proposition
**Proposition 17**. *`\label{prop:Pi}`{=latex} Let $\Pi$ be a rational polyhedral cone in $\overline{\mathord{\mathcal P}}^{\,\mathord{\mathbb  Q}}_L$. Then the number of $\mathord{\mathcal V}\sp*$-chambers that intersect $\Pi\cap\mathord{\mathcal P}_L$ is finite.*
:::

For the proof of Proposition `\ref{prop:Pi}`{=latex}, we need two corollaries of Lemma `\ref{lemma:HS}`{=latex}. Let $b$ be a point of $\partial\,\overline{\mathord{\mathbb H}}_L$. A *closed horoball* $\mathord{H\hskip -1pt B}_b$ with the base $b$ is a subset of $\mathord{\mathbb H}_L$ defined by $z_1\ge \gamma$ with some positive real constant $\gamma$ in the upper halfspace model `\eqref{eq:upper}`{=latex} with $b$ at $z_1=\infty$. Let $\mathord{\partial\,\hskip -1.2pt H\hskip -1.2pt B}_b$ be the horosphere defined by $z_1=  \gamma$. The map $\rho_b\colon \mathord{H\hskip -1pt B}_b\to \mathord{\partial\,\hskip -1.2pt H\hskip -1.2pt B}_b$ defined by $$\rho_b(z_1, z_2, \dots, z_m):=(\gamma, z_2, \dots, z_m)$$ is called the *natural projection*. Let $b=\pi_L(f)\in \partial\,\overline{\mathord{\mathbb H}}^{\,\mathord{\mathbb  Q}}_L$ be a rational boundary point, where $f$ is a non-zero vector in $\overline{\mathord{\mathcal P}}_L \cap L$ with $f^2=0$. We put $$\mathord{\mathcal V}_b:=\{\; {v\in \mathord{\mathcal V}} \; \mid \; {\langle{v, f}\rangle_{}=0} \;  \}.$$ If $v\in \mathord{\mathcal V}$ satisfies $v\notin\mathord{\mathcal V}_b$, then we have $\langle{v, f}\rangle_{}^2\ge 1$ because $f\in L$ and $\mathord{\mathcal V}\subset L\sp{\vee}$. By the property $\mathord{\rm [V1]}$, there exists a positive real number $\delta_{b}$ such that $$\delta_{b}< -\langle{v,f}\rangle_{}^2/v^2\quad \textrm{for any $v\in \mathord{\mathcal V}\setminus \mathord{\mathcal V}_b$}.$$ Therefore we obtain the following corollary of Lemma `\ref{lemma:HS}`{=latex}:

::: corollary
**Corollary 18**. *`\label{cor:HS}`{=latex} Let $b$ be a rational boundary point. If we choose a sufficiently small closed horoball $\mathord{H\hskip -1pt B}_b$ with the base $b$, then the following hold.*

*(1) Let $v$ be an element of $\mathord{\mathcal V}$. Then the hyperplane $\pi_L((v)\sp{\perp})$ of $\mathord{\mathbb H}_L$ intersects $\mathord{H\hskip -1pt B}_b$ if and only if $\pi_L((v)\sp{\perp})$ passes through $b$ at infinity.*

*(2) Let $D$ be a $\mathord{\mathcal V}\sp*$-chamber. If $b\notin \pi_L(\overline{D})$, then $\pi_L(D)\cap\mathord{H\hskip -1pt B}_b$ is empty, whereas if $b\in \pi_L(\overline{D})$, then $\pi_L(D)\cap\mathord{H\hskip -1pt B}_b=\rho_b\sp{-1}(\pi_L(D)\cap\mathord{\partial\,\hskip -1.2pt H\hskip -1.2pt B}_b )$ holds.*
:::

We regard $\mathord{\partial\,\hskip -1.2pt H\hskip -1.2pt B}_b$ as a Euclidean affine space. Then the family of affine hyperplanes $$\{\; {\pi_L((v)\sp{\perp})\cap \mathord{\partial\,\hskip -1.2pt H\hskip -1.2pt B}_b } \; \mid \; {v\in \mathord{\mathcal V}_b} \;  \}$$ of $\mathord{\partial\,\hskip -1.2pt H\hskip -1.2pt B}_b$ is locally finite, because $\mathord{\mathcal V}\sp*$ is locally finite in $\mathord{\mathcal P}_L$ and $\pi_L\sp{-1}(\mathord{\partial\,\hskip -1.2pt H\hskip -1.2pt B}_b )\subset\mathord{\mathcal P}_L$. Therefore we obtain the following:

::: corollary
**Corollary 19**. *`\label{cor:J}`{=latex} Let $b$ and $\mathord{H\hskip -1pt B}_b$ be as in Corollary `\ref{cor:HS}`{=latex}, and let $J$ be a compact subset of $\mathord{\partial\,\hskip -1.2pt H\hskip -1.2pt B}_b$. Then the number of $\mathord{\mathcal V}\sp*$-chambers that intersect the subset $\pi_L\sp{-1}(\rho_b\sp{-1}(J))$ of $\mathord{\mathcal P}_L$ is finite.*
:::

::: proof
*Proof of Proposition `\ref{prop:Pi}`{=latex}.* Let $v_1, \dots, v_n\in \overline{\mathord{\mathcal P}}_L\cap L$ be non-zero vectors such that rational polyhedral cone $\Pi$ is equal to $\mathord{\mathbb R}_{\ge 0} v_1+ \cdots+ \mathord{\mathbb R}_{\ge 0} v_n$. We number $v_1, \dots, v_n$ in such a way that $$v_1^2=\cdots=v_k^2=0
\quad\textrm{and}\quad
v_{k+1}^2>0, \;\; \dots, \;\;  v_{n}^2>0.$$ Let $b_i$ be the rational boundary point $\pi_L(v_i)$ for $i=1, \dots, k$, and let $\mathord{H\hskip -1pt B}_i$ be a sufficiently small closed horoball with the base $b_i$. The natural projection is denoted by $\rho_i :\mathord{H\hskip -1pt B}_i\to \mathord{\partial\,\hskip -1.2pt H\hskip -1.2pt B}_i$. We put $\mathord{H\hskip -1pt B}_i\sp{\mathord{\circ}}:=\mathord{H\hskip -1pt B}_i\setminus \mathord{\partial\,\hskip -1.2pt H\hskip -1.2pt B}_i$. Since $\pi_L(\Pi)\cap\partial\,\overline{\mathord{\mathbb H}}_L=\{b_1, \dots, b_k\}$, we see that $$\pi_L(\Pi)\sp\prime:=\pi_L(\Pi)\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^k (\mathord{H\hskip -1pt B}_i\sp{\mathord{\circ}}\cap \pi_L(\Pi))$$ is a compact subset of $\mathord{\mathbb H}_L$. Therefore the number of $\mathord{\mathcal V}\sp*$-chambers $D$ such that $\pi_L(D)$ intersects $\pi_L(\Pi)\sp\prime$ is finite. For $j\ne i$, let $p_{i, j}\in \mathord{\partial\,\hskip -1.2pt H\hskip -1.2pt B}_i$ denote the intersection point of $\mathord{\partial\,\hskip -1.2pt H\hskip -1.2pt B}_i$ and the geodesic line in $\mathord{\mathbb H}_L$ passing through $b_i$ at infinity and passing through $\pi_L(v_j)$ possibly at infinity. Then the convex hull $J_i$ of these points $p_{i, j}$ with $j\ne i$ in the Euclidean affine space $\mathord{\partial\,\hskip -1.2pt H\hskip -1.2pt B}_i$ is compact and satisfies $\mathord{H\hskip -1pt B}_i \cap \pi_L(\Pi)=\rho_i\sp{-1}(J_i)$. Consequently, the number of $\mathord{\mathcal V}\sp*$-chambers $D$ such that $\pi_L(D)$ intersects $\mathord{H\hskip -1pt B}_i \cap \pi_L(\Pi)$ is finite by Corollary `\ref{cor:J}`{=latex}. ◻
:::

## Proof of Theorem `\ref{thm:finiteautG}`{=latex}

We continue to assume that $\mathord{\mathcal V}$ satisfies $\mathord{\rm [V1]}$-$\mathord{\rm [V4]}$.

::: lemma
**Lemma 20**. *`\label{lem:clSigma}`{=latex} Let $\Delta$ be a subset of $\mathord{\mathcal N}_L\cap L\sp{\vee}$ such that $D=\Sigma_L(\Delta)\cap\mathord{\mathcal P}_L$ is a $\mathord{\mathcal V}\sp*$-chamber. Then $\Sigma_L(\Delta)$ is contained in $\overline{\mathord{\mathcal P}}_L$.*
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* Note that $\Sigma_L(\Delta)$ is a closed convex subset of $L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}$. Suppose that there exists an element $x_0\in \Sigma_L(\Delta)$ such that $x_0\notin\overline{\mathord{\mathcal P}}_L$. Let $U$ be a non-empty open subset of $D\sp{\mathord{\circ}}$. Then, for any $y\in U$, the line segment $\overline{x_0 y}$ of $L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}$ connecting $x_0$ and $y$ is contained in $\Sigma_L(\Delta)$, and $\overline{x_0 y}\cap \mathord{\mathcal P}_L$ is contained in $D$. Hence the intersection point $z(y)$ of $\overline{x_0 y}$ and $\partial\,\overline{\mathord{\mathcal P}}_L=\{ {x\in \overline{\mathord{\mathcal P}}_L} \,|\, {x^2=0}   \}$ belongs to the closure $\overline{D}$ of $D$ in $\overline{\mathord{\mathcal P}}_L$. Since $U$ is open, the subset $\pi_L(\{ {z(y)} \,|\, {y\in U}   \})$ of $\pi_L(\overline{D})\cap\partial\,\overline{\mathord{\mathbb H}}_L$ has uncountably many points, which contradicts the property $\mathord{\rm [V4]}$ of $\mathord{\mathcal V}$. ◻
:::

::: lemma
**Lemma 21**. *`\label{lem:span}`{=latex} Let $D$ be a $\mathord{\mathcal V}\sp*$-chamber. Then any defining set $\Delta$ of $D$ spans $L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}$.*
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* Let $V$ be the linear subspace of $L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}$ spanned by $\Delta$. The orthogonal complement $V\sp{\perp}$ of $V$ in $L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}$ is contained in $\Sigma_L(\Delta)$ by definition and hence $V\sp{\perp}\subset\overline{\mathord{\mathcal P}}_L$ by Lemma `\ref{lem:clSigma}`{=latex}. This holds only when $V\sp{\perp}=0$. ◻
:::

Theorem `\ref{thm:finiteautG}`{=latex} is now easy to prove by $\mathord{\rm [V3]}$. We give, however, a proof based on an algorithm (Algorithm `\ref{algorithm:autG}`{=latex}) to compute $\operatorname{\mathit {Aut}}\nolimits_G(D)$.

::: lemma
**Lemma 22**. *`\label{lem:iswall}`{=latex} Suppose that a defining set $\Delta_1$ of a $\mathord{\mathcal V}\sp*$-chamber $D$ satisfies the following; if $v, v\sp\prime\in \Delta_1$ are distinct, then $(v)\sp{\perp}\ne (v\sp\prime)\sp{\perp}$ holds. Let $v$ be an element of $\Delta_1$. (1) The hyperplane $(v)\sp{\perp}$ is a wall of $D$ if and only if $\Sigma_L(\Delta_1) \ne \Sigma_L(\Delta_1\setminus \{v\})$. (2) If $\Delta_1\setminus\{v\}$ does not span $L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}$, then $(v)\sp{\perp}$ is a wall of $D$.*
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* The 'only if' part of (1) is obvious by the assumption on $\Delta_1$. Conversely, suppose that $\Sigma_L(\Delta_1) \ne \Sigma_L(\Delta_1\setminus \{v\})$. Then there exists a vector $x_0\in L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}$ such that $\langle{v, x_0}\rangle_{}<0$ and $x_0\in \Sigma_L(\Delta_1\setminus \{v\})$. Assume that $(v)\sp{\perp}$ is not a wall of $D$. Then $D$ is equal to $\Sigma_L(\Delta_1\setminus \{v\})\cap \mathord{\mathcal P}_L$, and hence $\Sigma_L(\Delta_1\setminus \{v\})$ is contained in $\overline{\mathord{\mathcal P}}_L$ by Lemma `\ref{lem:clSigma}`{=latex}. In particular, we have $x_0\in \overline{\mathord{\mathcal P}}_L$. Let $y_0$ be a point of $D\sp{\mathord{\circ}}$. Then the intersection point $z_0$ of the line segment $\overline{x_0y_0}$ and $(v)\sp{\perp}$ satisfies $z_0^2> 0$, $\langle{v, z_0}\rangle_{}=0$ and $\langle{v\sp\prime, z_0}\rangle_{}>0$ for any $v\sp\prime$ in $\Delta_1\setminus \{v\}$. Since $\mathord{\mathcal V}\sp*$ is locally finite in $\mathord{\mathcal P}_L$, these mean that a sufficiently small open neighborhood of $z_0$ in $(v)\sp{\perp}$ is contained in $D$. In anyway, $(v)\sp{\perp}$ is a wall of $D$. Thus (1) is proved. If $(v)\sp{\perp}$ is not a wall of $D$, then $\Delta_1\setminus\{v\}$ is also a defining set of $D$. Hence (2) follows from Lemma `\ref{lem:span}`{=latex}. ◻
:::

::: algorithm
**Algorithm 23**. `\label{algorithm:mindefset}`{=latex} Let $\Delta$ be a *finite* defining set of a $\mathord{\mathcal V}\sp*$-chamber $D$. This algorithm calculates the primitively minimal defining set $\Delta_{L\sp{\vee}}(D)$ of $D$.

*Step 0*. We set $\Delta_1:=\{\}$ and $\Delta_2:=\{\}$.

*Step 1*. For each element $v\in \Delta$, we calculate the maximal positive integer $a_v$ such that $v/a_{v}\in L\sp{\vee}$, and append $v/a_{v}$ to the set $\Delta_1$. Then we have $D=\Sigma_L(\Delta_1)\cap \mathord{\mathcal P}_L$, and moreover, if $v, v\sp\prime\in \Delta_1$ are distinct, then $(v)\sp{\perp}\ne (v\sp\prime)\sp{\perp}$ holds.

*Step 2*. For each $v\in \Delta_1$, we carry out the following computation. Suppose that $\Delta_1\setminus\{v\}$ does not span $L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}$. Then $(v)\sp{\perp}$ is a wall of $D$ by Lemma `\ref{lem:iswall}`{=latex}, and we append $v$ to $\Delta_2$. Suppose that $\Delta_1\setminus\{v\}$ spans $L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}$. Then we can solve the following problem of linear programming, in which the variable $x$ ranges through the vector space $L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb  Q}$: $$\begin{equation}
\label{eq:LP}
\begin{cases}
\textrm{minimize} &  \langle{v, x}\rangle_{}\\
\textrm{subject to} & \langle{v\sp\prime, x}\rangle_{}\ge 0 \;\; \textrm{for all}\;\; v\sp\prime\in \Delta_1\setminus \{v\}.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}$$ (See, for example, Chvátal [@MR717219] for the algorithms of linear programming.) Note that the solution is either $0$ or unbounded to $-\infty$. If the solution is $0$, then $(v)\sp{\perp}$ is not a wall of $D$ by Lemma `\ref{lem:iswall}`{=latex}. Suppose that the solution is unbounded to $-\infty$. Then there exists a vector $x_0\in L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}$ such that $\langle{v, x_0}\rangle_{}<0$ and $x_0\in \Sigma_L(\Delta_1\setminus \{v\})$. Hence $(v)\sp{\perp}$ is a wall of $D$ by Lemma `\ref{lem:iswall}`{=latex}, and we append $v$ to the set $\Delta_2$.

*Step 3*. We then output $\Delta_2$ as $\Delta_{L\sp{\vee}}(D)$.
:::

Remark that, for any $\mathord{\mathcal V}\sp*$-chamber $D$, the primitively minimal defining set $\Delta_{L\sp{\vee}}(D)$ is finite by the property $\mathord{\rm [V3]}$ of $\mathord{\mathcal V}$ and Algorithm `\ref{algorithm:mindefset}`{=latex}. We use the obvious brute-force method based on the finiteness of $\Delta_{L\sp{\vee}}(D)$ in the following two algorithms.

::: algorithm
**Algorithm 24**. `\label{algorithm:autG}`{=latex} Suppose that the primitively minimal defining set $\Delta_{L\sp{\vee}}(D)$ of a $\mathord{\mathcal V}\sp*$-chamber $D$ is given. This algorithm calculates all elements of $\operatorname{\mathit {Aut}}\nolimits_G(D)$. Let $\Delta_{L\sp{\vee}}(D)^l$ denote the set of ordered $l$-tuples of distinct elements of $\Delta_{L\sp{\vee}}(D)$, where $l:=\operatorname{\mathrm {rank}}\nolimits L$. By Lemma `\ref{lem:span}`{=latex}, there exists an $l$-tuple $[v_1, \dots, v_l]\in \Delta_{L\sp{\vee}}(D)^l$ that forms a basis of $L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb  Q}$. We set $A:=\{\}$. For each $[v_1\sp\prime, \dots, v_l\sp\prime]\in \Delta_{L\sp{\vee}}(D)^l$, we calculate the linear transformation $g$ of $L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb  Q}$ such that $$v_i^g=v_i\sp\prime\qquad (i=1, \dots, l).$$ Recall that we can determine whether $g\in G$ or not by the assumption $\mathord{\rm [G]}$. If $g$ belongs to $G$ and induces a permutation of $\Delta_{L\sp{\vee}}(D)$, then we append $g$ to $A$. When this calculation is done for all $[v_1\sp\prime, \dots, v_l\sp\prime]\in \Delta_{L\sp{\vee}}(D)^l$, the set $A$ is equal to $\operatorname{\mathit {Aut}}\nolimits_G(D)$.
:::

::: proof
*Proof of Theorem `\ref{thm:finiteautG}`{=latex}.* Since $\Delta_{L\sp{\vee}}(D)$ is finite, Algorithm `\ref{algorithm:autG}`{=latex} terminates in finite steps. ◻
:::

::: algorithm
**Algorithm 25**. `\label{algorithm:Gequiv}`{=latex} Let $D$ and $D\sp\prime$ be $\mathord{\mathcal V}\sp*$-chambers. Suppose that $\Delta_{L\sp{\vee}}(D)$ and $\Delta_{L\sp{\vee}}(D\sp\prime)$ are given. This algorithm determines whether $D$ is $G$-congruent to $D\sp\prime$ or not. We fix an element $[v_1, \dots, v_l]$ of $\Delta_{L\sp{\vee}}(D)^l$ that forms a basis of $L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb  Q}$. For each $[v_1\sp{\prime\prime}, \dots, v_l\sp{\prime\prime}]\in \Delta_{L\sp{\vee}}(D\sp\prime)^l$, we calculate the linear transformation $g$ of $L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb  Q}$ that satisfies $v_i^g=v_i\sp{\prime\prime}$ for $i=1, \dots, l$. If $g$ belongs to $G$ and induces a bijection from $\Delta_{L\sp{\vee}}(D)$ to $\Delta_{L\sp{\vee}}(D\sp\prime)$, then $D$ and $D\sp\prime$ are $G$-congruent. If no such $[v_1\sp{\prime\prime}, \dots, v_l\sp{\prime\prime}]$ are found, then $D$ and $D\sp\prime$ are not $G$-congruent.
:::

::: remark
*Remark 26*. `\label{rem:intpt}`{=latex} Suppose that $p$ is a point in the interior of $D$. By `\eqref{eq:intpt}`{=latex}, we see that an element $g\in G$ is contained in $\operatorname{\mathit {Aut}}\nolimits_G(D)$ if and only if $p^g\in D$, and $g\in G$ induces an isomorphism from $D$ to $D\sp\prime$ if and only if $p^g\in D\sp\prime$.
:::

# Vinberg-Conway theory {#sec:Conway}

Let $n$ be $10$, $18$ or $26$. Throughout this section, we denote by $\mathord{\mathbf{L}}$ an even unimodular hyperbolic lattice $\mathrm{II}_{1, n-1}$ of rank $n$. Note that $\mathord{\mathbf{L}}$ exists and is unique up to isomorphism (see, for example,  [@MR0344216 Chapter V]). We fix a positive cone $\mathord{\mathcal P}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$ of $\mathord{\mathbf{L}}$. Vinberg [@MR0422505] and Conway [@MR1662447 Chapter 27] described the structure of $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}\sp*$-chambers; that is, the standard fundamental domains of the action of $W(\mathord{\mathbf{L}})$ on $\mathord{\mathcal P}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$ (see Example `\ref{example:W}`{=latex}).

::: definition
**Definition 27**. `\label{def:LLLweylvector}`{=latex} Let $\mathord{\mathcal D}$ be an $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}\sp*$-chamber. We say that a vector $w\in \mathord{\mathbf{L}}$ is a *Weyl vector of $\mathord{\mathcal D}$* if the $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$-minimal defining set $\Delta_{\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}}(\mathord{\mathcal D})$ of $\mathord{\mathcal D}$ is given by $$\begin{equation*}
\label{eq:DeltaL}
\Delta_{\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}}(\mathord{\mathcal D})=\{\; {r\in \mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}} \; \mid \; {\langle{w, r}\rangle_{}=1} \;  \}.
\end{equation*}$$
:::

If a Weyl vector of an $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}\sp*$-chamber $\mathord{\mathcal D}$ exists, then it is unique, because, as will be shown below, $\Delta_{\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}}(\mathord{\mathcal D})$ spans $\mathord{\mathbf{L}}\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}$. If $w$ is the Weyl vector of $\mathord{\mathcal D}$, then $w^g$ is the Weyl vector of $\mathord{\mathcal D}^g$ for any $g\in \mathord{\mathrm {O}}^+(\mathord{\mathbf{L}})$. Since any two $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}\sp*$-chambers are $\mathord{\mathrm {O}}^+(\mathord{\mathbf{L}})$-congruent, the Weyl vector of a single $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}\sp*$-chamber gives Weyl vectors of all $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}\sp*$-chambers via the action of $\mathord{\mathrm {O}}^+(\mathord{\mathbf{L}})$.

::: theorem
**Theorem 28** (Conway, Chapter 27 of [@MR1662447]). *`\label{thm:Weyl}`{=latex} For any $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}\sp*$-chamber $\mathord{\mathcal D}$, there exists a Weyl vector $w\in \mathord{\mathbf{L}}$ of $\mathord{\mathcal D}$. We have $$w^2=\begin{cases}
1240 & \textrm{if $n=10$} \\
620 & \textrm{if $n=18$} \\
0 & \textrm{if $n=26$} \\
\end{cases}
\;\;\quad\textrm{and}\quad\;\;
|\Delta_{\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}}(\mathord{\mathcal D})|=\begin{cases}
10 & \textrm{if $n=10$} \\
19 & \textrm{if $n=18$} \\
\infty  & \textrm{if $n=26$}. \\
\end{cases}$$*
:::

::: remark
*Remark 29*. The finite sets $\Delta_{\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}}(\mathord{\mathcal D})$ for $n=10$ and $18$ had been calculated by Vinberg [@MR0422505].
:::

We give an explicit description of the Weyl vectors and the set $\Delta_{\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}}(\mathord{\mathcal D})$, and prove the following:

::: proposition
**Proposition 30**. *`\label{prop:bdrQ}`{=latex} If $\mathord{\mathcal D}$ is an $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}\sp*$-chamber, then $\pi_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}(\overline{\mathord{\mathcal D}})\cap \partial\,\overline{\mathord{\mathbb H}}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$ is contained in $\partial\,\overline{\mathord{\mathbb H}}^{\,\mathord{\mathbb  Q}}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$.*
:::

## The case where $n=26$

We denote by $\Lambda$ the *negative-definite* Leech lattice; that is, $\Lambda$ is an even unimodular negative-definite lattice of rank $24$ such that $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\Lambda}=\emptyset$. We put $\mathord{\mathbf{L}}:=U\oplus \Lambda$, and choose $\mathord{\mathcal P}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$ in such a way that $2f_U+z_U\in \mathord{\mathcal P}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$.

::: theorem
**Theorem 32** (Conway and Sloane, Chapter 26 of [@MR1662447]). *`\label{thm:ConwaySloane}`{=latex} A vector $w\in \mathord{\mathbf{L}}$ is a Weyl vector of some $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}\sp*$-chamber if and only if $w$ is a non-zero primitive vector of norm $0$ contained in $\overline{\mathord{\mathcal P}}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$ such that $\langle {w}  \rangle\sp{\perp}/\langle {w}  \rangle$ is isomorphic to $\Lambda$, where $\langle {w}  \rangle\sp{\perp}$ is the orthogonal complement of the primitive submodule $\langle {w}  \rangle$ of $\mathord{\mathbf{L}}$.*
:::

Therefore $w_0:=f_U$ is the Weyl vector of an $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}\sp*$-chamber $\mathord{\mathcal D}_0$. For simplicity, we denote vectors of $\mathord{\mathbf{L}}\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}=(U\oplus\Lambda)\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}$ by $$[s, t, y]:=s f_U +t z_U +y, \quad\textrm{where}\quad s, t\in \mathord{\mathbb R},\;\; y\in \Lambda\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}.$$ Then we have $$\Delta_{\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}}(\mathord{\mathcal D}_0)=\{\; {r_{\lambda}} \; \mid \; {\lambda\in \Lambda} \;  \},
\quad\textrm{where}\quad
%r_{\lambda}:=\left[-\frac{\lambda^2}{2}, 1, \lambda\right].
r_{\lambda}:=\left[-\lambda^2/2, 1, \lambda\right].$$ Conway, Parker and Sloane [@MR660415] ([@MR1662447 Chapter 23]) proved that the covering radius of the Leech lattice is $\sqrt{2}$ (see also Borcherds [@MR791880]). A point $c$ of $\Lambda\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}$ is called a *deep hole* if $-(c-\lambda)^2\ge 2$ holds for any $\lambda\in \Lambda$. In Lemma 4.4 of [@MR913200], Borcherds observed the following:

::: lemma
**Lemma 33**. *`\label{lem:property_d_L_26}`{=latex} Let $b$ be a point of $\pi_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}} (\overline{\mathord{\mathcal D}}_0)\cap\partial\,\overline{\mathord{\mathbb H}}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$. Then either $b=\pi_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}(w_0)$ or there exists a deep hole $c$ such that $$b=\pi_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}(v_c), \quad\textrm{where}\quad
%v_c:=\left[-\frac{c^2}{2}+1, 1, c\right].
v_c:=\left[-{c^2}/{2}+1, 1, c\right].$$ In particular, the set $\pi_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}} (\overline{\mathord{\mathcal D}}_0)\cap\partial\,\overline{\mathord{\mathbb H}}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$ is contained in $\partial\,\overline{\mathord{\mathbb H}}^{\,\mathord{\mathbb  Q}}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$.*
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* Note that $\overline{\mathord{\mathcal P}}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$ is contained in $\{ {x\in \mathord{\mathbf{L}}\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}} \,|\, {\langle{w_0, x}\rangle_{}\ge 0}   \}$, and that $x\in \overline{\mathord{\mathcal P}}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$ satisfies $\langle{x, w_0}\rangle_{}=0$ if and only if $x$ belongs to the half-line $\mathord{\mathbb R}_{\ge 0} w_0$. Suppose that $b=\pi_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}(u)$, where $u=[s, t, y]$ is a non-zero vector of norm $0$ in $\overline{\mathord{\mathcal D}}_0$. Since $u\in \overline{\mathord{\mathcal P}}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$, we have $t=\langle{w_0, u}\rangle_{}\ge 0$, and $t=0$ holds if and only if $\mathord{\mathbb R}_{\ge 0} w_0= \mathord{\mathbb R}_{\ge 0} u$. Hence $t=0$ implies $b=\pi_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}(w_0)$. Suppose that $t>0$. We can assume that $t=1$. Since $u^2=0$, we have $2s-2+y^2=0$. Since $u\in \overline{\mathord{\mathcal D}}_0$, we have $$\langle{u, r_{\lambda}}\rangle_{}=-\frac{(y-\lambda)^2}{2}-1\ge 0\;\;\textrm{for any}\;\; \lambda\in \Lambda.$$ Therefore $y\in \Lambda\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}$ is a deep hole $c$ and $u=v_c$ holds. Let $p_1, \dots, p_N$ be the points of $\Lambda$ nearest to $c$; that is, $p_1, \dots, p_N$ are the points of $\Lambda$ satisfying $(p_i-c)^2=-2$. Then their differences $p_i-p_j$ span $\Lambda\otimes\mathord{\mathbb  Q}$, and $c$ is the intersection point of the bisectors of distinct two points of $p_1, \dots, p_N$. Hence $c$ belongs to $\Lambda\otimes\mathord{\mathbb  Q}$, and we have $\pi_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}(u)\in \partial\,\overline{\mathord{\mathbb H}}^{\,\mathord{\mathbb  Q}}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$. ◻
:::

*Remark 34*. `\label{rem:deepholes}`{=latex} The coordinates of deep holes of $\Lambda$ are explicitly given in Conway, Parker and Sloane [@MR660415].
:::

::: remark
*Remark 35*. `\label{rem:UE83}`{=latex} We have an isomorphism $$\mathord{\mathbf{L}}\cong  U\oplus E_8 \oplus E_8 \oplus E_8.$$ The vector $w_E\in U\oplus E_8^3$ given by $[30, 1, 1^8, 1^8, 1^8]$ in terms of the dual basis $f\sp{\vee}, z\sp{\vee}$, $e_1\sp{\vee}, \dots, e_8^{\prime\prime\vee}$ is a Weyl vector of $\mathord{\mathbf{L}}$. Indeed, let $u\in U\oplus E_8^3$ be the vector given by $[1, -2, 0^8, 0^8, 0^8]$ in terms of the dual basis above. Since $w_E^2=0$ and $\langle{w_0, u}\rangle_{}=1$, the vectors $w_E$ and $u$ span an even hyperbolic unimodular lattice of rank $2$, and its orthogonal complement in $U\oplus E_8^3$ is isomorphic to $\langle {w_E}  \rangle\sp{\perp}/\langle {w_E}  \rangle$. Calculating a Gram matrix of this orthogonal complement and using Algorithm `\ref{algo:QLc}`{=latex}, we confirm that $\langle {w_E}  \rangle\sp{\perp}/\langle {w_E}  \rangle$ has no $(-2)$-vectors, and therefore is isomorphic to $\Lambda$.

# Generalized Borcherds' method {#sec:Borcherds}

Suppose that $S$ is an even hyperbolic lattice with a fixed positive cone $\mathord{\mathcal P}_S$, and let $G$ be a subgroup of $\mathord{\mathrm {O}}\sp+(S)$ with finite index satisfying the existence of membership algorithm $\mathord{\rm [G]}$ in Section `\ref{sec:chamber}`{=latex} with $L$ replaced by $S$. We present an algorithm that calculates a set of generators of $\operatorname{\mathit {Aut}}\nolimits_G(N)=\{ {g\in G} \,|\, {N^g=N}   \}$ for a given $\mathord{\mathcal R}_S\sp*$-chamber $N$ under the assumptions $\mathord{\rm [SG1]}$, $\mathord{\rm [SG2]}$ and $\mathord{\rm [SG3]}$ below.

We recall the definition of the discriminant form of an even lattice. See Nikulin [@MR525944] for the details. For an even lattice $L$, the *discriminant group* $A_{L}:=L\sp{\vee}/L$ is equipped with a non-degenerate quadratic form $$q_L \colon A_{L}\to \mathord{\mathbb  Q}/2\mathord{\mathbb Z}, \qquad x \bmod L\mapsto x^2 \bmod 2\mathord{\mathbb Z},$$ which is called the *discriminant form of $L$*. Let $\mathord{\mathrm {O}}(q_L)$ denote the group of automorphisms of $(A_{L}, q_L)$. We have a natural homomorphism $$\eta_L \colon \mathord{\mathrm {O}}(L)\to \mathord{\mathrm {O}}(q_L).$$ For an isomorphism $\delta\colon (A_1, q_1)\mathbin{\,\raise -.6pt\rlap{$\to$}\raise 3.5pt \hbox{\hskip .3pt$\mathord{\sim}$}\;}(A_2, q_2)$ of discriminant forms, we denote by $$\delta_*\colon \mathord{\mathrm {O}}(q_1)\mathbin{\,\raise -.6pt\rlap{$\to$}\raise 3.5pt \hbox{\hskip .3pt$\mathord{\sim}$}\;}\mathord{\mathrm {O}}(q_2)$$ the induced isomorphism on the automorphism groups.

Let $n$ be $10$, $18$ or $26$. As in Section `\ref{sec:Conway}`{=latex}, we denote by $\mathord{\mathbf{L}}$ an even unimodular hyperbolic lattice of rank $n$. We assume that $S$ satisfies the following embeddability condition:

- \quad 

  [$S$ is primitively embedded into $\mathord{\mathbf{L}}$.]{.roman}

Let $R$ denote the orthogonal complement of $S$ in ${\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$. Then $R$ is an even negative-definite lattice. We assume that

- `\quad `{=latex}if $n=26$, the lattice $R$ cannot be embedded into the Leech lattice $\Lambda$.

For example, if $\mathord{\mathcal R}_R$ is non-empty, then $\mathord{\rm [SG2]}$ is satisfied, because $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\Lambda}=\emptyset$. In fact, in all examples that are treated in this paper, the assumption $\mathord{\rm [SG2]}$ is verified by showing $\mathord{\mathcal R}_R\ne \emptyset$.

We denote by $$x\mapsto x_S \quad\textrm{and}\quad x\mapsto x_R$$ the orthogonal projections from ${\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}$ to $S\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}$ and $R\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}$, respectively. Since ${\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$ is contained in $S\sp{\vee}\oplus R\sp{\vee}$, the images of ${\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$ by these projections are contained in $S\sp{\vee}$ and $R\sp{\vee}$, respectively. Since ${\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$ is unimodular, the result of Nikulin [@MR525944 Proposition 1.6.1] implies that the subgroup ${\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}/(S\oplus R)$ of $A_S\oplus A_R$ is the graph of an isomorphism $$\delta_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}} \colon (A_S, q_S)\mathbin{\,\raise -.6pt\rlap{$\to$}\raise 3.5pt \hbox{\hskip .3pt$\mathord{\sim}$}\;}(A_R, -q_R).$$ We assume that the subgroup $G$ of $\mathord{\mathrm {O}}^+(S)$ satisfies the following liftability condition (see Proposition `\ref{prop:liftg}`{=latex} below):

- \quad

  $\delta_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}*}(\eta_S(G))\subset \operatorname{\mathrm {Im}}\nolimits\eta_R$, `\;`{=latex}`\;`{=latex} [where $\delta_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}*}\colon \mathord{\mathrm {O}}(q_S)\mathbin{\,\raise -.6pt\rlap{$\to$}\raise 3.5pt \hbox{\hskip .3pt$\mathord{\sim}$}\;}\mathord{\mathrm {O}}(q_R)$ is induced by $\delta_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$]{.roman}.

For example, if $G$ is contained in $\eta_S\sp{-1}(\{\pm 1\})$, then $\mathord{\rm [SG3]}$ is satisfied.

Let $\mathord{\mathcal P}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$ be the positive cone of $\mathord{\mathbf{L}}$ that contains the fixed positive cone $\mathord{\mathcal P}_S$ of $S$. Let $r$ be an element of $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$. Then the hyperplane $(r)\sp{\perp}\in \mathord{\mathcal H}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$ of $\mathord{\mathcal P}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$ intersects $\mathord{\mathcal P}_S$ if and only if $r_S^2<0$ holds, and in this case, the hyperplane $(r_S)\sp{\perp}\in  \mathord{\mathcal H}_S$ of $\mathord{\mathcal P}_S$ is equal to the intersection $\mathord{\mathcal P}_S\cap (r)\sp{\perp}$. We put $$\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}:=\{\; {r_S} \; \mid \; {r\in \mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}},\;\; r_S^2<0} \;  \},$$ and show that the subset $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}$ of $\mathord{\mathcal N}_S\cap S\sp{\vee}$ has the properties $\mathord{\rm [V1]}$-$\mathord{\rm [V4]}$ given in Section `\ref{sec:chamber}`{=latex} with $L$ replaced by $S$.

::: proposition
**Proposition 36**. *If $v\in  \mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}$, then $-v^2\le 2$. In particular, $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}$ satisfies $\mathord{\rm [V1]}$.*
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* Since $R$ is negative-definite and $r_S^2+r_R^2=-2$ holds for any $r\in \mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$, we have $-2\le v^2$ for any $v=r_S\in \mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}$. ◻
:::

By Lemma `\ref{lem:locallyfinite}`{=latex}, the family of hyperplanes $$\begin{eqnarray*}
\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*&=&\{\; {(r_S)\sp{\perp}\in \mathord{\mathcal H}_S} \; \mid \; {r\in \mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}},\;\; r_S^2<0} \;  \} \\
&=&\{\; {\mathord{\mathcal P}_S\cap (r)\sp{\perp}} \; \mid \; {r\in \mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}},\;\; \mathord{\mathcal P}_S\cap (r)\sp{\perp}\ne \emptyset} \;  \}
\end{eqnarray*}$$ is locally finite in $\mathord{\mathcal P}_S$.

::: proposition
**Proposition 37**. *`\label{prop:liftg}`{=latex} Let $g$ be an element of $G$. Then there exists an element $\tilde {g}\in \mathord{\mathrm {O}}\sp+({\mathord{\mathbf{L}}})$ that leaves $S$ invariant and is equal to $g$ on $S$.*
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* By $\mathord{\rm [SG3]}$, there exists an element $h\in \mathord{\mathrm {O}}(R)$ such that $\delta_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}*}(\eta_S(g))=\eta_R(h)$. Then the action of $(\eta_S(g), \eta_R(h))$ on $A_S\oplus A_R$ preserves the graph ${\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}/(S\oplus R)$ of $\delta_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$, and hence the action of $(g, h)$ on $S\sp{\vee}\oplus R\sp{\vee}$ preserves ${\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}\subset S\sp{\vee}\oplus R\sp{\vee}$. The restriction $\tilde {g}\in \mathord{\mathrm {O}}({\mathord{\mathbf{L}}})$ of $(g, h)$ to ${\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$ belongs to $\mathord{\mathrm {O}}\sp+({\mathord{\mathbf{L}}})$, because $\tilde{g}$ leaves $\mathord{\mathcal P}_S\subset \mathord{\mathcal P}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$ invariant. Thus we obtain a desired lift $\tilde {g}\in \mathord{\mathrm {O}}\sp+({\mathord{\mathbf{L}}})$. ◻
:::

::: proposition
**Proposition 38**. *The action of $G$ on $\mathord{\mathcal N}_S\cap S\sp{\vee}$ leaves $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}$ invariant; that is, $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}$ satisfies $\mathord{\rm [V2]}$.*
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* For any $v\in \mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}$ and $g\in G$, we have $r\in \mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$ such that $v=r_S$ and a lift $\tilde {g}\in \mathord{\mathrm {O}}^+({\mathord{\mathbf{L}}})$ of $g$. Then $v^g=(r^{\tilde g})_S\in \mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}$ holds. ◻
:::

By the definition of $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}$, every $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chamber $D$ is written as $$D=\mathord{\mathcal D}\cap\mathord{\mathcal P}_S$$ by some $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}\sp*$-chamber $\mathord{\mathcal D}$.

::: definition
**Definition 39**. `\label{def:Snondeg}`{=latex} An $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}\sp*$-chamber $\mathord{\mathcal D}$ is said to be *$S$-nondegenerate* if $D=\mathord{\mathcal D}\cap\mathord{\mathcal P}_S$ is an $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chamber. In other words, $\mathord{\mathcal D}$ is $S$-nondegenerate if and only if $\mathord{\mathcal D}\cap\mathord{\mathcal P}_S$ contains a non-empty open subset of $\mathord{\mathcal P}_S$.
:::

::: definition
**Definition 40**. `\label{def:Sweylvector}`{=latex} Let $D$ be an $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chamber, and let $\mathord{\mathcal D}$ be an $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}\sp*$-chamber such that $D=\mathord{\mathcal D}\cap\mathord{\mathcal P}_S$. By certain abuse of terminology, we say that the Weyl vector $w\in {\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$ of $\mathord{\mathcal D}$ is a *Weyl vector of $D$*.
:::

::: remark
*Remark 41*. `\label{rem:DDDs}`{=latex} The set $\{ {r\in \mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}} \,|\, {\mathord{\mathcal P}_S\subset (r)\sp{\perp}}   \}$ is equal to $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{R}$. In particular, the number of hyperplanes $(r)\sp{\perp}\in \mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}\sp*$ passing through a point $v\in \mathord{\mathcal P}_S$ is at least $|\mathord{\mathcal R}_{R}|/2$, and is equal to $|\mathord{\mathcal R}_{R}|/2$ if $v$ is general. We remark two consequences of this fact.

Let $D$ be an $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chamber. Then there exists a canonical one-to-one correspondence between the set of $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}\sp*$-chambers $\mathord{\mathcal D}$ satisfying $D=\mathord{\mathcal D}\cap\mathord{\mathcal P}_S$ and the set of connected components of $$(R\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}) \setminus \bigcup_{\rho\in \mathord{\mathcal R}_R} [\rho]\sp{\perp},
\quad \textrm{where}\quad
[\rho]\sp{\perp}:=\{ {x\in R\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}} \,|\, {\langle{x, \rho}\rangle_{R}=0}   \}.$$ If $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}\sp*$-chambers $\mathord{\mathcal D}$ and $\mathord{\mathcal D}\sp\prime$ satisfy $D=\mathord{\mathcal D}\cap\mathord{\mathcal P}_S=\mathord{\mathcal D}\sp\prime\cap\mathord{\mathcal P}_S$, then there exists a sequence of reflections $s_i$ ($i=1, \dots, N$) of $\mathord{\mathbf{L}}$ with respect to $\rho_i\in \mathord{\mathcal R}_R\subset \mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$ such that their product $s_1\cdots s_N$ maps $\mathord{\mathcal D}$ to $\mathord{\mathcal D}\sp\prime$. Then the Weyl vector $w$ of $\mathord{\mathcal D}$ is mapped to the Weyl vector $w\sp\prime$ of $\mathord{\mathcal D}\sp\prime$ by $s_1\cdots s_N$. Since each $(-2)$-vector $\rho_i$ is contained in $R$, we have $w_S=w\sp\prime_S$. Therefore, for an $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chamber $D$, $w_S\in S\sp{\vee}$ is independent of the choice of a Weyl vector $w$ of $D$.

Let $\mathord{\mathcal D}$ be an $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}\sp*$-chamber, and let $v$ be a vector in $\mathord{\mathcal D}\cap \mathord{\mathcal P}_S$. If the number of walls of $\mathord{\mathcal D}$ passing though $v$ is equal to $|\mathord{\mathcal R}_{R}|/2$, then a small neighborhood of $v$ in $\mathord{\mathcal P}_S$ is contained in $\mathord{\mathcal D}\cap \mathord{\mathcal P}_S$, and hence $\mathord{\mathcal D}$ is $S$-nondegenerate, and $D=\mathord{\mathcal D}\cap \mathord{\mathcal P}_S$ contains $v$ in its interior.
:::

Next we consider the property $\mathord{\rm [V3]}$ for $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}$. Let $w$ be a Weyl vector of $\mathord{\mathbf{L}}$, and let $\mathord{\mathcal D}$ be the corresponding $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}\sp*$-chamber (not necessarily $S$-nondegenerate). Recall that the $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$-minimal defining set $\Delta_{\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}}(\mathord{\mathcal D})$ of $\mathord{\mathcal D}$ is equal to $\{ {r\in \mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}} \,|\, {\langle{w, r}\rangle_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}=1}   \}$. We put $$\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Deltasprime} 
\Delta_{w}:=\{\; {r\in \Delta_{\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}}(\mathord{\mathcal D})} \; \mid \; {r_S^2<0} \;  \}=\{\; {r\in \Delta_{\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}}(\mathord{\mathcal D})} \; \mid \; {(r)\sp{\perp}\cap\mathord{\mathcal P}_S\ne\emptyset} \;  \}
\end{equation}$$ and $$\begin{equation}
\mathord{\mathrm {pr}}_{S}(\Delta_w):=\{ {r_S} \,|\, {r\in\Delta_{w}}   \}.
\end{equation}$$ Then we have $\mathord{\mathcal D}\cap\mathord{\mathcal P}_S=\Sigma_{S} (\mathord{\mathrm {pr}}_S(\Delta_w))\cap \mathord{\mathcal P}_S$. Therefore, if $\mathord{\mathcal D}$ is $S$-nondegenerate, then $\mathord{\mathrm {pr}}_{S}(\Delta_w)$ is a defining set of the $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chamber $D=\mathord{\mathcal D}\cap\mathord{\mathcal P}_S$.

::: proposition
**Proposition 42**. *`\label{prop:proerty_c}`{=latex} For any Weyl vector $w$ in $\mathord{\mathbf{L}}$, the set $\Delta_{w}$ is finite. In particular, any $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chamber $D$ has a finite defining set, and hence $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}$ satisfies $\mathord{\rm [V3]}$.*
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* First we show $w_S^2>0$. Note that $w^2=w_S^2+w_R^2$ and $w_R^2\le 0$. Hence $w_S^2>0$ holds when $n=10$ or $n=18$ by Theorem `\ref{thm:Weyl}`{=latex}. Suppose that $n=26$ and $w_S^2=0$. Then $w_R=0$ and $w=w_S$ hold. Therefore $\langle {w}  \rangle\sp{\perp}/\langle {w}  \rangle$ would contain $R$, which contradicts Theorem `\ref{thm:ConwaySloane}`{=latex} and the assumption $\mathord{\rm [SG2]}$.

We denote by $d_R$ the order of the discriminant group $A_R$. Then $d_Rv^2\in \mathord{\mathbb Z}$ and $d_R^2v^2\in2\mathord{\mathbb Z}$ hold for any $v\in R\sp{\vee}$. We put $$\begin{equation}
\label{eq:nR}
n_R:=\{\; {c\in \mathord{\mathbb  Q}} \; \mid \; {d_Rc\in \mathord{\mathbb Z}, \;\;d_R^2c\in 2\mathord{\mathbb Z}, \;\; -2< c\le 0} \;  \},
\end{equation}$$ which is obviously finite. Since $R$ is negative-definite, the set $$\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Rdualc}
R\sp{\vee}[c]:=\{\; {v\in R\sp{\vee}} \; \mid \; {v^2=c} \;  \}
\end{equation}$$ is finite for each $c\in n_R$. In particular, the set $$\begin{equation}
\label{eq:aRc}
a_R[c]:=\{\; {\langle{w_R, v}\rangle_{R}} \; \mid \; {v\in R\sp{\vee}[c]} \;  \}
\end{equation}$$ is finite for each $c\in n_R$. Suppose that $r\in\Delta_w$, so that $r_S^2<0$. Note that $$r^2=r_S^2+r_R^2=-2\quad\textrm{and}\quad\langle{w, r}\rangle_{{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}}=\langle{w_S, r_S}\rangle_{S}+\langle{w_R, r_R}\rangle_{R}=1.$$ Since $r_R^2\le 0$, we have $-2\le r_S^2<0$ and $-2<r_R^2\le 0$. Hence $n\sp\prime:=r_R^2$ belongs to $n_R$, and $r_R$ is an element of $R\sp{\vee}[n\sp\prime]$. Since $w_S^2>0$, the quadratic part of the inhomogeneous quadratic form $x\mapsto x^2$ on the affine hyperplane $$\{\; {x\in S\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}} \; \mid \; {\langle{w_S, x}\rangle_{S}=b} \;  \}$$ of $S\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}$ is negative-definite for any $b\in \mathord{\mathbb R}$. If we put $a\sp\prime:=\langle{w_R, r_R}\rangle_{R} \in a_R[n\sp\prime]$, then $r_S$ belongs to the finite set $$\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Sdualna}
S\sp{\vee}[n\sp\prime, a\sp\prime]:=\{\; {v\in S\sp{\vee}} \; \mid \; {\langle{w_S, v}\rangle_{S}=1-a\sp\prime,\;\; v^2=-2-n\sp\prime} \;  \}.
\end{equation}$$ Since $n_R$, $R\sp{\vee}[n\sp\prime]$, $a_R[n\sp\prime]$ and $S\sp{\vee}[n\sp\prime, a\sp\prime]$ are finite, $\Delta_{w}$ is finite. ◻
:::

We state the above proof in the form of an algorithm.

::: algorithm
**Algorithm 43**. `\label{algorithm:Deltaw}`{=latex} Suppose that a Weyl vector $w\in \mathord{\mathbf{L}}$ is given. This algorithm calculates the set $\Delta_{w}$ defined by `\eqref{eq:Deltasprime}`{=latex}.

*Step 0*. We calculate $w_S\in S\sp{\vee}$, $w_R\in R\sp{\vee}$, and the set $n_R$ defined by `\eqref{eq:nR}`{=latex}. We set $\Delta\sp\prime:=\{\}$.

*Step 1*. For each $c\in n_R$, we calculate $R\sp{\vee}[c]$ defined by `\eqref{eq:Rdualc}`{=latex} by Algorithm `\ref{algo:QLc}`{=latex}, and calculate $a_R[c]$ defined by `\eqref{eq:aRc}`{=latex}.

*Step 2*. Using Algorithm `\ref{algo:QLcab}`{=latex}, we calculate the finite set $S\sp{\vee}[n\sp\prime, a\sp\prime]$ defined by `\eqref{eq:Sdualna}`{=latex} for each pair of $n\sp\prime\in n_R$ and $a\sp\prime\in a_R[n\sp\prime]$.

*Step 3*. For each triple $n\sp\prime\in n_R$, $v_R\in R\sp{\vee}[n\sp\prime]$ and $v_S\in S\sp{\vee}[n\sp\prime, a\sp\prime]$, where $a\sp\prime=\langle{w_R, v_R}\rangle_{R}$, we determine whether $v_S+v_R \in S\sp{\vee}\oplus R\sp{\vee}$ belongs to ${\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$ or not, and if $v_S+v_R\in {\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$, then we append $r:=v_S+v_R$ to $\Delta\sp\prime$.

*Step 4*. Output $\Delta\sp\prime$ as $\Delta_w$.
:::

We give a criterion of the $S$-nondegeneracy of a given $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}\sp*$-chamber.

::: criterion
**Criterion 44**. `\label{criterion:Snondeg}`{=latex} Let $\mathord{\mathcal D}$ be an $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}\sp*$-chamber with the Weyl vector $w$. Then $\mathord{\mathcal D}$ is $S$-nondegenerate if and only if there exists a vector $v\in \mathord{\mathcal P}_S$ that satisfies the finite number of strict inequalities $$\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ineqSnondeg}
\langle{v, r_S}\rangle_{S}>0\quad\textrm{for any $r\in \Delta_w$}.
\end{equation}$$ If $v\in \mathord{\mathcal P}_S$ satisfies these inequalities, then the $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chamber $\mathord{\mathcal D}\cap\mathord{\mathcal P}_S$ contains $v$ in its interior.
:::

Next we consider the property $\mathord{\rm [V4]}$ for $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}$.

::: proposition
**Proposition 45**. *`\label{prop:proerty_d}`{=latex} For any $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chamber $D$, we have $\pi_S(\overline{D})\cap\partial\,\overline{\mathord{\mathbb H}}_S\subset \partial\,\overline{\mathord{\mathbb H}}^{\,\mathord{\mathbb  Q}}_S$. In particular, $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}$ satisfies $\mathord{\rm [V4]}$.*
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* Let $\mathord{\mathcal D}$ be an $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}\sp*$-chamber such that $D=\mathord{\mathcal D}\cap\mathord{\mathcal P}_S$. Then we have $\overline{D}=\overline{\mathord{\mathcal D}}\cap\overline{\mathord{\mathcal P}}_S$. Since $\partial\,\overline{\mathord{\mathbb H}}^{\,\mathord{\mathbb  Q}}_S=\partial\,\overline{\mathord{\mathbb H}}_S\cap\partial\,\overline{\mathord{\mathbb H}}^{\,\mathord{\mathbb  Q}}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$ under the canonical inclusion $\overline{\mathord{\mathbb H}}_S\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathord{\mathbb H}}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$, the assertion follows from Proposition `\ref{prop:bdrQ}`{=latex}. ◻
:::

Thus the subset $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}$ of $\mathord{\mathcal N}_S\cap S\sp{\vee}$ has the properties $\mathord{\rm [V1]}$-$\mathord{\rm [V4]}$. Hence we can apply Algorithms `\ref{algorithm:mindefset}`{=latex}, `\ref{algorithm:autG}`{=latex} and `\ref{algorithm:Gequiv}`{=latex} described in Section `\ref{sec:chamber}`{=latex} to $\mathord{\mathcal V}=\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}$.

::: algorithm
**Algorithm 46**. `\label{algorithm:aSnS}`{=latex} Suppose that a Weyl vector $w\in \mathord{\mathbf{L}}$ of an $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chamber $D$ is given. This algorithm calculates the primitively minimal defining set $\Delta_{S\sp{\vee}}(D)$ of $D$.

*Step 0*. We calculate $\Delta_w$ by Algorithm `\ref{algorithm:Deltaw}`{=latex}.

*Step 1*. We calculate the defining set $\mathord{\mathrm {pr}}_S(\Delta_{w})$ of $D$.

*Step 2*. We then calculate $\Delta_{S\sp{\vee}}(D)$ from $\mathord{\mathrm {pr}}_S(\Delta_{w})$ by Algorithm `\ref{algorithm:mindefset}`{=latex}.
:::

::: remark
*Remark 47*. `\label{rem:prSDelta}`{=latex} Let $D$ and $D\sp\prime$ be $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chambers. By Algorithms `\ref{algorithm:autG}`{=latex}, `\ref{algorithm:Gequiv}`{=latex} and `\ref{algorithm:aSnS}`{=latex}, we can calculate $\operatorname{\mathit {Aut}}\nolimits_G(D)$ and determine whether $D$ and $D\sp\prime$ are $G$-congruent or not from Weyl vectors $w$ of $D$ and $w\sp\prime$ of $D\sp\prime$. Note that, by Remark `\ref{rem:DDDs}`{=latex}, the defining set $\mathord{\mathrm {pr}}_S(\Delta_w)$ of $D$ is independent of the choice of the Weyl vector $w$. Moreover, by Proposition `\ref{prop:liftg}`{=latex}, if $g\in \operatorname{\mathit {Aut}}\nolimits_G(D)$, then $g$ preserves $\mathord{\mathrm {pr}}_S(\Delta_w)$, and if $D\sp\prime=D^g$, then $g$ maps $\mathord{\mathrm {pr}}_S(\Delta_w)$ to $\mathord{\mathrm {pr}}_S(\Delta_{w\sp\prime})$ bijectively. Hence, when we apply Algorithms `\ref{algorithm:autG}`{=latex} and `\ref{algorithm:Gequiv}`{=latex} to $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chambers, we can use $\mathord{\mathrm {pr}}_S(\Delta_w)$ and $\mathord{\mathrm {pr}}_S(\Delta_{w\sp\prime})$ in the place of the primitively defining sets $\Delta_{S\sp{\vee}}(D)$ and $\Delta_{S\sp{\vee}}(D\sp\prime)$.
:::

Let $w$ be a Weyl vector of an $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chamber $D$, and let $v$ be an element of $\Delta_{S\sp{\vee}}(D)$. Then $(v)\sp{\perp}$ is a wall of $D$. We calculate the $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chamber that is adjacent to $D$ across $(v)\sp{\perp}$. First we prepare an auxiliary algorithm.

::: algorithm
**Algorithm 48**. `\label{algorithm:passing}`{=latex} Suppose that $v\in \mathord{\mathcal N}_S\cap S\sp{\vee}$ is given. We regard the hyperplane $(v)\sp{\perp}$ of $\mathord{\mathcal P}_S$ as a linear subspace of the larger space $\mathord{\mathcal P}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$; that is, $(v)\sp{\perp}$ is of codimension $\operatorname{\mathrm {rank}}\nolimits R+1$ in $\mathord{\mathcal P}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$, whereas $(r)\sp{\perp}$ for $r\in \mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$ is of codimension $1$ in $\mathord{\mathcal P}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$. This algorithm calculates the set $$\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Pv}
P_v:=\{\; {r\in \mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}} \; \mid \; {(v)\sp{\perp}\subset (r)\sp{\perp}} \;  \}=\{\; {r\in \mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}} \; \mid \; {r_S\in \mathord{\mathbb R}v} \;  \}.
\end{equation}$$ We set $P:=\{\}$. There exist only a finite number of $\alpha\in \mathord{\mathbb  Q}$ such that $\alpha v \in S\sp{\vee}$ and $\alpha^2 v^2\ge -2$. For each such rational number $\alpha$ and each $u\in R\sp{\vee}[c]$, where $c=-2-\alpha^2 v^2$ and $R\sp{\vee}[c]$ defined by `\eqref{eq:Rdualc}`{=latex}, we determine whether $\alpha v+u\in S\sp{\vee}\oplus R\sp{\vee}$ belongs to ${\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$ or not, and if $\alpha v+u\in {\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$, then we append $r:=\alpha v+u\in {\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$ to $P$. Then we output $P$ as $P_v$. (Remark that $P_v$ includes the subset $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{R}$ of $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$.)
:::

We consider the linear subspace $$V:=\mathord{\mathbb R}v\oplus (R\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R})$$ of $\mathord{\mathbf{L}}\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}$. Let $\langle{\phantom{|}, \phantom{|}}\rangle_{V}\colon V\times V\to \mathord{\mathbb R}$ denote the restriction of $\langle{\phantom{|}, \phantom{|}}\rangle_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$ to $V$. Note that $\langle{\phantom{|}, \phantom{|}}\rangle_{V}$ is negative-definite. Let $x\mapsto x_V$ denote the orthogonal projection from ${\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}$ to $V$. Each element $r$ of $P_v$ defined by `\eqref{eq:Pv}`{=latex} belongs to $V$. Hence $r_V=r$ holds for any $r\in P_v$. We denote by $\{\mathord{\mathcal D}_0, \dots, \mathord{\mathcal D}_m\}$ the set of $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}\sp*$-chambers containing the hyperplane $(v)\sp{\perp}$ of $\mathord{\mathcal P}_S$ (that is, the linear subspace of $\mathord{\mathcal P}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$ with codimension $\operatorname{\mathrm {rank}}\nolimits R+1$), and put $$\mathord{\mathcal D}\sp{\mathord{\circ}}_{j, V}:=
\{\; {x\in V} \; \mid \; {\langle{x, r}\rangle_{V}>0 \;\;\textrm{for any}\;\; r \in P_v\cap \Delta_{\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}}(\mathord{\mathcal D}_j)} \;  \}.$$ Then $\mathord{\mathcal D}_j\mapsto \mathord{\mathcal D}\sp{\mathord{\circ}}_{j, V}$ gives a one-to-one correspondence from $\{\mathord{\mathcal D}_0, \dots, \mathord{\mathcal D}_m\}$ to the set of connected components of $$V\;\setminus\; \bigcup_{r \in P_v}[r]\sp{\perp}_V, 
\;\;\;\;\textrm{where}\;\;\;\;
[r]_V\sp{\perp}:=\{\; {x\in V} \; \mid \; {\langle{x, r}\rangle_{V}=0} \;  \}.$$ Let $w_j$ be the Weyl vector of $\mathord{\mathcal D}_j$. By renumbering $\mathord{\mathcal D}_0, \dots, \mathord{\mathcal D}_m$, we can assume that $D=\mathord{\mathcal P}_S\cap \mathord{\mathcal D}_0$ and that $w_0$ is the given Weyl vector $w$ of $D$. Since $\langle{w_{j,V}, r}\rangle_{V}=\langle{w_{j}, r}\rangle_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}=1$ for any $r\in P_v\cap\Delta_{\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}}(\mathord{\mathcal D}_j)$, the vector $w_{j, V}$ belongs to $\mathord{\mathcal D}\sp{\mathord{\circ}}_{j, V}$. There exists an $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}\sp*$-chamber $\mathord{\mathcal D}_{\mathord{{\rm opp}}}$ among $\{\mathord{\mathcal D}_0, \dots, \mathord{\mathcal D}_m\}$ such that $$\mathord{\mathcal D}\sp{\mathord{\circ}}_{\mathord{{\rm opp}}, V}=-\mathord{\mathcal D}\sp{\mathord{\circ}}_{0, V}.$$ Then $\mathord{\mathcal P}_S\cap \mathord{\mathcal D}_{\mathord{{\rm opp}}}$ is the $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chamber $D\sp\prime$ adjacent to $D$ across $(v)\sp{\perp}$. We calculate the Weyl vector of $\mathord{\mathcal D}_{\mathord{{\rm opp}}}$. Let $u$ be a sufficiently general vector of ${\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}\otimes\mathord{\mathbb  Q}$, and let $\varepsilon$ be a sufficiently small positive real number. Consider the oriented line segment $$p(t):=(1-t) w_V+t(-w_V+\varepsilon u_V)\qquad (0\le t\le 1)$$ in $V$ from $w_{V}=w_{0, V}\in \mathord{\mathcal D}\sp{\mathord{\circ}}_{0, V}$ to $-w_V+\varepsilon u_V\in \mathord{\mathcal D}\sp{\mathord{\circ}}_{\mathord{{\rm opp}}, V}$. Let $P\sp\prime_v=\{r_1, \dots, r_N\}\subset P_v$ be a complete set of representatives of $P_v/\{\pm 1\}$. For $i=1, \dots, N$, let $t_i$ be the value of $t$ such that $p(t_i) \in [r_i]_V\sp{\perp}$. Since $\langle{x, r_i}\rangle_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}=\langle{x_V, r_i}\rangle_{V}$ for any $x\in {\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}$, we have $$t_i=\left(2-\varepsilon \frac{\langle{u, r_i}\rangle_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}}{\langle{w, r_i}\rangle_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}}\right)\sp{-1}.$$ Since $u$ is general, we can assume that $t_1, \dots, t_N$ are distinct. We put the numbering of the elements $r_1, \dots, r_N$ of $P_v\sp\prime$ so that $t_1<\dots<t_N$ holds. Let $s_i\in \mathord{\mathrm {O}}^+({\mathord{\mathbf{L}}})$ denote the reflection with respect to $r_i$. Then $\mathord{\mathcal D}_0$ and $\mathord{\mathcal D}_{\mathord{{\rm opp}}}$ are related by $$\mathord{\mathcal D}_{\mathord{{\rm opp}}}=\mathord{\mathcal D}_{0}^{s_1 s_2\dots s_N}.$$ Therefore $w^{s_1 s_2\dots s_N}$ is the Weyl vector of $\mathord{\mathcal D}_{\mathord{{\rm opp}}}$. By this consideration, we obtain the following:

::: algorithm
**Algorithm 49**. `\label{algorithm:adjacent}`{=latex} Suppose that a Weyl vector $w\in\mathord{\mathbf{L}}$ of an $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chamber $D$ and an element $v$ of $\Delta_{S\sp{\vee}}(D)$ are given. This algorithm calculates a Weyl vector $w\sp\prime$ of the $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chamber $D\sp\prime$ adjacent to $D$ across the wall $(v)\sp{\perp}$.

We calculate the set $P_v$ by Algorithm `\ref{algorithm:passing}`{=latex}, and choose a complete set of representatives $P\sp\prime_v=\{r_1, \dots, r_N\}$ of $P_v/\{\pm 1\}$. We also choose a vector $u$ of ${\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}\otimes\mathord{\mathbb  Q}$ such that $i\ne j$ implies ${\langle{u, r_i}\rangle_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}}/{\langle{w, r_i}\rangle_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}} \ne  {\langle{u, r_j}\rangle_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}}/{\langle{w, r_j}\rangle_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}}$. We sort the elements $r_i$ of $P\sp\prime_v$ so that $$i<j\;\;\Longrightarrow\;\;  
\frac{\langle{u, r_i}\rangle_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}}{\langle{w, r_i}\rangle_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}} <  \frac{\langle{u, r_j}\rangle_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}}{\langle{w, r_j}\rangle_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}}$$ holds. Then $w^{s_1 s_2\dots s_N}$ is a Weyl vector of $D\sp\prime$, where $s_i\in \mathord{\mathrm {O}}^+(\mathord{\mathbf{L}})$ is the reflection with respect to $r_i$.
:::

# The main algorithm {#sec:main}

We present our main algorithm, and prove its termination and correctness. Let $G$, $S$ and $\mathord{\mathbf{L}}$ be as in the previous section. Let $N$ be an $\mathord{\mathcal R}_S\sp*$-chamber. Since $\mathord{\mathcal R}_S$ is contained in $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}$, the $\mathord{\mathcal R}_S\sp*$-chamber $N$ is a union of $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chambers. We fix an $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chamber $D_0$ contained in $N$. An *$N$-chain* is a finite sequence $$D\sp{(0)}, D\sp{(1)}, \dots, D\sp{(l)}$$ of $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chambers contained in $N$ such that $D\sp{(a+1)}$ is adjacent to $D\sp{(a)}$ for each $a$. The *length* of an $N$-chain $D\sp{(0)}, \dots, D\sp{(l)}$ is defined to be $l$. Let $D$ be an $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chamber contained in $N$. Since $N$ is connected, there exists an $N$-chain $D\sp{(0)},  \dots, D\sp{(l)}$ such that $D\sp{(0)}=D_0$ and $D\sp{(l)}=D$. The *level of $D$* is defined to be the minimum of the lengths of all $N$-chains from $D_0$ to $D$.

In the actual execution of the following algorithm, we use a Weyl vector to store an $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chamber in the computer memory. Thus, for example, the set $\mathord{\mathbb D}$ is realized as a set of vectors of $\mathord{\mathbf{L}}$ in the computer.

::: algorithm
**Algorithm 50**. `\label{algo:main}`{=latex} Suppose that a Weyl vector $w_0$ of an $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chamber $D_0$ is given. Let $N$ be the $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{S}\sp*$-chamber containing $D_0$. This algorithm calculates a finite set $\varGamma$ of generators of $\operatorname{\mathit {Aut}}\nolimits_G(N)$, a finite set $\mathord{\mathbb D}$ of $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chambers contained in $N$ such that the union $$\begin{equation}
\label{eq:FN}
F_{N}:=\bigcup_{D_i\in \mathord{\mathbb D}} D_i
\end{equation}$$ satisfies the property (2) in Proposition `\ref{prop:adj}`{=latex} below, and a set $\mathord{\mathcal B}$ of $(-2)$-vectors of $S$ that satisfies the property (3) in Proposition `\ref{prop:adj}`{=latex}. In fact, the set $\mathord{\mathbb D}$ is a complete set of representatives of $G$-congruence classes of $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chambers, and it is a union of non-empty subsets $\mathord{\mathbb D}_{\ell}$, where elements of $\mathord{\mathbb D}_{\ell}$ are of level $\ell$.

\smallskip

*Step 0*. Set $\varGamma$ to be $\{\}$, $\mathord{\mathbb D}_0$ to be $\{D_0\}$, and $\mathord{\mathcal B}$ to be $\{\}$.

*Step 1*. Calculate the set $\Delta_{S\sp{\vee}}(D_0)$ from $w_0$ by Algorithm `\ref{algorithm:aSnS}`{=latex}.

*Step 2*. Execute $\mathord{\mathtt{adj}}(0)$, where $\mathord{\mathtt{adj}}(\ell)$ is the following procedure, which calls $\mathord{\mathtt{adj}}(\ell+1)$ at the last step if the condition for termination is not fulfilled.

\smallskip 

*The procedure $\mathord{\mathtt{adj}}(\ell)$.* Suppose that, for each $\lambda=0, \dots, \ell$, a non-empty finite set $\mathord{\mathbb D}_{\lambda}$ of $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chambers with the following properties has been calculated.

- Each $D\in \mathord{\mathbb D}_{\lambda}$ is contained in $N$, and is of level $\lambda$, and

- if $D, D\sp\prime\in \cup_{\lambda=0}^{\ell}\mathord{\mathbb D}_{\lambda}$ are distinct, then $D$ and $D\sp\prime$ are not $G$-congruent.

Suppose also that, for each $D \in \cup_{\lambda=0}^{\ell} \mathord{\mathbb D}_{\lambda}$, we have calculated $\Delta_{S\sp{\vee}} (D)$. This procedure calculates $\mathord{\mathbb D}_{\ell+1}$ and $\Delta_{S\sp{\vee}} (D)$ for each $D\in \mathord{\mathbb D}_{\ell+1}$.

Let $D_{k+1}, \dots, D_{k+m}$ be the elements of $\mathord{\mathbb D}_{\ell}$, where $k$ is the sum of the cardinalities of $\mathord{\mathbb D}_{\lambda}$ with $\lambda<\ell$ (we put $k=-1$ if $\ell=0$), and $m$ is the cardinality of $\mathord{\mathbb D}_{\ell}$.

- Put $\mathord{\mathbb D}\sp\prime:=\{\}$ and set $l:=k+m+1$.

- For each $D_{i}\in \mathord{\mathbb D}_{\ell}$, we make the following calculation.

  - Calculate the finite group $\operatorname{\mathit {Aut}}\nolimits_G(D_{i})$ from $\Delta_{S\sp{\vee}}(D_{i})$ by Algorithm `\ref{algorithm:autG}`{=latex}, and append a finite set of generators of $\operatorname{\mathit {Aut}}\nolimits_G(D_{i})$ to $\varGamma$.

  - Note that $\operatorname{\mathit {Aut}}\nolimits_G(D_{i})$ acts on $\Delta_{S\sp{\vee}}(D_{i})$. Decompose $\Delta_{S\sp{\vee}}(D_{i})$ into the $\operatorname{\mathit {Aut}}\nolimits_G(D_{i})$-orbits $o_1, \dots, o_t$. Since $\mathord{\mathcal R}_S\sp*$ is $G$-invariant, the set $o_{\nu}\sp*:=\{ {(v)\sp{\perp}} \,|\, {v\in o_{\nu}}   \}$ is either disjoint from $\mathord{\mathcal R}_S\sp*$ or entirely contained in $\mathord{\mathcal R}_S\sp*$. Let $v$ be an element of $o_{\nu}$. Since $v$ is primitive in $S\sp{\vee}$, we have $o_{\nu}\sp*\subset \mathord{\mathcal R}_S\sp*$ if and only if there exists a positive integer $\alpha$ such that $\alpha^2 v^2=-2$ and $\alpha v\in S$.

  - For each orbit $o_{\nu}$ such that $o_{\nu}\sp*$ is disjoint from $\mathord{\mathcal R}_S\sp*$, we make the following calculation.

    - Choose a vector $v \in o_{\nu}$, and calculate a Weyl vector $w\sp\prime\in {\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$ of the $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chamber $D\sp\prime$ adjacent to $D_{i}$ across the wall $(v)\sp{\perp}$ by Algorithm `\ref{algorithm:adjacent}`{=latex}. We then calculate $\Delta_{S\sp{\vee}}(D\sp\prime)$ by Algorithm `\ref{algorithm:aSnS}`{=latex}. Since $(v)\sp{\perp}\notin \mathord{\mathcal R}_S\sp*$ and $D_i\subset N$, we have $D\sp\prime\subset N$. Since $D_i$ is of level $\ell$, we see that $D\sp\prime$ is of level $\le \ell+1$.

    - By Algorithm `\ref{algorithm:Gequiv}`{=latex}, determine whether $D\sp\prime$ is $G$-congruent to an $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chamber $D\sp{\prime\prime}$ in $$\tilde{\mathord{\mathbb D}}:=\mathord{\mathbb D}_0\cup\dots\cup \mathord{\mathbb D}_{\ell}\cup \mathord{\mathbb D}\sp\prime=\{D_0, D_1, \dots, D_{l-1}\}$$ or not. If $D\sp{\prime}=D\sp{\prime\prime \, h}$ for some $D\sp{\prime\prime}\in \tilde{\mathord{\mathbb D}}$ and $h\in G$, then append $h$ to $\varGamma$. If there exist no such $D\sp{\prime\prime}\in \tilde{\mathord{\mathbb D}}$ and $h\in G$, then $D\sp\prime$ represents a new $G$-congruence class and its level is $\ell+1$, and hence we put $D_{l}:=D\sp\prime$, append $D_{l}$ to $\mathord{\mathbb D}\sp\prime$ and increment $l$ by $1$.

  - For each orbit $o_{\nu}$ such that $o_{\nu}\sp*\subset \mathord{\mathcal R}_S\sp*$, choose a vector $v\in o_{\nu}$, find a positive integer $\alpha$ such that $r:=\alpha v \in \mathord{\mathcal R}_{S}$, and append $r$ to $\mathord{\mathcal B}$.

- If $\mathord{\mathbb D}\sp\prime\ne \emptyset$, then put $\mathord{\mathbb D}_{\ell+1}:=\mathord{\mathbb D}\sp\prime$, which has the properties \[D1\] and \[D2\] above, and execute $\mathord{\mathtt{adj}}(\ell+1)$. If $\mathord{\mathbb D}\sp\prime= \emptyset$, then put $$\mathord{\mathbb D}:=\mathord{\mathbb D}_0\cup \mathord{\mathbb D}_1\cup\cdots\cup  \mathord{\mathbb D}_{\ell},$$ and terminate.
:::

::: proposition
**Proposition 51**. *`\label{prop:terminates}`{=latex} Algorithm `\ref{algo:main}`{=latex} terminates.*
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* By construction, any two distinct $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chambers in $\mathord{\mathbb D}_0\cup\dots\cup \mathord{\mathbb D}_{\ell} \cup \mathord{\mathbb D}\sp\prime$ are not $G$-congruent during the calculation. Thus Proposition `\ref{prop:terminates}`{=latex} follows from Theorem `\ref{thm:finiteGequiv}`{=latex}. ◻
:::

::: proposition
**Proposition 52**. *`\label{prop:adj}`{=latex} (1) The group $\operatorname{\mathit {Aut}}\nolimits_G(N)$ is generated by $\varGamma$.*

*(2) For any $v\in N$, there exists an element $g\in \operatorname{\mathit {Aut}}\nolimits_G(N)$ such that $v^g\in F_{N}$, where $F_{N}$ is defined by `\eqref{eq:FN}`{=latex}.*

*(3) Let $r$ be an element of the $\mathord{\mathcal R}_S$-minimal defining set $\Delta_{\mathord{\mathcal R}_S}(N)$ of $N$. Then there exists an element $g\in \operatorname{\mathit {Aut}}\nolimits_G(N)$ such that $r^g\in \mathord{\mathcal B}$.*
:::

:::: proof
*Proof.* Since each $D_i\in \mathord{\mathbb D}$ is contained in $N$, we have $\operatorname{\mathit {Aut}}\nolimits_G(D_i)\subset \operatorname{\mathit {Aut}}\nolimits_G(N)$ by `\eqref{eq:intpt}`{=latex}, and hence all elements of $\varGamma$ appended in Step 2-1 is an element of $\operatorname{\mathit {Aut}}\nolimits_G(N)$. If $h\in G$ is appended to $\varGamma$ in Step 2-3-2, then we have $D\sp{\prime}=D\sp{\prime\prime \, h}$ for some $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chambers $D\sp\prime$ and $D\sp{\prime\prime}$ contained in $N$, and hence $h\in \operatorname{\mathit {Aut}}\nolimits_G(N)$ by `\eqref{eq:intpt}`{=latex}. Therefore the subgroup $\langle {\varGamma}  \rangle$ of $G$ generated by $\varGamma$ is contained in $\operatorname{\mathit {Aut}}\nolimits_G(N)$.

To prove the rest of Proposition `\ref{prop:adj}`{=latex}, it is enough to show the following. This claim also proves that $\mathord{\mathbb D}$ is a complete set of representatives of $G$-congruence classes of $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chambers contained in $N$.

::: claim
**Claim 53**. `\label{claim}`{=latex} For an arbitrary $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chamber $D$ contained in $N$, there exists an element $\gamma\in \langle {\varGamma}  \rangle$ such that $D^\gamma\in \mathord{\mathbb D}$.
:::

Indeed, suppose that Claim `\ref{claim}`{=latex} is proved. Let $g$ be an arbitrary element of $\operatorname{\mathit {Aut}}\nolimits_G(N)$. Since $D_0^g\subset N$, there exists an element $\gamma\in \langle {\varGamma}  \rangle$ such that $(D_0^g)^\gamma$ is equal to some $D_i \in \mathord{\mathbb D}$. Since $D_i$ and $D_0$ are $G$-congruent and $D_i, D_0\in \mathord{\mathbb D}$, we have $D_0=D_i$. Therefore $g\gamma \in \operatorname{\mathit {Aut}}\nolimits_G(D_0)\subset \langle {\varGamma}  \rangle$ follows, and hence we have $g\in \langle {\varGamma}  \rangle$. Let $v\in N$ be an arbitrary vector, and let $D$ be an $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chamber containing $v$ and contained in $N$. By Claim `\ref{claim}`{=latex}, we have $\gamma\in \langle {\varGamma}  \rangle$ such that $D^\gamma=D_i \in \mathord{\mathbb D}$. Then we have $v^\gamma\in F_{N}$. Suppose that $r\in \Delta_{\mathord{\mathcal R}_S}(N)$. Then there exist an $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chamber $D$ contained in $N$ and a vector $v\in \Delta_{S\sp{\vee}}(D)$ such that $r=\alpha v$ for some positive integer $\alpha$. By Claim `\ref{claim}`{=latex}, we have $\gamma\in \langle {\varGamma}  \rangle$ such that $D^\gamma=D_i \in \mathord{\mathbb D}$. Then $v^\gamma \in \Delta_{S\sp{\vee}}(D_i)$ is contained in an $\operatorname{\mathit {Aut}}\nolimits_{G}(D_i)$-orbit $o_{\nu}$ such that $o_{\nu}\sp*\subset\mathord{\mathcal R}_{S}\sp*$. Hence there exists an element $\gamma\sp\prime\in \operatorname{\mathit {Aut}}\nolimits_{G}(D_i)$ such that $r^{\gamma \gamma\sp\prime}$ is appended to $\mathord{\mathcal B}$ in Step 2-3-3.

Now we prove Claim `\ref{claim}`{=latex}. We fix an $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chamber $D$ contained in $N$, and prove the existence of $\gamma\in \langle {\varGamma}  \rangle$ such that $D^\gamma\in \mathord{\mathbb D}$. An $N$-chain $D\sp{(0)}, D\sp{(1)}, \dots, D\sp{(l)}$ of $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chambers is said to be *$D$-admissible* if $D\sp{(0)}$ belongs to $\mathord{\mathbb D}$ and there exists an element $\gamma\in \langle {\varGamma}  \rangle$ such that $D\sp{(l)}=D^{\gamma}$. Since $N$ is connected, there exists at least one $D$-admissible $N$-chain. It is enough to show that there exists a $D$-admissible $N$-chain of length $0$. We suppose that the $D$-admissible $N$-chain with *minimal* length $$D\sp{(0)}=D_i, \; D\sp{(1)}, \; \dots, \; D\sp{(l)}=D^{\gamma}\qquad(D_i\in \mathord{\mathbb D}, \; \gamma\in \langle {\varGamma}  \rangle)$$ is of length $l>0$, and derive a contradiction. Let $v\sp\prime\in \Delta_{S\sp{\vee}}(D_i)$ be the vector such that $(v\sp\prime)\sp{\perp}$ is the wall between $D\sp{(0)}=D_i$ and $D\sp{(1)}$. Since $D_i$ and $D\sp{(1)}$ are contained in $N$, the $\operatorname{\mathit {Aut}}\nolimits_G(D_i)$-orbit $o_{\nu}\subset \Delta_{S\sp{\vee}}(D_i)$ containing $v\sp\prime$ satisfies $o_{\nu}\sp*\cap \mathord{\mathcal R}_{S}\sp*=\emptyset$. Let $v$ be the vector of $o_{\nu}$ chosen in Step 2-3-1, and let $g\in \operatorname{\mathit {Aut}}\nolimits_G(D_i)$ be an element that maps $v\sp\prime$ to $v$. Then $D^{(1)g}$ is the $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chamber adjacent to $D\sp{(0)}=D_i$ across $(v)\sp{\perp}$. Since $g\in \langle {\varGamma}  \rangle$, the $N$-chain $$D\sp{(0)}=D_i, \, D^{(1)g}, \, \dots, \, D^{(l)g}=D^{\gamma g}$$ is $D$-admissible. By the minimality of $l$, $D^{(1) g}$ does not belong to $\mathord{\mathbb D}$. Hence, in Step 2-3-2, the $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chamber $D^{(1) g}$ is not appended to $\mathord{\mathbb D}\sp\prime$, which means that there exist a chamber $D\sp{\prime\prime}\in\mathord{\mathbb D}$ and and an element $h\in G$ such that $D\sp{(1) g h}=D\sp{\prime\prime}$. The element $h\sp{-1}$ is appended to $\varGamma$ in Step 2-3-2, and hence $\gamma  g h\in \langle {\varGamma}  \rangle$. Then $$D^{(1) g h}=D\sp{\prime\prime}, \, D^{(2) g h}, \, \dots, D^{(l) g h}=D^{\gamma g h}$$ is a $D$-admissible $N$-chain of length $l-1$, which is a contradiction. ◻

# The automorphism group of a $K3$ surface {#sec:AutX}

In this section, we review the classical theory of the automorphism groups of $K3$ surfaces. Let $X$ be an algebraic $K3$ surface with the Néron-Severi lattice $S_X$ of rank $>1$. Then $S_X$ is an even hyperbolic lattice. Let $\mathord{\mathcal P}(X)$ denote the positive cone of $S_X$ containing an ample class. The nef cone $\mathord{\mathrm{Nef}}(X)$ of $X$ is defined by $$\mathord{\mathrm{Nef}}(X):=\{\; {v\in S_X\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}} \; \mid \; {\langle{v, [C]}\rangle_{}\ge 0\;\;\textrm{for any curve $C$ on $X$}} \;  \},$$ where $[C]\in S_X$ is the class of a curve $C$. Note that, by Kleiman's criterion, $\mathord{\mathrm{Nef}}(X)$ is contained in the closure $\overline{\mathord{\mathcal P}}(X)$ of $\mathord{\mathcal P}(X)$ in $S_X\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}$. We put $$N(X):=\mathord{\mathrm{Nef}}(X)\cap \mathord{\mathcal P}(X)=\mathord{\mathrm{Nef}}(X)\setminus (\mathord{\mathrm{Nef}}(X) \cap \partial\,\overline{\mathord{\mathcal P}}(X)).$$ It is known that $N(X)$ is an $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{S_X}\sp*$-chamber, and that the $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{S_X}$-minimal defining set $\Delta_{\mathord{\mathcal R}_{S_X}}(N(X))$ consists of the classes of smooth rational curves on $X$ (see, for example, Rudakov-Shafarevich [@MR633161]). For simplicity, we put $$\operatorname{\mathit {Aut}}\nolimits(N(X)):=\{\; {g\in \mathord{\mathrm {O}}^+({S_X})} \; \mid \; {N(X)^g=N(X)} \;  \}=\operatorname{\mathit {Aut}}\nolimits_{\mathord{\mathrm {O}}^+({S_X})}(N(X)).$$ By a *$(-2)$-wall*, we mean a wall bounding $N(X)$; that is, a wall $([C])\sp{\perp}$, where $C$ is a smooth rational curve on $X$.

## Complex $K3$ surfaces {#subsec:AutXC}

Suppose that $X$ is defined over $\mathord{\mathbb C}$. With the cup-product, the second cohomology group $H:=H^2(X, \mathord{\mathbb Z})$ is an even unimodular lattice of signature $(3, 19)$. Let $T_X$ denote the orthogonal complement of ${S_X}$ in $H$, which we call the *transcendental lattice* of $X$. We regard a non-zero holomorphic $2$-form $\omega_X$ on $X$ as a vector of ${T_X}\otimes\mathord{\mathbb C}$, and put $$\begin{equation}
\label{eq:CX}
C_{X}:=\{\; {g\in \mathord{\mathrm {O}}({T_X})} \; \mid \; {\omega_X^g=\lambda\, \omega_X\;\;\textrm{for some}\;\; \lambda\in \mathord{\mathbb C}\sp{\times}} \;  \}.
\end{equation}$$ Since $H$ is unimodular, the subgroup $H/({S_X}\oplus {T_X})$ of the discriminant group $A_{S_X}\oplus A_{T_X}$ of ${S_X}\oplus {T_X}$ is the graph of an isomorphism $$\delta_{ST}\colon (A_{S_X}, q_{S_X})\;\mathbin{\,\raise -.6pt\rlap{$\to$}\raise 3.5pt \hbox{\hskip .3pt$\mathord{\sim}$}\;}\;(A_{T_X}, -q_{T_X})$$ by Nikulin [@MR525944 Proposition 1.6.1], which induces an isomorphism of the automorphism groups $\delta_{ST*}\colon \mathord{\mathrm {O}}(q_{S_X})\mathbin{\,\raise -.6pt\rlap{$\to$}\raise 3.5pt \hbox{\hskip .3pt$\mathord{\sim}$}\;}\mathord{\mathrm {O}}(q_{T_X})$ of discriminant forms. Recall that, for an even lattice $L$, we have a natural homomorphism $\eta_L\colon \mathord{\mathrm {O}}(L)\to \mathord{\mathrm {O}}(q_L)$.

::: theorem
**Theorem 58** (Piatetski-Shapiro and Shafarevich [@MR0284440]). *`\label{thm:aut}`{=latex} Via the natural actions of $\operatorname{\mathrm {Aut}}\nolimits(X)$ on the lattices ${S_X}$ and ${T_X}$, the automorphism group $\operatorname{\mathrm {Aut}}\nolimits(X)$ of $X$ is identified with $$\{\; {(g, h)\in \operatorname{\mathit {Aut}}\nolimits(N(X))\times C_{X}} \; \mid \; {\delta_{ST*}(\eta_{S_X}(g))=\eta_{T_X}(h)} \;  \}.$$*
:::

Since $\mathord{\mathrm {O}}(q_{T_X})$ is finite, the subgroup $$\begin{equation}
\label{eq:GXcomplex}
G_X:=\{\; {g\in \mathord{\mathrm {O}}^+({S_X})} \; \mid \; {\delta_{ST*}(\eta_{S_X}(g)) \in \eta_{T_X}(C_X)} \;  \}
\end{equation}$$ of $\mathord{\mathrm {O}}^+({S_X})$ has finite index.

::: corollary
**Corollary 59**. *`\label{cor:KerIm}`{=latex} The kernel of the natural homomorphism $\varphi_X\colon \operatorname{\mathrm {Aut}}\nolimits(X)\to \mathord{\mathrm {O}}(S_X)$ is isomorphic to $\operatorname{\mathrm {Ker}}\nolimits(\eta_{T_X}) \cap {C_X}$, and its image is equal to $$\operatorname{\mathit {Aut}}\nolimits_{G_X}(N(X))=\{ {g\in G_X} \,|\, {N(X)^g=N(X)}   \}.$$*
:::

## Supersingular $K3$ surfaces in odd characteristics {#subsec:AutssK3}

Suppose that $X$ is a supersingular $K3$ surface defined over an algebraically closed field $k$ of *odd* characteristic $p$. By Artin [@MR0371899], we know that the discriminant group $A_{{S_X}}$ is a $p$-elementary abelian group of rank $2\sigma$, where $\sigma$ is a positive integer $\le 10$, which is called the *Artin invariant of $X$*. The $\mathord{\mathbb F}_p$-vector space $S_0:=p S_X\sp{\vee}/p{S_X}$ of dimension $2\sigma$ has a natural quadratic form $$%Q_0\;\;:\;\; px \bmod p{S_X} \mapsto px^2 \bmod p \quad (x\in S_X\dual)
Q_0\colon  px \bmod p{S_X} \mapsto px^2 \bmod p \quad (x\in S_X\sp{\vee})$$ that takes values in $\mathord{\mathbb F}_p$. We denote by $\mathord{\mathrm {O}}(Q_0)$ the finite group of automorphisms of $(S_0, Q_0)$. We have a natural homomorphism $\mathord{\mathrm {O}}({S_X}) \to \mathord{\mathrm {O}}(Q_0)$. We denote by $\varphi\colon S_0\otimes k\to S_0\otimes k$ the map $\mathord{\mathrm {id}}_{S_0}\otimes F_k$, where $F_k$ is the Frobenius map of $k$. Let $c_{\mathord{{\rm DR}}}\colon {S_X}\to H^2_{\mathord{{\rm DR}}}(X/k)$ denote the Chern class map. Then the kernel $\operatorname{\mathrm {Ker}}\nolimits(\bar{c}_{\mathord{{\rm DR}}})$ of the induced homomorphism $\bar{c}_{\mathord{{\rm DR}}}\colon {S_X}\otimes k \to H^2_{\mathord{{\rm DR}}}(X/k)$ from ${S_X}\otimes k=S_X/p S_X$ to $H^2_{\mathord{{\rm DR}}}(X/k)$ is contained in $S_0\otimes k$. (Note that we have $p S_X\sp{\vee}\subset S_X$.) The subspace $$K:=\varphi\sp{-1}(\operatorname{\mathrm {Ker}}\nolimits(\bar{c}_{\mathord{{\rm DR}}}))$$ of $S_0\otimes k$ is called the *period of $X$*.

::: theorem
**Theorem 60** (Ogus [@MR563467], [@MR717616]). *`\label{thm:ssK3aut}`{=latex} Via the natural action of $\operatorname{\mathrm {Aut}}\nolimits(X)$ on ${S_X}$, the automorphism group $\operatorname{\mathrm {Aut}}\nolimits(X)$ of $X$ is identified with $$\{\; {g\in \operatorname{\mathit {Aut}}\nolimits(N(X))} \; \mid \; {K^g=K} \;  \}.$$*
:::

Since $\mathord{\mathrm {O}}(Q_0)$ is finite, the subgroup $$\begin{equation}
\label{eq:GXss}
G_X:=\{\; {g\in \mathord{\mathrm {O}}^+({S_X})} \; \mid \; {K^g=K} \;  \}
\end{equation}$$ of $\mathord{\mathrm {O}}^+({S_X})$ has finite index.

::: corollary
**Corollary 61**. *The natural homomorphism $\operatorname{\mathrm {Aut}}\nolimits(X)\to \mathord{\mathrm {O}}(S_X)$ is injective, and its image is equal to $\operatorname{\mathit {Aut}}\nolimits_{G_X}(N(X))$.*
:::

# Geometric application of Algorithm `\ref{algo:main}`{=latex} {#sec:geometricapplication}

Let $X$ be a complex algebraic $K3$ surface or a supersingular $K3$ surface in odd characteristic. Let $S_X$ be the Néron-Severi lattice of $X$, and let $N(X)$ be the $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{S_X}\sp*$-chamber $\mathord{\mathrm{Nef}}(X)\cap \mathord{\mathcal P}(X)$ in the positive cone $\mathord{\mathcal P}(X)$ containing an ample class. Let $G_X$ be the subgroup of $\mathord{\mathrm {O}}^+(S_X)$ defined by `\eqref{eq:GXcomplex}`{=latex} or `\eqref{eq:GXss}`{=latex}. Applying Algorithm `\ref{algo:main}`{=latex} to the case $S=S_X$, $G=G_X$ and $N=N(X)$, we can calculate a finite set of generators of $$\operatorname{\mathrm {Im}}\nolimits(\varphi_X\colon \operatorname{\mathrm {Aut}}\nolimits(X)\to \mathord{\mathrm {O}}(S_X))=\operatorname{\mathit {Aut}}\nolimits_{G_X}(N (X))$$ and a closed domain $F_{N(X)}$ of $N(X)$ with the properties given in Proposition `\ref{prop:adj}`{=latex}, provided that the following hold:

- The subgroup $G_X$ of $\mathord{\mathrm {O}}^+(S_X)$ satisfies the condition $\mathord{\rm [G]}$ of the existence of a membership algorithm in Section `\ref{sec:chamber}`{=latex}.

- We can find a primitive embedding of $S_X$ into an even unimodular hyperbolic lattice $\mathord{\mathbf{L}}$ of rank $10$, $18$ or $26$ such that the orthogonal complement $R$ of $S_X$ in $\mathord{\mathbf{L}}$ satisfies $\mathord{\rm [SG2]}$ and $\mathord{\rm [SG3]}$ in Section `\ref{sec:Borcherds}`{=latex}.

- We can find a Weyl vector of an $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chamber $D_0$ contained in the $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{S}\sp*$-chamber $N(X)$.

We discuss these requirements for a complex $K3$ surface $X$. Let $\rho_X$ denote the Picard number of $X$, and let $T_X$ denote the transcendental lattice of $X$.

## Requirement (1). {#subsec:req1}

By the definition `\eqref{eq:GXcomplex}`{=latex} of $G_X$, we have a membership algorithm for the subgroup $G_X$ of $\mathord{\mathrm {O}}^+(S_X)$ if we have explicit descriptions of the isomorphism $\delta_{ST}\colon (A_{S_X}, q_{S_X})\mathbin{\,\raise -.6pt\rlap{$\to$}\raise 3.5pt \hbox{\hskip .3pt$\mathord{\sim}$}\;}(A_{T_X}, -q_{T_X})$ induced by the even unimodular overlattice $H^2(X, \mathord{\mathbb Z})$ of $S_X\oplus T_X$, the homomorphisms $\eta_{S_X}\colon \mathord{\mathrm {O}}(S_X)\to \mathord{\mathrm {O}}(q_{S_X})$, $\eta_{T_X}\colon \mathord{\mathrm {O}}(T_X)\to \mathord{\mathrm {O}}(q_{T_X})$, and the subgroup $C_X$ of $\mathord{\mathrm {O}}(T_X)$.

Suppose that $C_X=\{\pm 1\}$. Then the condition $g\in G_X$ is reduced to the condition $\eta_{S_X}(g)\in\{\pm 1\}$, and hence all we need to do is to calculate the homomorphism $\eta_{S_X}$. This assumption $C_X=\{\pm 1\}$ holds in many cases. For example, if $\rho_X<20$ and the period $\omega_X$ is generic in $T_X\otimes\mathord{\mathbb C}$, then $C_X=\{\pm 1\}$. Indeed, since the eigenspaces in $T_X\otimes\mathord{\mathbb C}$ of any $g\in \mathord{\mathrm {O}}(T_X)\setminus \{\pm 1\}$ are proper subspaces, a period $\omega_X$ for which $C_X\ne \{\pm 1\}$ must lie in a countable union of proper subspaces of $T_X\otimes\mathord{\mathbb C}$.

## Requirement (2). {#subsec:req2}

We have the following:

::: proposition
**Proposition 62**. *`\label{prop:embeddable}`{=latex} For a complex algebraic $K3$ surface $X$, the lattice $S_X$ has a primitive embedding into an even unimodular hyperbolic lattice $\mathord{\mathbf{L}}_{26}$ of rank $26$.*
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* Recall that $T_X$ is an even lattice of signature $(2, 20-\rho_X)$ such that $(A_{T_X}, q_{T_X})$ is isomorphic to $(A_{S_X}, -q_{S_X})$. By Nikulin [@MR525944 Theorem 1.10.1], the existence of the lattice $T_X$ implies the existence of an even lattice $R$ of signature $(0, 26-\rho_X)$ with $(A_R, q_R)\cong (A_{T_X}, q_{T_X})$. Hence, by Nikulin [@MR525944 Proposition 1.6.1], $S_X$ can be embedded primitively into an even unimodular hyperbolic lattice of rank $26$ with $R$ being the orthogonal complement. Since an even unimodular hyperbolic lattice of rank $26$ is unique up to isomorphism, Proposition `\ref{prop:embeddable}`{=latex} follows. ◻
:::

Therefore $S_X$ always satisfies the condition $\mathord{\rm [SG1]}$ in Section `\ref{sec:Borcherds}`{=latex}. It is, however, difficult in general to obtain a primitive embedding $S_X\hookrightarrow\mathord{\mathbf{L}}$ explicitly. In fact, by Nikulin [@MR525944 Proposition 1.6.1], this problem is equivalent to construct a negative-definite lattice $R$ of rank equal to $\operatorname{\mathrm {rank}}\nolimits\mathord{\mathbf{L}}-\operatorname{\mathrm {rank}}\nolimits S_X$ such that $(A_R, q_R)\cong (A_{S_X}, -q_{S_X})$ holds. For the algorithm to construct an integral lattice in a given genus explicitly, see Conway and Sloane [@MR1662447 Chapter 15].

::: remark
*Remark 63*. In the case where $X$ is supersingular, we can use the table [@MR1118842] of positive-definite integral lattices of rank $4$.
:::

Once a primitive embedding $S_X\hookrightarrow\mathord{\mathbf{L}}$ is obtained, we can calculate a Gram matrix of $R$ of the orthogonal complement, the isomorphism $\delta_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}\colon (A_{S_X}, q_{S_X})\mathbin{\,\raise -.6pt\rlap{$\to$}\raise 3.5pt \hbox{\hskip .3pt$\mathord{\sim}$}\;}(A_R, -q_R)$ of discriminant forms induced by $\mathord{\mathbf{L}}\subset S_X\sp{\vee}\oplus R\sp{\vee}$, and the induced isomorphism $\delta_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}*}\colon \mathord{\mathrm {O}}(q_{S_X})\mathbin{\,\raise -.6pt\rlap{$\to$}\raise 3.5pt \hbox{\hskip .3pt$\mathord{\sim}$}\;}\mathord{\mathrm {O}}(q_{R})$. Since both $R$ and $\Lambda$ are negative-definite, we can enumerate all embeddings of $R$ into $\Lambda$, and hence the condition $\mathord{\rm [SG2]}$ can be checked. In practice,  $\mathord{\rm [SG2]}$ is often verified simply by showing that $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{R}\ne \emptyset$. Note that $\mathord{\mathrm {O}}(R)$ is a finite group, and hence its image by $\eta_R\colon \mathord{\mathrm {O}}(R)\to \mathord{\mathrm {O}}(q_R)$ can be calculated. By the definition of $G_X$, the subgroup $\eta_{S_X}(G_X)$ of $\mathord{\mathrm {O}}(q_{S_X})$ is equal to $$\operatorname{\mathrm {Im}}\nolimits\eta_{S_X}\cap \delta_{ST*}\sp{-1}(\eta_{T_X}(C_X)).$$ Hence, if $\delta_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}*}\sp{-1}( \operatorname{\mathrm {Im}}\nolimits\eta_{R})$ contains $\delta_{ST*}\sp{-1}(\eta_{T_X}(C_X))$, then the liftability condition $\mathord{\rm [SG3]}$ is satisfied. This sufficient condition for $\mathord{\rm [SG3]}$ is fulfilled in the following cases that occur frequently: the case where $\eta_{R}$ is surjective, or the case where $C_X=\{\pm 1\}$.

## Requirement (3). {#subsec:req3}

In order to find an initial $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chamber $D_0$ contained in $N(X)$, we can employ one of the following two methods.

Let $\mathord{\mathcal D}_0$ be the $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}\sp*$-chamber corresponding to the standard Weyl vector $w_0$ given in Section `\ref{sec:Conway}`{=latex}. We choose an interior point $u_0$ of $\mathord{\mathcal D}_0$. When $\operatorname{\mathrm {rank}}\nolimits\mathord{\mathbf{L}}$ is $10$ or $18$, $w_0$ is an interior point of $\mathord{\mathcal D}_0$. When $\operatorname{\mathrm {rank}}\nolimits\mathord{\mathbf{L}}$ is $26$ and $w_0=f_U$, we can use $$u_0:=3f_U+z_U \in \mathord{\mathbf{L}}=U\oplus \Lambda,$$ because we have $u_0^2=4$, $\langle{u_0, w_0}\rangle_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}=1$ and $\langle{u_0, r_{\lambda}}\rangle_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}=1-\lambda^2/2>0$ for all $\lambda\in \Lambda$.

Next we find an ample class $a_0\in S_X$. The method of this step depends, of course, on what kind of geometric information of $X$ is available. Once an ample class $a_0$ is obtained, we can determine whether a given vector $v\in S_X$ is ample or not by means of the following criterion and Algorithms `\ref{algo:QLcab}`{=latex} and `\ref{algo:isnef}`{=latex}. Note that a vector of $S_X$ is ample if and only if it belongs to the interior of $N(X)$. Therefore a vector $v\in S_X$ is ample if and only if

- $v^2>0$ and $\langle{v, a_0}\rangle_{}>0$, so that $v\in \mathord{\mathcal P}(X)$,

- the set $\{ {r\in \mathord{\mathcal R}_{S_X}} \,|\, {\langle{v, r}\rangle_{}=0}   \}$ is empty, and

- the set $\{ {r\in \mathord{\mathcal R}_{S_X}} \,|\, {\langle{v, r}\rangle_{}<0,\, \langle{a_0, r}\rangle_{}>0}   \}$ is empty, so that the line segment in $\mathord{\mathcal P}(X)$ connecting $a_0$ and $v$ does not intersect any hyperplane $(r)\sp{\perp}$ perpendicular to some $(-2)$-vector $r$.

For example, we can produce many ample classes $Aa_0+u$ by putting $A$ to be sufficiently large integers and $u\in S_X$ to be vectors with relatively small coordinates.

We choose a general ample class $a\in S_X$. (The cases where $a$ is not general enough so that we have to re-choose $a$ are indicated below.) Let $i_0\colon S_X\hookrightarrow\mathord{\mathbf{L}}$ be a primitive embedding. We consider the oriented line segment $$\ell(t):=(1-t)\ i_0(a)+t\ u_0\quad ( 0\le t\le 1)$$ in $\mathord{\mathcal P}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}$ from $i_0(a)$ to $u_0$. By Algorithm `\ref{algo:isnef}`{=latex}, we calculate the finite set $$\{\; {r\in \mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}} \; \mid \; {\langle{u_0, r}\rangle_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}>0,\;\; \langle{i_0(a), r}\rangle_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}<0} \;  \}=\{r_1, \dots, r_N\}$$ and sort the elements of this set in such a way that $$t_1\le t_2<\dots\le t_N, \quad\textrm{where $t_i$ satisfies $\langle{\ell(t_i), r_i}\rangle_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}=0$}.$$ If $t_1, \dots, t_N$ are not distinct, we re-choose $a$. We assume that $t_1, \dots, t_N$ are distinct. Then the oriented line segment $\ell$ intersects the hyperplanes $(r_1)\sp{\perp}$, ..., $(r_N)\sp{\perp}$ in this order. We replace the embedding $i_0$ by $$i:=s_{r_N}\circ \cdots \circ s_{r_1}\circ i_0,$$ and consider $S_X$ as a primitive sublattice of $\mathord{\mathbf{L}}$ by $i$. Then the ample class $a$ is contained in $\mathord{\mathcal D}_0\cap \mathord{\mathcal P}(X)$. We check whether the inequalities `\eqref{eq:ineqSnondeg}`{=latex} in Criterion `\ref{criterion:Snondeg}`{=latex} are satisfied for $v=a$ and $w=w_0$. If not, then we re-choose $a$ and repeat the process again. If `\eqref{eq:ineqSnondeg}`{=latex} is satisfied, then $D_0:=\mathord{\mathcal D}_0\cap \mathord{\mathcal P}(X)$ is an $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chamber containing $a$ in its interior. In particular, $D_0$ is contained in $N(X)$ and the standard Weyl vector $w_0$ is a Weyl vector of $D_0$.

The other method does not necessarily succeed, but is practically useful. In fact, we have used this method in [@MR3286672] and [@KondoShimada]. Suppose that $\operatorname{\mathrm {rank}}\nolimits\mathord{\mathbf{L}}=26$. We choose an ample class $a\in S_X$ that is primitive in $S_X$. By Algorithm `\ref{algo:QLc}`{=latex}, we can calculate the list of all vectors $v\in R$ such that $a^2+v^2=0$. For each $v$ in this list, we determine whether the vector $w:=a+v\in S_X\oplus R$ of $\mathord{\mathbf{L}}$ is a Weyl vector or not by Theorem `\ref{thm:ConwaySloane}`{=latex}; we check whether $\langle {w}  \rangle\sp{\perp}/\langle {w}  \rangle$ is a negative-definite unimodular lattice with no $(-2)$-vectors by applying Algorithm `\ref{algo:QLc}`{=latex} to a Gram matrix of $\langle {w}  \rangle\sp{\perp}/\langle {w}  \rangle$. Suppose that a Weyl vector $w_0:=a+v_0$ of this form is found, and let $\mathord{\mathcal D}_0$ be the corresponding $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}\sp*$-chamber. We check whether the inequalities `\eqref{eq:ineqSnondeg}`{=latex} in Criterion `\ref{criterion:Snondeg}`{=latex} are satisfied for $v=a$ and $w=w_0$. If `\eqref{eq:ineqSnondeg}`{=latex} is satisfied, then $D_0:=\mathord{\mathcal D}_0\cap \mathord{\mathcal P}(X)$ is an $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S}\sp*$-chamber containing $a$ in its interior. In particular, $D_0$ is contained in $N(X)$ and the Weyl vector $w_0$ is a Weyl vector of $D_0$.

# Complex elliptic $K3$ surfaces with Picard number $3$ {#sec:rho3}

We demonstrate Algorithm `\ref{algo:main}`{=latex} on certain complex $K3$ surfaces with Picard number $3$, because, in this case, we can draw pictures of the closed domain $F_{N(X)}$ defined by `\eqref{eq:FN}`{=latex} in the hyperbolic plane.

Let $X$ be a complex $K3$ surface with Picard number $3$ and with a Jacobian fibration $$\phi\colon X\to\mathord{\mathbb  P}^1$$ whose Mordell-Weil group $\mathord{\mathrm{MW}}_{\phi}$ is of rank $1$. We assume that the period $\omega_X$ of $X$ is generic in $T_X\otimes\mathord{\mathbb C}$, which implies that the group $C_X$ defined by `\eqref{eq:CX}`{=latex} is equal to $\{\pm 1\}$. We denote by $f_{\phi}\in S_X$ the class of a fiber of $\phi$ and by $z_{\phi}\in S_X$ the class of the zero section of $\phi$. Then there exists a vector $v_3\in S_X$, unique up to sign, such that $f_{\phi}, z_{\phi}, v_3$ form a basis of $S_X$, and that the Gram matrix of $S_X$ with respect to $f_{\phi}, z_{\phi}, v_3$ is $$M:=\left[
\begin{array}{ccc}
0 & 1 & 0 \\
1 & -2 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & -2k
\end{array}
\right],$$ where $k:=-v_3^2/2$. Since $\mathord{\mathrm{MW}}_{\phi}$ is of rank $1$, there exist no reducible fibers of $\phi$ and hence we have $k>1$ (see, for example, [@MR1081832]). A vector $\xi f_\phi +\eta z_\phi +\zeta v_3$ of $S_X\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}$ is written as $[\xi, \eta, \zeta]$. The discriminant group $A_{S_X}$ of $S_X$ is a cyclic group of order $2k$ generated by $u_3 \bmod S_X$, where $u_3:=[0,0,-1/2k]$. Since $k>1$, we have $-1\notin \operatorname{\mathrm {Ker}}\nolimits(\eta_{T_X})$. Therefore, by Corollary `\ref{cor:KerIm}`{=latex} and the assumption $C_X=\{\pm 1\}$, the natural homomorphism $\operatorname{\mathrm {Aut}}\nolimits(X)\to \mathord{\mathrm {O}}^+(S_X)$ is injective, and $\operatorname{\mathrm {Aut}}\nolimits(X)\cong \operatorname{\mathit {Aut}}\nolimits_{G_X}(N(X))$ holds, where $G_X$ is defined by `\eqref{eq:GXcomplex}`{=latex}. We describe the group $G_X$ in terms of matrices. Note that the class $a:=[2,1,0]\in S_X$ is nef, and hence $g\in \mathord{\mathrm {O}}(S_X)$ belongs to $\mathord{\mathrm {O}}^+(S_X)$ if and only if $\langle{a^g, a}\rangle_{S_X}>0$. Therefore $G_X$ is canonically identified with $$\{\; {g\in \mathord{\mathrm {GL}}_3(\mathord{\mathbb Z})} \; \mid \; { g\,M\,{}^t g=M, \;\; a\, g\, M\, {}^ta >0, \;\;  u_3 g\equiv \pm u_3\bmod \mathord{\mathbb Z}^3} \;  \}.$$ This identification provides us with the membership algorithm for $G_X$ in the condition $\mathord{\rm [G]}$ in Section `\ref{sec:chamber}`{=latex}.

We embed $S_X$ into the even unimodular hyperbolic lattice $\mathord{\mathbf{L}}=U\oplus E_8$ of rank $10$ primitively. Note that $\mathord{\rm [SG2]}$ is irrelevant in this case, and $\mathord{\rm [SG3]}$ holds because $C_X=\{\pm 1\}$. Let $\mathord{\mathcal D}_0$ be the $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}}\sp*$-chamber with the Weyl vector $w_0\in \mathord{\mathbf{L}}=U\oplus E_8$ given in Section `\ref{subsec:10}`{=latex}. We will find a primitive embedding $i\colon S_X\hookrightarrow\mathord{\mathbf{L}}$ such that $D_0:=i\sp{-1}(\mathord{\mathcal D}_0)$ is an $\mathord{\mathcal R}_{\mathord{\mathbf{L}}|S_X}\sp*$-chamber contained in $N(X)$ by the method described in Section `\ref{subsec:req3}`{=latex}. To find an initial primitive embedding $i_0$, it is enough to choose a primitive vector $v_3\sp\prime\in E_8$ such that $v_3\sp{\prime 2}=-2k$, and define $i_0\colon S_X\hookrightarrow\mathord{\mathbf{L}}$ by $$i_0(f_\phi)=f_U, \quad
i_0(z_\phi)=z_U, \quad
i_0(v_3)=v_3\sp\prime,$$ where $f_U$ and $z_U$ are the basis of the hyperbolic plane $U$ with respect to which the Gram matrix is `\eqref{eq:nonstandardU}`{=latex}. As the general ample class $a$, we use $[3A, A, -1]$ with $A$ large enough.

There exist two obvious elements in $\operatorname{\mathrm {Aut}}\nolimits(X)$. We have a canonical bijection $$\mathord{\mathrm{MW}}_{\phi}\;\;\cong\;\;\{\; {[k s^2, 1, s]} \; \mid \; {s\in\mathord{\mathbb Z}} \;  \}$$ by sending sections of $\phi$ to the classes of their images. The Mordell-Weil group $\mathord{\mathrm{MW}}_{\phi}\cong \mathord{\mathbb Z}$ acts on $X$ as translations. We also have the inversion automorphism $\iota_X\colon X\to X$ induced by the multiplication by $-1$ on the generic fiber of $\phi$. Therefore $\operatorname{\mathrm {Aut}}\nolimits(X)$ contains $\mathord{\mathrm{MW}}_{\phi}\rtimes\langle {\iota_X}  \rangle\cong \mathord{\mathbb Z}/2\mathord{\mathbb Z}* \mathord{\mathbb Z}/2\mathord{\mathbb Z}$, which is generated by the two involutions $$h_1 :=\iota_X= \left[
\begin{array}{ccc}
1 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 1 & 0\\
0 & 0 & -1
\end{array}\right],
\quad
h_2 := \left[
\begin{array}{ccc}
1 & 0 & 0 \\
k & 1 & -1\\
2k & 0 & -1
\end{array}\right].$$

To describe the closed domain $F_{N(X)}$ satisfying the properties given in Proposition `\ref{prop:adj}`{=latex}, we use the following method. The norm of $[1,x,y]\in S_X\otimes\mathord{\mathbb R}$ is $2x-2x^2-2ky^2$. Hence, by the map $[1,x,y]\mapsto (x, y)$, the hyperbolic plane $\mathord{\mathbb H}_{S_X}$ associated with $S_X$ is identified with $$H_X:=\{\; {(x, y)\in \mathord{\mathbb R}^2} \; \mid \; {(x-1/2)^2+(\sqrt{k} y)^2<1/4} \;  \}.$$ The vector $f_{\phi}$ corresponds to the point $(0, 0)$ of $\overline{H}_X$, and the hyperplane $(z_{\phi})\sp{\perp}$ is given by $x=1/2$. The Poincaré disk model of the hyperbolic plane $\mathord{\mathbb H}_{S_X}$ is given by the mapping $$\begin{equation}

\end{equation}$$ from $H_X$ to $\varDelta:=\{ {z\in \mathord{\mathbb C}} \,|\, {|z|< 1}   \}$. This map sends the boundary point corresponding to $f_{\phi}$ to $1\in\bar{\varDelta}$, and the hyperplane $(z_{\phi})\sp{\perp}$ to the imaginary axis in $\varDelta$.

# Singular $K3$ surfaces {#sec:singK3}

Recall that a $K3$ surface defined over $\mathord{\mathbb C}$ is singular if its Picard number attains the possible maximum $20$. We demonstrate Algorithm `\ref{algo:main}`{=latex} on singular $K3$ surfaces $X$ such that their transcendental lattices $T_X$ satisfy $\operatorname{\mathrm {disc}}\nolimits T_X\le 16$. There exist, up to isomorphisms, exactly eleven singular $K3$ surfaces with $\operatorname{\mathrm {disc}}\nolimits T_X\le 16$. Table `\ref{table:singK3}`{=latex} shows Gram matrices $$\left[
\begin{array}{cc}
a & b \\
b & c
\end{array}
\right]$$ of the transcendental lattices of these $K3$ surfaces. We embed $S_X$ into an even unimodular hyperbolic lattice $\mathord{\mathbf{L}}$ of rank $26$. By the *root-$R$ condition*, we mean that there should exist a negative-definite root lattice $R$ of rank $6$ that satisfies $(A_{R}, q_{R})\cong (A_{T_X}, q_{T_X})$. If $T_X$ satisfies the root-$R$ condition, then there exists a primitive embedding $S_X\hookrightarrow\mathord{\mathbf{L}}$ such that the orthogonal complement is isomorphic to a root lattice $R$, and hence Borcherds' method terminates quickly (see Remark `\ref{rem:Rroots}`{=latex}). The column root-$R$ of Table `\ref{table:singK3}`{=latex} indicates the existence of such a root lattice $R$, and its $ADE$-type if it exists.

The singular $K3$ surfaces of Nos. 1, 2, 3, 7 and 11 have been studied previously. Therefore we treat the other six singular $K3$ surfaces Nos. 4, 5, 6, 8, 9 and 10.

## The period and ample classes of $X$

The period $\omega_X$ is given in $T_X\otimes\mathord{\mathbb C}$ as a vector $(1, \alpha)$, where $\alpha$ is a root of $$a+2bx+cx^2=0.$$ The choice of a root of this quadratic equation does not matter, because, in the eleven cases in Table `\ref{table:singK3}`{=latex}, the orientation reversing of $T_X$ yields an isomorphic oriented lattice; that is, the $\mathord{\mathrm {GL}}_2(\mathord{\mathbb Z})$-equivalence class of $T_X$ contains only one $\mathord{\mathrm{SL}}_2(\mathord{\mathbb Z})$-equivalence class (see [@MR1820211]). Since $\mathord{\mathrm {O}}(T_X)$ is finite, we can calculate the subgroup $C_X$ defined by `\eqref{eq:CX}`{=latex}. It turns out that, in the six cases Nos. 4, 5, 6, 8, 9 and 10, we have $C_X=\{\pm 1\}$. Therefore the conditions $\mathord{\rm [G]}$ and $\mathord{\rm [SG3]}$ for $G_X$ and $R$ are satisfied (see Sections `\ref{subsec:req1}`{=latex} and `\ref{subsec:req2}`{=latex}).

In the following, we use the general theory of elliptic surfaces, for which we refer to [@MR1081832] or [@MR1813537]. Shioda and Inose [@MR0441982] showed that every singular $K3$ surface $X$ has a Jacobian fibration $\phi\colon X\to\mathord{\mathbb  P}^1$ with two singular fibers $\phi\sp{-1}(p)$ and $\phi\sp{-1}(p\sp\prime)$ of type $II^*$. Hence $S_X$ contains an even unimodular hyperbolic lattice $\mathord{\mathbf{L}}_{18}(\phi):=U_{\phi}\oplus E_8\oplus E_8$ of rank $18$ as a sublattice, where $U_{\phi}$ is spanned by the class $f_{\phi}$ of a fiber of $\phi$ and the class $z_{\phi}$ of the zero section of $\phi$ (and hence the Gram matrix of $U_{\phi}$ with respect the basis $f_{\phi}, z_{\phi}$ is `\eqref{eq:nonstandardU}`{=latex}), and each copy of $E_8$ is spanned by the classes of irreducible components of a singular fiber of type $II^*$. Since $\mathord{\mathbf{L}}_{18}(\phi)$ is unimodular, there exists a negative-definite lattice $T_X^{-}$ of rank $2$ such that $$S_X=\mathord{\mathbf{L}}_{18}(\phi)\oplus T_X^{-}.$$ We have $(A_{T_X^{-}}, q_{T_X^{-}})\cong(A_{T_X}, -q_{T_X})$. Since the Jacobian fibration $\phi$ is not unique, the isomorphism class of $T_X^{-}$ is not unique in general (see [@MR2346573] and [@MR2452829]). However, for the eleven cases in Table `\ref{table:singK3}`{=latex}, $T_X^{-}$ is uniquely determined by the condition $(A_{T_X^{-}}, q_{T_X^{-}})\cong(A_{T_X}, -q_{T_X})$ and is isomorphic to $(-1)T_X$.

From Table `\ref{table:orbitdecompD0}`{=latex}, we see the following.

In No. 4, the automorphism group $\operatorname{\mathrm {Aut}}\nolimits(X)$ is generated by the finite group $\operatorname{\mathrm {Aut}}\nolimits(X, h_E)$ of order $48$ and six extra automorphisms corresponding to the six walls of $D_0$ that are not $(-2)$-walls. Exactly four orbits consist of $(-2)$-walls, and hence the number of orbits of $\operatorname{\mathrm {Aut}}\nolimits(X)$ on the set of smooth rational curves on $X$ is at most $4$.

In No. 6, $\operatorname{\mathrm {Aut}}\nolimits(X)$ is generated by the finite group $\operatorname{\mathrm {Aut}}\nolimits(X, h_E)$ of order $144$ and six extra automorphisms. The number of orbits of $\operatorname{\mathrm {Aut}}\nolimits(X)$ on the set of smooth rational curves is at most $4$.

In No. 8, $\operatorname{\mathrm {Aut}}\nolimits(X)$ is generated by the finite group $\operatorname{\mathrm {Aut}}\nolimits(X, h_E)$ of order $720$ and seven extra automorphisms, and $\operatorname{\mathrm {Aut}}\nolimits(X)$ acts on the set of smooth rational curves transitively.
