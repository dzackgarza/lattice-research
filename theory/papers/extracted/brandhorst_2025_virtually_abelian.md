# Introduction

According to the Tits alternative [@tits], any finitely generated subgroup of isometries of a hyperbolic lattice $L$ is either virtually solvable or it contains a free, non-abelian subgroup, where we say that a property of a group holds *virtually* if it holds for a finite index subgroup. In view of our geometric applications, we focus on a specific subgroup of isometries of $L$, namely its *symmetry group*. Let $\mathbb{H}_L$ be the hyperbolic space associated to the hyperbolic lattice $L$. The *Weyl group* $W(L)\subseteq \mathop{\mathrm{O}}^+(L)$ is the subgroup generated by reflections into $(-2)$-roots of $L$, and we denote by $\mathcal{D}_L$ the closure of a fundamental domain for the action of $W(L)$ on the positive cone of $L$. Then the symmetry group of $L$ can be equivalently defined as the quotient $\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(\mathcal{D}_L) \coloneqq \mathop{\mathrm{O}}^+(L)/W(L)$, or as the subgroup of $\mathop{\mathrm{O}}^+(L)$ preserving $\mathcal{D}_L$.

Basic results in hyperbolic geometry show that the symmetry group $\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(\mathcal{D}_L)$ is virtually solvable if and only if it is virtually abelian, and this is the case exactly when $\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(\mathcal{D}_L)$ is *elementary*, i.e. it has a finite orbit in $\mathbb{H}_L$ (cf. [@ratcliffe Theorems 5.5.9 and 5.5.10]). There are three types of elementary groups of isometries in $\mathop{\mathrm{O}}^+(L)$ (cf. [@ratcliffe §5.5]):

1.  Elementary groups *of elliptic type* have a fixed point in the interior of $\mathbb{H}_L$, and they are finite;

2.  Elementary groups *of parabolic type* have precisely one fixed point in the boundary $\partial \mathbb{H}_L$, and they have zero entropy (cf. [@brandhorst.mezzedimi Theorem 3.7]);

3.  Elementary groups *of hyperbolic type* have two fixed points in $\partial \mathbb{H}_L$, and they are virtually cyclic.

The main goal of this paper is to complete the classification of virtually solvable (or abelian) symmetry groups of hyperbolic lattices. In rank $\le 2$, it is well known that the symmetry group $\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(\mathcal{D}_L)$ is either finite or elementary of hyperbolic type (see e.g. [@galluzzi.lombardo.peters]). Hyperbolic lattices of rank $\ge 3$ with finite symmetry group were classified by Nikulin [@nikulin.finite.aut.greater.5; @nikulin.finite.aut.3] and Vinberg [@vinberg.finite.aut.4], while lattices with elementary symmetry groups of parabolic type were recently classified in [@brandhorst.mezzedimi] (and independently by X. Yu in [@yu.entropy] in the case of Picard lattices of K3 surfaces). By Nikulin [@nikulin.interesting Theorem 1], the number of isomorphism classes of lattices of a fixed rank $n\geq 3$ with elementary symmetry group of hyperbolic type is finite. Our main result is the following:

::: {#thm:main .theorem}
**Theorem 1**. *A hyperbolic lattice of rank $\ge 3$ has a virtually solvable symmetry group if and only if it belongs to an explicit list of $360$ lattices. More precisely, $\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(\mathcal{D}_L)$ is:*

1.  *finite if and only if $L$ belongs to Nikulin--Vinberg's list of $118$ lattices. In particular $\mathop{\mathrm{rk}}(L)\le 19$.*

2.  *elementary of parabolic type if and only if $L$ belongs to the list in [@brandhorst.mezzedimi] of $194$ Borcherds lattices. In particular $\mathop{\mathrm{rk}}(L)\le 26$.*

3.  *elementary of hyperbolic type if and only if $L$ belongs to the list of $48$ lattices in [13](#thm:classification.3){reference-type="ref+Label" reference="thm:classification.3"} and [15](#thm:classification.4){reference-type="ref+Label" reference="thm:classification.4"}. In particular $\mathop{\mathrm{rk}}(L)\le 4$.*
:::

Thanks to the Torelli theorem, the automorphism group of a K3 surface $X$ over an algebraically closed field $k$ can be identified, up to a finite group, with the symmetry group of its Picard lattice $\mathop{\mathrm{Pic}}(X)$. Therefore a byproduct of the classification in [1](#thm:main){reference-type="ref+Label" reference="thm:main"} is the following:

::: corollary
**Corollary 2**. *Let $X$ be a K3 surface over an algebraically closed field $k$. The automorphism group $\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(X)$ is virtually solvable if and only if either $\rho(X)=\mathop{\mathrm{rk}}(\mathop{\mathrm{Pic}}(X))\le 2$, or $\mathop{\mathrm{Pic}}(X)$ belongs to an explicit list of $359$ lattices of rank $\ge 3$. If this is the case, the rank of a maximal abelian subgroup of $\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(X)$ is at most $8$.*
:::

K3 surfaces with an elementary automorphism group of elliptic or parabolic type are the so-called K3 surfaces *of zero entropy*, and they were studied in our previous paper [@brandhorst.mezzedimi]. From a dynamical point of view, K3 surfaces with an elementary automorphism group of hyperbolic type are more interesting: all its automorphisms of infinite order have positive entropy [@ratcliffe Theorem 5.5.8], and therefore its elliptic fibrations (if they exist at all) have finite Mordell--Weil group (see [3](#lem:detect_virtually_abelian){reference-type="ref+Label" reference="lem:detect_virtually_abelian"}).

## Strategy and outline {#strategy-and-outline .unnumbered}

It follows from a result of Nikulin [@nikulin.factor.groups Theorem 9.1.1] that, if a hyperbolic lattice of rank $\ge 5$ has an elementary symmetry group, then it is either of elliptic or parabolic type (cf. [@brandhorst.mezzedimi Theorem 3.9]). Therefore, the core of our classification is in rank $3$ and $4$. Let $L$ by a hyperbolic lattice of rank $3$ or $4$ with an elementary symmetry group of hyperbolic type. Then it is $2$-reflective of hyperbolic type in the sense of [@nikulin.interesting Definition 2]. By [@nikulin.interesting Lemma 1] we know that $L$ admits a $\mathbb{Q}$-basis of $(-2)$-roots with bounded intersection multiplicity. This makes the classification in [1](#thm:main){reference-type="ref+Label" reference="thm:main"} into a finite, though impractically long, task of checking all the possible lattices. Therefore, we develop three tests, the *overlattice test* (see [6](#lem:overlattice){reference-type="ref+Label" reference="lem:overlattice"}), the *sublattice test* (see [\[alg:sublattice\]](#alg:sublattice){reference-type="ref+Label" reference="alg:sublattice"}) and the *cusp test* (see [\[alg:cusp test\]](#alg:cusp test){reference-type="ref+Label" reference="alg:cusp test"}) that allow us to exclude all but few lattices. We then employ the first author's implementation in OSCAR [@OSCAR] of Borcherds' method [@shimada.borcherds] as formulated by Shimada to obtain the classification in [1](#thm:main){reference-type="ref+Label" reference="thm:main"}. Our computations also confirm the correctness of Nikulin's and Vinberg's classifications of hyperbolic lattices with finite symmetry group and rank $3$ and $4$ in [@nikulin.finite.aut.3] and [@vinberg.finite.aut.4].

Let us briefly outline the contents of the paper. In [2](#sec:preliminaries){reference-type="ref+Label" reference="sec:preliminaries"} we recall some known properties of hyperbolic lattices with an elementary symmetry group of hyperbolic type, and we prove the simple, but crucial, [4](#prop:easy_fundamental_chamber){reference-type="ref+Label" reference="prop:easy_fundamental_chamber"}. In [3](#sec:tests){reference-type="ref+Label" reference="sec:tests"} we introduce the three tests discussed above, and then in [4](#sec:classification){reference-type="ref+Label" reference="sec:classification"} we apply them to obtain the final classification. In [5](#appendix){reference-type="ref+Label" reference="appendix"} we give an algorithm realising the weak approximation theorem for orthogonal groups and use it to determine if two indefinite lattices of rank at least $3$ are isometric. The algorithm is used in our classification to reduce computation times and to ensure that the final result consists of pairwise non-isometric lattices.

# Preliminaries {#sec:preliminaries}

We keep notations as in our previous article [@brandhorst.mezzedimi]. We refer to [@brandhorst.mezzedimi §2] for the basic definitions about lattices, and only recall the notions that will be strictly necessary for our purposes.

Let $L$ be a hyperbolic lattice, and denote by $\mathbb{H}_L$ the hyperbolic space associated to $L$. We choose a (closed) fundamental chamber $\mathcal{D}_L$ for the action of the Weyl group $W(L)$ on $L_\mathbb{R}:=L \otimes \mathbb{R}$, and, by a slight abuse of notation, we denote again by $\mathcal{D}_L$ the image of $\mathcal{D}_L$ in $\mathbb{H}_L$. The chamber $\mathcal{D}_L$ is a locally polyhedral convex cone, whose walls are given by $r^\perp$, where $r$ runs along the simple $(-2)$-roots of $L$. The chambers $\{w(\mathcal{D}_L)\}_{w\in W(L)}$ are called *Weyl chambers* of $L$, and they form a tessellation of $\mathbb{H}_L$. If $L'$ is another lattice in $L\otimes \mathbb{Q}$ of full rank, then we always choose $\mathcal{D}_{L'}$ such that $\mathcal{D}_{L'}^\circ\cap \mathcal{D}_{L}^\circ\ne\emptyset$ (note that if $L'$ is of smaller rank, this may not be possible).

The group $\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(\mathcal{D}_L)$ of isometries of $L$ preserving the fundamental chamber $\mathcal{D}_L$ is called the *symmetry group* of $L$. By construction it coincides with the quotient $\mathop{\mathrm{O}}^+(L)/W(L)$. The symmetry group $\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(\mathcal{D}_L)$ is *elementary* if it has a finite orbit in $\mathbb{H}_L$. If $\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(\mathcal{D}_L)$ is infinite, we say that it is *elementary of hyperbolic type* if it has two fixed points in the boundary $\partial \mathbb{H}_L$ (cf. [@ratcliffe §5.5]). In this case $\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(\mathcal{D}_L)$ is virtually cyclic, and all the isometries in $\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(\mathcal{D}_L)$ of infinite order have positive entropy [@ratcliffe Theorem 5.5.8]. Symmetry groups of hyperbolic lattices of rank $\le 2$ are either finite or elementary of hyperbolic type (see [@galluzzi.lombardo.peters]). More precisely, a hyperbolic lattice of rank $2$ has finite symmetry group if and only if it does not represent $0$ or $-2$ [@galluzzi.lombardo.peters Corollary 3.4]. We collect in the following lemma the known properties of hyperbolic lattices with an elementary symmetry group of hyperbolic type.

::: {#lem:detect_virtually_abelian .lemma}
**Lemma 3**. *Let $L$ be a hyperbolic lattice with an elementary symmetry group of hyperbolic type. Then:*

1.  *The rank of $L$ is at most $4$.*

2.  *If $0\ne v\in L$ is an isotropic vector, its stabilizer in $\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(\mathcal{D}_L)$ is finite and $v^\perp/\langle v \rangle$ is a root lattice.*

3.  *A finite index sublattice of $L$ is spanned by $(-2)$-roots $\{\delta_i\}_{i=1,\ldots,\mathop{\mathrm{rk}}(L)}$ such that $-2 \le \delta_i . \delta_j \le 18$ for all $i,j$.*
:::

::: proof
*Proof.*

1.  It follows by [@nikulin.factor.groups Theorem 9.1.1] and the preceding discussion (also cf. [@brandhorst.mezzedimi Theorem 3.9]).

2.  It follows by [@brandhorst.mezzedimi Theorem 3.9 and Proposition 3.2] that $v^\perp/ \langle v \rangle$ is an overlattice of a root lattice. Since it has rank at most $2$, and the root lattices of rank $2$ $A_1,A_2,A_1\oplus A_1$ have no non-trivial overlattices, it is a root lattice.

3.  This is [@nikulin.interesting Lemma 1].

 ◻
:::

We conclude this section with a crucial proposition, which is a refinement of [@mezzedimi.entropy Theorem 3.6]. We say that two groups $G_1,G_2$ are *commensurable* if they admit isomorphic finite index subgroups $H_1\subseteq G_1$, $H_2\subseteq G_2$.

::: {#prop:easy_fundamental_chamber .proposition}
**Proposition 4**. *Let $L$ be a hyperbolic lattice and $M\subseteq L$ a primitive sublattice of corank $1$. If $\mathop{\mathrm{disc}}(L)\ge 2\mathop{\mathrm{disc}}(M)$, then $M_\mathbb{R}\cap \mathcal{D}_{L}$ is a Weyl chamber of $M$. In particular $\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(\mathcal{D}_L)$ has a subgroup commensurable with $\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(\mathcal{D}_M)$.*
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* The fundamental chamber $\mathcal{D}_L$ of $L$ is cut out by hyperplanes given by the $(-2)$-roots of $L$, and therefore $M_\mathbb{R}\cap \mathcal{D}_{L}$ is cut out by the hyperplanes $H_r \coloneqq r^\perp\otimes \mathbb{R}$ given by $(-2)$-roots $r\in L$ of the form $r=x+y$, with $x\in M^\vee$ and $y\in N^\vee$, where $N\coloneqq M^\perp$. Note that $N$ is negative definite. Clearly $M_\mathbb{R}\cap \mathcal{D}_L$ is contained in a Weyl chamber of $M$, which we denote by $\mathcal{D}_M$. If $x^2 \ge 0$, then $H_r$ does not intersect $\mathcal{D}_{M}^\circ$, since $M$ is a hyperbolic lattice. Therefore we may assume $x^2 < 0$, or equivalently $y^2>-2$.

In turn, the Weyl chamber of $M$ is cut out by a subset of such hyperplanes, namely those corresponding to $(-2)$-roots of $M$, or equivalently those with $y=0$. In particular $M_\mathbb{R}\cap \mathcal{D}_{L}$ coincides with the Weyl chamber $\mathcal{D}_M$ if there is no $(-2)$-root $r=x+y\in L$ with $x\in M^\vee$, $y\in N^\vee$, and $y^2 \in (-2,0)$.

By contradiction, assume that there is such a $(-2)$-root $r=x+y$. The vector $y\in N^\vee$ belongs to the image $\pi(L)$ of the orthogonal projection $\pi: L \to N^\vee$. Set $n\coloneqq [L:M\oplus N] = [\pi(L):N]$, and let $-2k$ be the norm of the generator of the rank $1$ lattice $N$. We have $$\mathop{\mathrm{disc}}(L)\cdot n^2 = \mathop{\mathrm{disc}}(M)\mathop{\mathrm{disc}}(N)=2k \mathop{\mathrm{disc}}(M),$$ so $$-2 < y^2 \le \max(\pi(L)) = -2k/n^2  = -\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{disc}}(L)}{\mathop{\mathrm{disc}}(M)},$$ which implies that $\mathop{\mathrm{disc}}(L)< 2\mathop{\mathrm{disc}}(M)$, a contradiction.

For the second statement, observe that there exists a finite index subgroup $G$ of $\mathop{\mathrm{O}}(M)$ of isometries that extend to $L$. Then, by the first part, the injective map $G\hookrightarrow \mathop{\mathrm{O}}(L)$ descends to an inclusion $G \cap \mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(\mathcal{D}_M)\hookrightarrow \mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(\mathcal{D}_L)$, giving the desired claim. ◻
:::

The following lemma will allow us to easily apply [4](#prop:easy_fundamental_chamber){reference-type="ref+Label" reference="prop:easy_fundamental_chamber"} in explicit examples.

::: {#lem:easy_vectors .lemma}
**Lemma 5**. *Let $L$ be an even lattice and $v \in L$ primitive. Set $k \mathbb{Z}= v.L$, $kg=|v^2|$ and $M=v^\perp \subseteq L$. Then $$2\mathop{\mathrm{disc}}(M) \leq \mathop{\mathrm{disc}}(L) \quad \Leftrightarrow \quad 2g \leq k.$$ Moreover, $k \mid \mathop{\mathrm{disc}}(L)$.*
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* We note that $g=[L:M \oplus \mathbb{Z}v]$. Then $$\mathop{\mathrm{disc}}(L) g^2 = |v^2|\mathop{\mathrm{disc}}(M)=kg \mathop{\mathrm{disc}}(M)$$ so $$2\mathop{\mathrm{disc}}(M) = 2 \mathop{\mathrm{disc}}(L)g/k \leq \mathop{\mathrm{disc}}(L)$$ if and only if $$2 g \leq k.$$ Since $g$ divides $\mathop{\mathrm{disc}}(M)$, we get that $\mathop{\mathrm{disc}}(L)=k \mathop{\mathrm{disc}}(M)/g$, i.e. $k \mid \mathop{\mathrm{disc}}L$. ◻
:::

# The tests {#sec:tests}

In this section we present the tests that we use to decide whether a given hyperbolic lattice of rank $3$ or $4$ has an elementary symmetry group of hyperbolic type. The first one is the *overlattice test*, and it is given by the following easy observation.

::: {#lem:overlattice .lemma}
**Lemma 6**. *If $L$ has a virtually abelian symmetry group and $L'$ is an overlattice of $L$, then $L'$ has virtually abelian symmetry group as well. Moreover, if $L$ has elementary symmetry group of hyperbolic type, then the symmetry group of $L'$ is either finite or elementary of hyperbolic type as well.*
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* The subgroup $S$ of symmetries of $L'$ that preserve $L$ is of finite index in $\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(\mathcal{D}_{L'})$. Since the fundamental chamber of $L'$ is contained in the fundamental chamber of $L$, we have $S \subseteq \mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(\mathcal{D}_L)$. Therefore $S$ is virtually abelian and hence so is $\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(\mathcal{D}_{L'})$. If $\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(\mathcal{D}_L)$ is elementary of hyperbolic type, then $S$ is either finite or elementary of hyperbolic type as well. ◻
:::

The second test is called the *sublattice test*. It is based on the following proposition, which in turn relies on [4](#prop:easy_fundamental_chamber){reference-type="ref+Label" reference="prop:easy_fundamental_chamber"}.

::: {#prop:rank2sublattice .proposition}
**Proposition 7**. *Let $L$ be a hyperbolic lattice of rank $3$, and assume that there exist two *non-isometric* primitive sublattices $M_1,M_2$ of $L$ of rank $2$, not representing $0$ or $-2$, such that $\mathop{\mathrm{disc}}(L)\ge 2\mathop{\mathrm{disc}}(M_i)$ for $i=1,2$. Then the action of $\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(\mathcal{D}_L)$ on $\mathbb{H}_L$ is not elementary, and in particular $\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(\mathcal{D}_L)$ is not virtually abelian.*
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* By assumption, the symmetry group of $M_1$ and $M_2$ is elementary of hyperbolic type, and in particular it is virtually cyclic. By [4](#prop:easy_fundamental_chamber){reference-type="ref+Label" reference="prop:easy_fundamental_chamber"}, and up to replacing $M_2$ by $w(M_2)$ for some element $w \in W(L)$, we have $\mathcal{D}_{M_i} = M_i \otimes \mathbb{R}\cap \mathcal{D}_{L}$.

Let $f_1,f_2$ be symmetries of infinite order of $M_1$ and $M_2$, respectively. [4](#prop:easy_fundamental_chamber){reference-type="ref+Label" reference="prop:easy_fundamental_chamber"} implies that (some iterate of) $f_1$ and $f_2$ extend to symmetries of $L$, which, by abuse of notation, we still denote $f_1$ and $f_2$; we claim that the subgroup $\Gamma \coloneqq \langle f_1,f_2\rangle$ of $\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(\mathcal{D}_L)$ is not elementary, so that $\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(\mathcal{D}_L)$ is not elementary as well.

If $\Gamma$ is elementary, there exists a vector $v\in \partial \overline{\mathbb{H}}_L$ whose orbits under $f_1$ and $f_2$ are finite. The only finite orbit of $f_1$ (resp. $f_2$) is given by the two points in $\partial \overline{\mathbb{H}}_{M_1}$ (resp. $\partial \overline{\mathbb{H}}_{M_2}$). These two vectors generate $(M_1)_\mathbb{R}$ (resp. $(M_2)_\mathbb{R}$), hence if $\Gamma$ is elementary, then $\partial \overline{\mathbb{H}}_{M_1} = \partial \overline{\mathbb{H}}_{M_2}$ and in particular $(M_1)_\mathbb{R}= (M_2)_\mathbb{R}$. Since $M_1$ and $M_2$ are both primitive sublattices of $L$, this implies that $M_1 = M_2$, which is in contradiction to the assumption that they are not isometric. ◻
:::

# An algorithm for weak approximation and an isometry test {#appendix}

In this section we give an algorithm to determine if two non-degenerate indefinite integer lattices of rank at least $3$ are isometric.\
The difficulty is the following: in order to decide if two lattices $L_1$ and $L_2$ in the same genus are in the same spinor genus (and therefore isometric), one has to produce an isometry $f\colon L_1 \to L_2 \otimes \mathbb{Q}$ such that $[L_2:f(L_1) \cap L_2]$ is coprime to $2\det(L_1)$. [@chan.gao.weak.approx] shows that finding such a proper isometry $f$ can be done by an exhaustive search. Following [@meara.quadratic 101:7], our approach is based on splitting local isometries into reflections and approximating them globally.

This allows to effectively decide isometry of any two integer lattices: The case of rank $1$ is trivial and rank $2$ is solved by Gauss' reduction of binary forms. The definite case is checked with [@plesken.souvignier]. Finally, two degenerate lattices are isometric if and only if they have the same rank (as abelian groups) and $L/\ker(L) \cong L'/\ker(L')$.

The algorithms are implemented as part of the computer algebra system OSCAR [@OSCAR].\
Recall that, for $V$ a rational quadratic space and $x \in V$ with $x^2 \neq 0$, the reflection in $x$ is defined by $s_x(y) = y- \frac{2(x.y)}{x^2} x$. For the estimates, we use the normalized $p$-adic absolute value with $|p|_p=1/p$ and the maximum norm $|| \cdot ||_p$ on endomorphisms, matrices and vectors. The normalized $p$-adic valuation is denoted by $\nu_p(x)$ and satisfies $\nu_p(p)=1$. If confusion is unlikely, we drop $p$ from the indices.

We recall the following definition from [@brandhorst.veniani.hensel-lifting Section 4]:

::: definition
**Definition 18**. Let $f\colon L \to M$ be a linear map of $\mathbb{Z}_p$-lattices of the same rank. We say that $f$ is *compatible* if $f(L \cap p^iL^\vee)=M \cap p^iM^\vee$ for all $i \in \mathbb{Z}$. For an integer $a \geq 0$, we call a compatible linear map $f$ *$a$-approximate* if there exists an isometry $\tilde f \colon L \to M$ such that $\tilde f \equiv f \bmod p^a\mathrm{Hom}(L,M)$.
:::

If $L$ and $M$ are $\mathbb{Z}_p$-lattices and $f\colon L \to M\otimes \mathbb{Q}_p$ is a $\mathbb{Z}_p$-linear map, then we say that $f$ is $a$-approximate if $f\colon L \to f(L)$ is $a$-approximate. Conditions to be $a$-approximate in terms of matrices are spelled out in [@brandhorst.veniani.hensel-lifting Definition 4.2].

::: {#lem:error .lemma}
**Lemma 20**. *Let $V=\mathbb{Q}^n$ be a quadratic space with Gram matrix $G$ and $h,x \in V$. Let $$c = ||2G|| \cdot  |x^2|^{-1} \cdot ||x|| \max \left\{ |x^2|^{-1} \cdot ||G||, 1\right\}$$ and $$d = \max\{1,  ||2 G x|| /|x^2|\}$$ If $||h|| < ||x||$, then $|| s_x - s_{x+h}|| \leq c \cdot ||h||$ and $||s_x|| = ||s_{x+h}|| \leq d.$*
:::

::: proof
*Proof.* $$\begin{array}{ll}
    & s_{x}(y) - s_{x+h}(y) = \dfrac{2 (x+h,y)}{(x+h)^2}(x+h) - \dfrac{2 (x,y)}{x^2}x\\[15pt]
    =& 2\left(\dfrac{1}{(x+h)^2}-\dfrac{1}{x^2} \right)(x,y) x
     + 2\dfrac{(h,y)}{(x+h)^2}x    + 2\dfrac{(h,y)}{(x+h)^2}h
     + 2\dfrac{(x,y)}{(x+h)^2}h 
\end{array}$$ We may estimate the error norm by the maximum over each summand and use that $|(x,y)|\leq ||G|| \cdot ||x|| \cdot ||y||$. Then we arrive at $$||2G|| \cdot ||y|| \cdot  |(x+h)^2|^{-1} \cdot \max \left\{ |x^2|^{-1}|(x+h)^2 - x^2| , ||h|| \cdot ||x|| , ||h||^2\right\},$$ which simplifies to $$||2G|| \cdot ||y|| \cdot  |(x+h)^2|^{-1} \cdot \max \left\{ |x^2|^{-1}|2x.h - h^2| , ||h|| \cdot ||x|| , ||h||^2\right\}$$ and is bounded above by $$||2G|| \cdot ||y|| \cdot  |(x+h)^2|^{-1} \cdot ||h|| \max \left\{ |x^2|^{-1}||2x|| \cdot ||G||, |x^2|^{-1} ||G|| \cdot ||h|| , ||x|| , ||h||\right\}.$$ Using the assumption $||h|| < ||x||$ we arrive at the claim. ◻
