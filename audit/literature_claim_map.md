# Literature claim map: Coble surfaces, K3 covers, and the 9-dimensional period picture

This note records the standard literature backbone that the repo should cite before
appealing to local computation.

## Standard claim map

### Coble surface from a 10-nodal rational sextic

- The classical starting point is the rational plane sextic with ten nodes studied by
  Coble.
- Blowing up the ten nodes of such a sextic produces the standard rational surface model
  underlying a Coble surface.
- Canonical sources:
  - Coble (1917) for the 10-nodal rational sextic itself.
  - Coble (1929) for the surrounding classical point-configuration geometry.

### K3 double cover and lattice generated by the hyperplane and exceptional classes

- The double cover of `P^2` branched along the sextic produces the associated K3 cover.
- On the blowup model, the natural divisor classes are the pullback of the hyperplane
  and the ten exceptional curves.
  Their intersection pairing is computed directly from the blowup formulas, giving the
  diagonal form `diag(2,-2,...,-2)` on the evident lattice.
- This is the lattice-theoretic input behind the repo's `S_Co` / `T_Co` language.
- Canonical sources:
  - Coble (1917), Coble (1929) for the geometric setup.
  - Nikulin (1979) for the lattice-theoretic embedding and discriminant-form framework.

### Period domain and dimension count

- Once the relevant K3 lattice has signature `(2,9)`, the associated Type IV period
  domain has complex dimension `9`.
- The moduli statement is therefore not something the repo needs to rediscover from
  computation; the computational role is to produce exact worked examples and supporting
  checks inside that standard period-domain picture.
- Canonical sources:
  - Scattone (1987) for Type IV / Baily–Borel compactification background on the K3
    side.
  - Sterk (1991) for the Enriques-side period-space and cusp framework.
  - Dolgachev–Kondō (2013) for the Coble / nodal Enriques moduli picture and rationality
    statements.

### Torelli step

- Passing from the lattice/polarized Hodge data of the K3 cover to the moduli
  description is a standard Torelli move, not a new repo theorem.
- Canonical source:
  - Friedman (1984) as a standard reference for global Torelli for K3 surfaces.

## Repo-facing consequence

Use the literature above for the ambient facts:

- existence and classical geometry of the 10-nodal sextic model;
- K3-cover/lattice setup;
- Type IV dimension count once the lattice is fixed;
- Torelli-based passage to the moduli description.

Use repo computations only for the complementary tasks they actually solve:

- explicit exact sextic equations;
- exact node verification and coordinates;
- exact orbit/stabilizer computations;
- audits of candidate constructions that are not already settled by the cited sources.
